# Guide to Gunsmithing

How to make your own firearms, from the first hand cannons to factory-built assault rifles: which research you need, where each gun is made, what it costs and how the parts go together. For how to load, fire and fit attachments to a gun once you have it, see the [Guide to Weapons](Guide_to_Weapons.md).

```admonish info
This guide follows [Civilizations & Nomads](../gamemodes/Civilizations_and_Nomads.md), where your faction's [research](Guide_to_Research.md) decides what you can make. On other maps the current era decides instead, and some maps only let blacksmith jobs use an anvil.
```

## At a glance

| Era | What you can make | Where | Research |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Medieval to Industrial | Fire lance, hand cannon, arquebus | Iron in hand, **Firearms** menu | Gunpowder |
| Medieval to WW1 | Matchlock musket, matchlock tanegashima | Iron in hand, **Firearms** menu | Gunsmithing |
| Imperial (1713) | Flintlock muskets, musketoons, blunderbusses and pistols | Iron on an anvil, or assembled from parts | Gunsmithing |
| Industrial (1873) | Revolvers, lever-action repeaters, single-shot rifles, coach gun, Gewehr 71 | Steel on a steel-faced or steel anvil | Rifling |
| WW1 (1903) onward | Custom guns of your own design | Gunsmithing bench | Cartridge Firearms |
| WW1 onward | Bolt-action, single-shot, pistol and revolver parts and receivers | Fabricator, assembled at a firearm maintenance bench | Cartridge Firearms |
| WW1 onward | Emplaced machine guns | Steel on an anvil | Cartridge Firearms and later |
| WW2 (1943) onward | Self-loading rifles, submachine guns and machine guns, as custom designs or factory parts | Gunsmithing bench or fabricator | Self-Loading Firearms |
| Cold War (1969) onward | Assault rifles (AK and AR families), modern pistols, shotguns and machine guns | Gunsmithing bench or fabricator | Automatic Weapons |

All of these are on the **Military** line of the research tree. Gunsmithing and Rifling both follow Gunpowder (Rifling also needs Steelmaking), and Cartridge Firearms, Self-Loading Firearms and Automatic Weapons follow Rifling in that order.

## Your workshop

| Station | How to make it | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Anvil** | See [Anvils](Guide_to_Metallurgy.md#anvils). Needs a Crafting skill of **1.20x** to use. | Forging whole guns and flintlock parts |
| **Steel-faced anvil** | Use **5 steel** on an iron anvil once you know Steelmaking or Tatara Smelting | Working steel before the Industrial Age |
| **Gunsmithing bench** | **13 softwood**, in the economy & production menu, after **Gunsmithing** | Designing and building custom guns, rechambering |
| **Firearm maintenance bench** | **18 iron**, in the production menu, after **Gunsmithing** | Fitting barrels and bolts, salvaging parts, repairs |
| **Fabricator** | See [Fabricators](Guide_to_Factory_Automation.md#fabricators) | Mass-producing gun parts and bare receivers |

A gun is no use without ammunition. Gunpowder, musket balls, paper cartridges and metal cartridges are covered under [Projectiles and Gunpowder](Guide_to_Crafting.md#projectiles-and-gunpowder) and [Iron projectiles](Guide_to_Crafting.md#iron-projectiles).

## Early gunpowder weapons

Once your people know **Gunpowder**, hold iron in your hand, open the crafting menu and look under **Firearms**. Gunpowder unlocks the fire lance, hand cannon and arquebus. The matchlock musket and tanegashima fire much faster than the arquebus and also need **Gunsmithing**.

| Gun | Iron | Available until |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fire lance | 6 | End of the Industrial Age |
| Hand cannon | 14 | End of the Industrial Age |
| Arquebus | 15 | End of the Industrial Age |
| Matchlock musket | 18 | End of WW1 |
| Matchlock tanegashima | 18 | End of WW1 |

On persistent worlds such as Gaia these guns stay available after the listed age. They load loose powder and a ball, or a musket cartridge. See [Matchlocks, arquebuses and handcannons](Guide_to_Weapons.md#matchlocks-arquebuses-and-handcannons--late-medieval-).

## Flintlocks

Flintlocks need **Gunsmithing**. There are two ways to make one.

**Forged whole.** In the Imperial and Industrial Ages (and later on persistent worlds such as Gaia), use iron on an anvil and choose **guns**:

| Gun | Iron |
| --- | --- |
| Crude musket | 15 |
| Flintlock pistol | 20 |
| Flintlock musketoon | 25 |
| Flintlock blunderbuss | 25 |
| Flintlock musket | 30 |

**Assembled from parts.** In the Imperial and Industrial Ages (and later on persistent worlds such as Gaia), a smith can forge the barrel and lock while a woodworker makes the stock:

1. Make a **pistol butt** (1 softwood) or **musket stock** (3 softwood) from the softwood **Tools** menu.
2. On an anvil, choose **flintlock parts** and forge a **pistol barrel** (20 iron) or **musket barrel** (30 iron), and a **flintlock mechanism** (2 steel, on a steel-faced or steel anvil).
3. Use sharpened flint on the mechanism, the barrel on the stock, then the mechanism on the barreled stock.

The full rules are in [Flintlock parts assembly](Guide_to_Weapons.md#flintlock-parts-assembly). A flintlock's flint wears out after a few dozen shots; see [Gunflint in flintlocks](Guide_to_Flint.md#gunflint-in-flintlocks).

## The gunsmithing bench

From WW1 on, the gunsmithing bench lets you design your own gun from a stock, a receiver, a feeding system and a barrel, and then build copies of it.

**Stocking the bench.** Click the bench with wood and steel sheets to load them; the bench shows how much of each it holds. Every part is paid for out of that stock.

**Designing a gun.** You need a Crafting skill of **2.5x** and coins worth **50 silver** (about 13 gold coins) in one hand; the coins are spent when you finish. Click the bench with an empty hand, press **Start New Gun**, then:

1. Choose a **stock**.
2. Choose a **receiver**.
3. Choose a **feeding system**.
4. Choose a **barrel**.
5. Choose a **caliber**.
6. Keep the default look or pick another one.
7. Name the gun (up to 15 characters) and press **Finish**.

You get the gun and a **blueprint** of it. If the bench runs short of materials at any step, the design is cancelled.

**Building from a blueprint.** Anyone with a Crafting skill of **1.5x** can click a stocked bench with a blueprint to build another copy of that gun. No coins are needed, only the same wood and steel.

What the bench offers depends on your research:

| Research | Stocks | Receivers | Feeding systems | Barrels |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Cartridge Firearms** | Rifle wooden stock (7 wood), carbine wooden stock (5 wood), pistol grip (3 steel) | Bolt-action (5 steel), revolver (4), small semi-auto (6), pump-action (5) | Internal magazine (4 steel), tubular (5) | Pistol (4 steel), rifle (3), long rifle (8) |
| **Self-Loading Firearms** adds | Steel stock (5 steel) | Large semi-auto (8 steel), small open-bolt (8), large open-bolt (11) | External magazine (8 steel), large external magazine (10), open belt-fed (6) | Carbine (5 steel), air-cooled (10) |
| **Automatic Weapons** adds | Folding stock (7 steel) | Dual selective fire (13 steel), triple selective fire (14) | | |

Some receivers fix the rest of the design:

- A **revolver** always has a revolving cylinder (3 steel).
- A **pump-action** always has a tubular magazine and fires 12 gauge.
- A **small semi-auto** takes a removable magazine (6 steel) and a pistol barrel.
- A **belt-fed** gun needs an air-cooled barrel.

| Receiver | Calibers |
| --- | --- |
| Pump-action | 12 gauge |
| Bolt-action, large semi-auto | 7.92x57mm Mauser, 6.5x50mm, 7.62x39mm, 5.56x45mm |
| Revolver, small semi-auto, small open-bolt | 9x19 Parabellum, 9x18 Makarov, .45 Colt |
| Large open-bolt, dual and triple selective fire | 7.62x39mm, 5.56x45mm |

**Rechambering.** Click the bench with most cartridge guns to convert them to another caliber the bench knows. It takes a short while and costs nothing, but permanently makes the gun about 10% less accurate. Matchlocks, flintlocks, cap-and-ball guns and bows cannot be rechambered.

## Factory-made guns

Real gun families, from the Mauser to the AK and AR, are built from parts. A fabricator makes every part and a bare **receiver** for each family. Then:

1. Take the receiver to a **firearm maintenance bench**.
2. Fit the **bolt** (or bolt carrier, lever or breechblock) and the **barrel** while standing next to the bench.
3. Fit the **magazine** or magazine well and the **stock**; these go on by hand.

The finished gun is named after its family and caliber. A barrel and magazine of the same cartridge decide what the gun fires, so the same receiver can become, for example, a 7.62x39mm or a 5.45x39mm AK.

| Research | Families | Fabricator |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cartridge Firearms** | Mauser, Mosin, Arisaka, Lee-Enfield, Springfield, Lebel, Berthier, Carcano, Murata, Berdan, Sharps, Rolling-block, Martini-Henry, lever-action, pre-war pistols and revolvers, pump-action shotgun, coach gun, Remington 11 | Assembly (tier 1) |
| **Self-Loading Firearms** | SKS, Gewehr 41/43, SVT, M1 Garand, M1 Carbine and the other self-loaders, anti-tank rifles, wartime submachine guns, StG-44, FG42, wartime machine guns | Assembly (tier 1) |
| **Automatic Weapons** | Post-war pistols, shotguns and submachine guns | Assembly (tier 1) |
| **Automatic Weapons** | AK and AR rifles, M24, SVD, VSS, Barretts, BAMR, post-war automatic rifles and machine guns | Automated (tier 2) |

A typical bolt-action rifle costs 10 steel and 3 wood in parts: a 4-steel receiver, a 2-steel bolt, a 3-steel barrel, a 1-steel magazine and a 3-wood stock. An AK costs 6 steel for the receiver, 3 for the bolt carrier, 3 for the barrel, 2 for the magazine well and 2 wood or steel for the stock. Every recipe is listed under [Modular gun parts](Guide_to_Weapons.md#modular-gun-parts).

## Emplaced machine guns

Heavy machine guns on their own tripods or wheels are forged whole: use steel on an anvil and choose **machine guns**. The Maxim, MG 08 and Vickers need **Cartridge Firearms** and 50 steel; later guns, up to the 30mm autocannon, need **Self-Loading Firearms** or **Automatic Weapons**. See [Emplaced machine guns](Guide_to_Weapons.md#emplaced-machine-guns) for the full list and their ammunition.

From the Cold War on, the same anvil menu also forges a makeshift **RPG-7** from 40 steel once you know **Missiles**.

## Working on your guns

- **Swapping parts.** Hold a modular gun, right-click it and choose **Gun Parts** to see its parts, their condition and what any part you carry would change. Unload the gun first. Stocks and magazines come off by hand; barrels and bolts need a firearm maintenance bench next to you. See [Modular gun parts](Guide_to_Weapons.md#modular-gun-parts).
- **Salvage.** A spare part used on a firearm maintenance bench is scrapped for about half its materials, less if it is worn.
- **Wear.** Every shot wears the barrel. A worn-out barrel stops the gun firing until you replace it.
- **Repairs.** A firearm cleaning kit (5 iron) restores lightly damaged guns, and the firearm maintenance bench repairs worse damage. See [Cleaning and repairs](Guide_to_Weapons.md#cleaning-and-repairs).
- **Attachments.** Rails, bipods, lights and slings are covered in [Firearm maintenance and configuration](Guide_to_Weapons.md#firearm-maintenance-and-configuration).

## Tips

- Plan your research. Each firearms step opens a new workshop, so a faction that stops at Rifling is still forging revolvers at the anvil while its neighbours build bolt-action rifles.
- One skilled gunsmith can design a gun and hand the blueprint to less skilled crafters, who can then build it as often as the bench has materials.
- Factory parts from one family fit every gun of that family, so a stock of spare barrels keeps a whole armoury firing.
- Make the ammunition before the guns. A gun nobody can load is just a heavy club.
