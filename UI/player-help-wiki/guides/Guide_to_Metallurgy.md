# Guide to Metallurgy

Sticks, stones and bones can only carry you so far, but Mother Nature is infinitely generous with her gifts, among which she includes a marvelous class of materials - metals. Grateful and astounded, we accept it and pledge that it will not be wasted on trifles.  It shall serve us greatly in mastering ways of cutting, slashing, gutting, chopping and slicing. We shall use it to harness flames and tame explosions, to travel swiftly across vast distances and to bring the Torch of Civilization to the realms of dark ignorance! Bless you, Mother!

<hr>

Metallurgy concerns manipulating raw ores and turning them into metals, from which a myriad of tools, weapons and devices could be produced.

Before proceeding to metallurgy, it is recommended to check the **[Guide to Crafting](Guide_to_Crafting.md)** for the basics of crafting.

**Building the metallurgy equipment (kiln, bloomery etc.) requires certain research levels – check the [Full Crafting List](../Full_Crafting_List.md)**.

## Basics

### Obtaining metals

Different metals are produced from **[ores](Guide_to_Crafting.md#ores)** in different furnaces. See sections on **[Furnaces](#furnaces)** and on respective **[metals](#ores-and-metals)** for more details.

### General steps of metalworking

The primary component of metalworking is **[molds](#molds)**. At the very least, you will need a **blacksmith pot** or a **blacksmith jug** to melt ores in them.

* Put ore or metal (ingots) in a blacksmith pot or a blacksmith jug.
* Place a pot or a jug inside a **kiln**.
* Light fire in the kiln and wait until the ore melts.
* Pour molten metal into a mold of your choosing.
* Take the mold and click a suitable(1) anvil with it(2). 
* Select a metal item to make(3).

(1): Some metals could only be worked on a more advanced anvil. For example, iron items are produced on the iron, steel-faced and steel anvils, not on the stone one. See the **[Anvils](#anvils)** section.

(2): To make other items that are not cast via molds, click the anvil with metal ingots or sheets.

(3): The list of available items depends on the epoch.

## Molds

Molds are made with **[Clay](../Full_Crafting_List.md#clay)** (to make clay, fill a container – a barrel, a bucket etc. – with water, dig dirt piles and click them with the container). Molds are used to cast tools and melee weapons from different metals. Each mold has a certain volume it can hold.  1 unit of volume = 1 unit of ore = 1 unit of molten metal = 1 metal ingot. Making different items requires a different amount of metal.

**Clay molds are made raw and need to be fired in [a campfire or an oven](Guide_to_Crafting.md#campfire-and-ovens) before using!**

## Furnaces

Furnaces are operated similarly to the **[campfire and ovens](Guide_to_Crafting.md#campfire-and-ovens)**, apart from that they do not have continuous fire - the fire will go out after an operation is finished. To insert items into a furnace be on help intent.

Bloomeries and blast furnaces burn **charcoal**, **coke** or **wood**. Raw mineral coal is refused, because its sulphur ruins iron. A firing needs one charcoal to bring the furnace to heat, plus one more charcoal for every two ore inside. A log counts as half a charcoal and coke as one and a half. Examine a furnace to see how much fuel its next firing needs. Kilns still burn 2 fuel per firing, and coal works in them.

## Ores and Metals

Some particulars about smelting certain ores and working with certain metals.

### Kiln-only

**Tin**, **copper**, **zinc**, **gold**, **silver**, **bronze** and **brass** only need a kiln. Just melt ores in jugs and pots.

#### [Bronze](../Full_Crafting_List.md#bronze)
<img src="../images/sheet_bronze.png">

Bronze is an alloy of tin and copper. You need to melt **equal amounts**  (1:1 ratio) of tin and copper ore in the same jug or pot to produce bronze. For example, 1 unit of tin and 1 unit of copper make 1 unit of bronze. If you load more of one metal than the other, the spare stays in the vessel unmelted: pour the bronze out and fire the vessel again to melt it.

#### [Brass](../Full_Crafting_List.md#brass)
<img src="../images/sheet_brass.png">

Brass is an alloy of zinc and copper, available from 313 B.C. Its zinc comes from **calamine**, a pale zinc ore found by mining, crushing stone or sifting soil. Melt **equal amounts** (1:1 ratio) of calamine and copper ore in the same jug or pot to produce brass, the same way as bronze: 1 unit of calamine and 1 unit of copper make 1 unit of brass. Calamine melted on its own gives **zinc**, which can be cast into zinc ingots.

Brass ingots are used for ammunition casings, instruments such as the trombone and handbell, globes, razor blades and statues. Zinc ingots are used for electroplating; see the [Guide to Electroplating](Guide_to_Electroplating.md).

### [Iron](../Full_Crafting_List.md#iron)
<img src="../images/sheet_iron.png">
* Smelt raw iron ore in the bloomery to obtain **sponge iron**.
* Work sponge iron at any anvil into usable iron ingots.
* Pig iron from a blast furnace is too brittle to hammer. Refine it into iron in a **finery forge** (2 pig iron make 1 iron) or a **puddling furnace** (see [Steelworks](#steelworks)).
* To cast iron tools or weapons with molds, **put iron ingots in a jug or a pot and melt them in a kiln**.

### [Steel](../Full_Crafting_List.md#steel)
<img src="../images/sheet_steel.png">

Steel is iron with just the right amount of carbon. It is hard to make. Before the steelworks of the industrial age, smiths only had small, slow ways to make it.

* **Steely bloom.** Pump the bellows beside a bloomery before you light it. Some of the sponge iron from a blown firing comes out as **steely bloom**. Sort it on an anvil into **blister steel**.
* **Carburizing pot** (8 clay, Iron Smithing). Pack wrought iron bars into it with charcoal: one charcoal per bar, plus 4 to hold the heat. Then seal it with an empty hand. After ten minutes the bars come out as **blister steel**, though a few take too little carbon and stay iron. Iron tools packed in with the bars come out **case-hardened** and a little better. A pot sometimes cracks as it cools.
* **Crucible steel** (Crucible Steel research). Make a crucible from 1 clay and fire it. Pack it with 2 iron and 1 charcoal, then seal it in hand. Set it in a kiln and fire the kiln three times, pumping the bellows before each firing. If the crucible holds, it breaks open on a cake of **fine steel**. Skilled smiths crack fewer crucibles.
* **Tamahagane**, folded from a tatara bloom, gives **fine steel** (see below).
* In the industrial age, steel is made in bulk at the [steelworks](#steelworks).

To cast steel tools or weapons with molds, **put steel sheets in a jug or a pot and melt them in a kiln**.

#### Steel grades

| Grade | Made by | What you forge from it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blister steel | Steely bloom, carburizing pot, cementation furnace | A little worse than usual |
| Steel | Folding blister steel, Bessemer converter, recycling | Normal |
| Fine steel | Crucible, tamahagane, open hearth, electric arc furnace | A little better than usual |

To even out blister steel, **fold** it on an anvil that works steel: every 2 bars of blister steel make 1 bar of steel. A quench tub hardens a forged piece by the same step whatever its grade, so fine steel stays the best.

### Steelworks

| Works | Made from | Research | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Coke bank | 15 clay | Blast Furnaces & Coke | Load 5 to 20 coal and light it. Five minutes later every 5 coal are 3 **coke**, which burns hot and clean enough for iron. |
| Finery forge | 15 iron | Blast Furnaces & Coke | Burns the carbon out of pig iron: 2 pig iron make 1 wrought iron. Burns charcoal or coke. |
| Cementation furnace | 30 stone | Blast Furnaces & Coke | A big carburizing pot: up to 30 iron bars become blister steel over a long firing. Burns charcoal. |
| Puddling furnace | 30 stone | Blast Furnaces & Coke | Turns up to 10 pig iron into wrought iron. While it boils, stir it with an empty hand: stirred, 10 pig iron give 8 iron; left alone, only half that. Coal is fine here. |
| Bessemer converter | 40 iron | Steelmaking | Load 5 to 15 pig iron and coke, then light it. Air roars through the melt. When the flame at the mouth **drops**, tip it with an empty hand: 10 pig iron give 9 steel. Tip too early and it is still pig iron. Tip too late, or not at all, and it burns down to wrought iron. |
| Open hearth furnace | 50 stone | Open Hearth Steel | Slowly melts pig iron and scrap steel together into **fine steel**. Burns coal, coke or charcoal. |
| Electric arc furnace | 40 steel | Alloys | Cable it to a powered grid. It melts scrap steel, iron or pig iron into **fine steel**, burns no fuel, and pauses while the power is off. |

### Alloy steels

From the industrial age, once Open Hearth Steel can be researched, mining sometimes turns up **nickel ore**. **Cobalt ore** follows from the Early Modern age and **chromite** from the World War age. Add them to the open hearth or the electric arc furnace beside the charge (up to 20 lumps; examine the furnace to see them). Whatever is not used stays in for the next firing, and **Empty** gives it back.

| Alloy | Where | Add | What it is good for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nickel steel | Open hearth | 1 nickel ore for every 4 steel tapped | Blades a little better than fine steel. Armour forged from it stops blows a little better and **bullets and arrows much better**. |
| Maraging steel | Electric arc furnace, from the Cold War age | 1 nickel and 1 cobalt ore for every 2 bars | Comes out **unaged**: set it in a kiln and fire the kiln once to age it hard (up to 20 bars a firing). Aged, it is the best steel for blades and armour. Forged before aging, it is only ordinary steel. |
| Inconel | Electric arc furnace | 3 nickel ore and 1 chromite for every bar | Not a steel, and not forged: it keeps its strength red hot. Overhaul a locomotive with 5 Inconel and its engine wears half as fast (see [Trains](Guide_to_Trains.md#overhaul)). |

Armour forged on an anvil from any of these steels keeps the better protection, up to a limit.

### Tamahagane (steel from iron sand)

Steel does not need iron ore. Smiths in Japan made it from black iron sand in a clay furnace called a **tatara**.

#### Iron sand
<img src="../images/iron_sand.png">

From the Classical Age on, iron sand is gathered, never mined. Asia's rivers and coasts give more of it than other lands.

| How | Where | You get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hold a wooden bowl and use **Pan river sediment**, then pick **Iron sand** | Dirt or sand beside shallow fresh water | 3 iron sand per two-minute pan (5 on Asia) |
| **Sift soil for resources** (by hand or with a sieve), then pick **Iron sand** | Beach sand | 2 iron sand per sift (4 on Asia) |
| Use the **washing channel** with an empty hand | A channel built beside shallow fresh water | 2 iron sand per 30 seconds (4 on Asia) |
| Use ordinary sand on a washing channel, or on a wooden bowl while standing beside shallow fresh water | Anywhere with fresh water | 1 iron sand for every 5 sand (2 on Asia) |

<img src="../images/kanna_channel.png"> The **washing channel** is a wooden sluice trough (6 wood, Tatara Smelting research). Running water carries the light soil away and the heavy black sand settles behind the riffles. Each spot gives 20 loads before the soil there is washed out. After that, one load comes back every minute. A hammer takes the channel apart.

An ordinary **bloomery** also smelts iron sand: every 2 iron sand become 1 sponge iron. The blast furnace will not take it.

#### The tatara
<img src="../images/tatara.png"> <img src="../images/tatara_on.png">

The **tatara** is a clay furnace made from 20 clay once you know **Tatara Smelting** (Iron Smithing first). It is built for a single firing.

1. **Charge it by turns.** Put in charcoal, then iron sand on top, and repeat. It holds up to 30 of each. Charge it at least 3 times, or it makes only half the kera. Only iron sand matched by charcoal smelts.
2. **Light it** with at least 3 charcoal and 3 iron sand inside. The firing lasts ten minutes. You can keep charging it for the first five.
3. **Work the bellows.** Set bellows beside the tatara and keep pumping them through the firing (one pump blows for a minute and a half). With no blast you get 1 kera for every 6 iron sand. With the bellows going all the way through you get 1 for every 3.
4. **Break it open.** When the fire dies the furnace is spent. Break it with a hammer or pickaxe to get the **kera**, some **pig iron** (1 for every 10 iron sand) and 5 clay back. Then build a new one for the next firing.

#### Kera and tamahagane
<img src="../images/kera.png"> <img src="../images/tamahagane.png">

Use **kera** on an iron anvil to break and sort it. Half of it is **tamahagane**, three tenths is soft wrought iron, and the rest is slag. Then use tamahagane on the anvil to fold it: every 3 chunks become **2 fine steel sheets**. The pig iron from the tatara is refined like any other pig iron.

To forge things from that steel you need an anvil that works steel. Tatara Smelting also lets you weld a steel face onto an iron anvil (see [Anvils](#anvils)).

## Anvils
Anvils are used to forge armor and weapons across the centuries, from ancient swords to muskets and makeshift AK-47s. See the **[Guide to Weapons](Guide_to_Weapons.md)** for more information on specific weapons and their availability in different ages.

**Using anvils requires a Crafting skill of 1.20x.**

Click anvils with a mold with molten metal or with some metal sheets or ingots to craft metal items.

Using iron, steel, bronze or kevlar sheets on an anvil opens the **Crafting window** with everything that metal can be forged into there, in one list. It works like the window for a material in your hand: search by name, use the tabs, click an item to see its cost and press **Craft**, or double-click a ready item to forge one straight away. Items you have too little metal for are listed under **Missing something**, and ones you have not learned under **Not learned yet**. Stay next to the anvil with the metal in your active hand while you work. Molds still ask what to cast in pop-up menus, and **Classic interface** brings the menus back for sheet metal too.

### Quench tub

A **quench tub** is a wooden tub of water that stands next to an anvil. Make one from 8 soft wood once you know Iron Smithing, set it beside the anvil and fasten it with a wrench or hammer.

- Fill it with a bucket (it holds 60 units), pipe water to it, or build it beside running water such as a river with a current, an aqueduct channel, a fountain, a tap or a sink, so it never runs dry. Examine it to see how much it holds.
- Every **iron** or **steel** piece you forge on that anvil is quenched as it comes off. It uses 10 units of water, and the piece comes out hardened at **Excellent** quality, so it hits harder.
- Without a tub, or with the tub dry, forging works as usual. Bronze, copper and the other soft metals gain nothing from quenching. Iron and steel pieces made from a mold at the anvil are quenched the same way.

#### Clay hardening

Once you know **Japanese Swordsmithing** (Steel Blades and Tatara Smelting first), a steel **katana, wakizashi, tachi, tanto or naginata** forged beside a watered quench tub can be clay-hardened. Forge it with the steel (sheets, or a mold of steel) in your working hand and clay (a clay lump or unfired clay) in your other hand, or leave the clay on the anvil's tile; the tub must be fastened down within a tile of the anvil. If something is missing, the game tells you what. The blade is coated thin at the edge and thick at the spine before the quench. It uses 1 clay and the tub's water, and comes out harder than plain quenching makes it, with a hamon line along the edge. Japanese Swordsmithing also teaches the **tachi**, the older long cavalry sword.

### Trip hammer

<img src="../images/Trip_hammer.png">

A **trip hammer** is a heavy hammer lifted by a turning shaft and dropped on the work. Fastened next to an anvil, it **halves the time of everything you forge there**, though no piece takes less than a second.

- Build it from **10 wood**, with **6 iron** and **1 shaft** held or at your feet. You need to know **Watermills**.
- Stand it next to the anvil and fasten it with a wrench.
- It only strikes while a treadwheel, horse gin, mill wheel or windmill is turning next to it. See [Mechanical power](Guide_to_Pottery_and_Lime.md#mechanical-power).
- Click it with an empty hand, or examine it, to see whether it is striking.

### Machine shop

Three later machines also stand next to an anvil and take work off the smith. Fasten each with a wrench. An anvil uses its best hammer and its best fitting machine together, so a steam hammer, a drill press and a hydraulic press around one anvil all count.

| | Machine | Made from | Research | Works when | At the anvil |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <img src="../images/Steam_hammer.png"> | **Steam hammer** | 16 iron, 1 shaft, 1 spring | Steam Power | it has fuel in its firebox | everything takes about a third of the time |
| <img src="../images/Drill_press.png"> | **Drill press** | 10 iron, 1 gear set, 1 shaft, 1 drive belt | Machined Tools | a treadwheel, horse gin, mill wheel or windmill turns next to it | guns, gun parts and machine parts take two fifths less time |
| <img src="../images/Hydraulic_press.png"> | **Hydraulic press** | 14 iron, 1 shaft, 2 bearings | Factories | always; it is pumped by hand | helmets, armour and shields take two fifths less time |

- The parts are [machine parts](Guide_to_Tool_Materials.md) of any material, held or at your feet when you build.
- Stoke the steam hammer by using coal, charcoal, wood, planks or bamboo on it. One coal pays for 4 forging jobs, one charcoal for 3 and one piece of wood for 1. The firebox holds fuel for 60 jobs.
- A steam hammer replaces a trip hammer: only the better of the two counts.
- Click a machine with an empty hand, or examine it, to see whether it is ready.
- No piece takes less than a second, however many machines help.

### Rolling mill

<img src="../images/Rolling_mill.png">

A **rolling mill** squeezes hot metal between two heavy rolls, and makes long stock from less metal than forging it.

- Build it from **16 iron**, with **2 gear sets**, **2 shafts** and **2 bearings** held or at your feet. You need to know **Railways and Steam Locomotion**.
- Fasten it with a wrench next to a treadwheel, horse gin, mill wheel or windmill. It only rolls while that machine is turning, and a faster one rolls faster. See [Mechanical power](Guide_to_Pottery_and_Lime.md#mechanical-power).
- Use iron or steel on it. It keeps rolling until the metal in your hand runs out or you step away, and the stock lands on the mill's tile.

| Metal used | The mill rolls | By hand |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 iron | 2 pairs of [rails](Guide_to_Trains.md) | 1 pair |
| 1 steel | 3 pairs of rails | - |
| 10 steel | 1 [hull armour plate](Guide_to_Space_Stations.md) | 15 steel |

You still need the research for what you roll, as you would to forge it.
