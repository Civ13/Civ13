# Aircraft

Light planes are the first things in Civ13 that leave the ground. There are seven hangar models: the two-seat **light plane**, the four-seat **cabin plane**, the small two-seat **trainer**, the **cargo plane**, the bigger **twin-engine plane**, the **airliner** and the **helicopter**, and planes can also be built by hand or from a vehicle sheet. Every plane takes off and lands best on a **runway** and flies on the air level above the map. Flying is only possible on maps that have an air level: at the moment that is [North and South America](../maps/The_Americas.md) and [Gaia](../maps/Gaia.md), the persistent lands. Elsewhere the plane rolls along but never lifts off, and the pilot is told that there is no airspace.

**Powered Flight** is a World War II (1943) node in the Vehicles line. It needs **Combustion Engine**, and Aviation & Rocketry is researched after it. The hangar is in the **production** crafting category and runway in **roads**. The hangar only assembles planes for a faction that has finished the Powered Flight node. With Powered Flight alone it builds the wood-and-cloth **trainer** and **light plane**. The all-metal **cabin**, **cargo** and **twin-engine** planes and the **airliner** also need **Aviation & Rocketry**, and so does a twin-engine plane built by hand.

## Building the hangar and the plane

1. Lay a power cable, then craft the aircraft hangar from 40 steel while standing on or next to the cable. The **4x4** square to its **northeast** must be dry, open floor: the finished plane appears there. The hangar is not built if the research, the bay or the cable is missing, and you are told which.
2. Feed wood or aluminium for the airframe, and steel, glass, cloth and cable stacks into the hangar, and drag four unused, unbroken, unattached wheels onto it. Drag an adjacent laid cable onto it to connect a network with **20 available power**, as for the automobile workshop. Examine the hangar to see what it holds.
3. Click the hangar, choose a model and stay next to it while it works. Loose objects or people in the bay stop the assembly, and nothing is spent until the plane is finished. **Unload inputs** returns the stock.
4. The plane is delivered with a gasoline engine, an **empty** fuel tank and its key. Fill the tank with gasoline before you try to taxi; see [Engines](Guide_to_Crafting.md#engines) for fuel.

The plane can also be built **piece by piece**, by several people. Choose **Start a Light plane piece by piece** with the bay clear. Then bring each piece in turn to the hangar or to the airframe in the bay: the **airframe** (2 pieces, 10 wood each), **landing gear** (4 wheels, dragged on), the **engine and propeller** (10 steel and 8 cable), the **skin** (2 pieces, 10 wood each), the **canopy** (6 glass) and the **seats** (8 cloth). The aluminium planes pay their airframe and skin in aluminium instead. The plane takes shape from the tail to the nose. Fitting pieces needs no power. When the last piece is on, choose **Roll out**, which needs 20 power and gives the same plane, key and empty tank as a one-go build. A half-built plane survives saves, and **Abandon** loses everything fitted so far. The bay cannot hangar a plane while a project is in it. See [Building a car piece by piece](Guide_to_Crafting.md#building-a-car-piece-by-piece) for how stacks and wheels are taken.

The light plane is four tiles long and three wide: the nose with the engine and propeller, the cockpit between the wings, a fuselage section with one passenger seat, and the tail. The pilot sits in the cockpit and flies from the **control column**.

The **cabin plane** has the same airframe and size, so it fits the same hangar bay and carrier elevators, but its whole fuselage row is a cabin with **three passenger seats**. The extra seats need a longer take-off roll: **10 tiles** of runway or **20** of open ground. Built piece by piece, it takes the same pieces as the light plane, with 8 cloth for the seats.

The **trainer** is the smallest plane: just the nose, the wings and cockpit, and a tail with an instructor's seat behind the pilot. It is three tiles long and turns in a **3x3** square, is the cheapest to build and takes off soonest: **6 tiles** of runway or **12** of open ground.

The **cargo plane** has the light plane's size, but its fuselage row is three **cargo bays** instead of seats, so only the pilot flies in it. Anything put on a cargo bay flies with the plane and stays aboard when it is hangared. It needs **9 tiles** of runway or **18** of open ground.

![Render using Civ13 sprites: the twin-engine plane, five tiles across with an engine and propeller on each wing, beside the three-tile light plane](../images/civ13-twin-engine-plane.png)

The **twin-engine plane** is **5 tiles** across the wings and 4 long, with an engine and propeller on each wing, the pilot and three passenger seats. The hangar builds it in the **5x5** square northeast of the press, so clear that much room first; a hangar placed for the smaller planes still works. Each engine has its own fuel tank, so fill both. Starting the engine from the control column starts the other engine too, and with both running it lifts more weight. A wing engine with an empty tank stays off. It needs **12 tiles** of runway or **24** of open ground, and to use a runway every tile under it must be runway, so lay it **5 tiles wide**. It is too big to hangar.

The **airliner** is the twin-engine plane with a long cabin: 5 tiles across and **6** long, with the pilot and **six passenger seats** either side of an aisle you can walk down, and a door on each cabin row. The hangar builds it in a **6x6** square northeast of the press. Like the twin it has an engine and a fuel tank on each wing. It needs **16 tiles** of runway, 5 wide, or **30** of open ground, and more when it is loaded. It is too big to hangar.

## The helicopter

After **Rocketry** the hangar also builds a **UH-1 helicopter**: 3 tiles wide and 7 long, with the pilot on the front right, a co-pilot beside them and five cabin seats. It is built in the **7x7** square northeast of the hangar, so clear that much room first. Its turboshaft engine burns **diesel**. It needs no runway: start the engine, and **Climb** spools the rotor up for a few seconds and lifts it straight up. Stopped in the air it hovers and turns on the spot. Descend close to the ground at low speed to set it down on any dry, fairly level ground. If the engine dies in the air it sinks fast and lands hard, so restart it quickly. It is too big to hangar.

## Designing your own plane

Hold a blank **ship's draught** and a pen or pencil, use the draught and choose **Draw a plane on the sheet**. The designer opens on a light plane you can change tile by tile: the size, the engine, where the tanks, the pilot and the seats go. The **Checks** list says what would keep it on the ground. The [crafting guide](Guide_to_Crafting.md#drawing-a-vehicle-sheet) covers the window itself.

A single-engine airframe is **3 wide** and **3 to 6 long**. A twin-engine airframe is **5 wide** and **4 to 6 long**: every row past the fourth is one more cabin row behind the wings, so a 5 by 6 design has the airliner's body. Under **Start from**, the **twin transport** is a ready-made 5 by 5 twin with a pilot and four seats.

A longer plane carries more and pays for it on the ground. The wings of a twin bear more weight with each cabin row, and every two sections past the light plane's twelve add a tile to the take-off roll (two on open ground). A heavy load still lengthens the roll, slows the climb and burns more fuel, so check the figures before filling every tile with seats and tanks.

## Building a plane from a sheet

A plane drawn on a [vehicle sheet](Guide_to_Crafting.md#drawing-a-vehicle-sheet) can be built at the hangar: use the sheet on the hangar, with the square northeast of it clear as far as the plane's longest side (6x6 for a 6-long plane). A twin's sheet needs **Aviation & Rocketry**. See the crafting guide for what it costs.

## Building a plane by hand

Without a hangar, a plane can be put together from loose parts like a [hand-built vehicle](Guide_to_Crafting.md#modular-tanks). Craft the airframe sections, airframe controls and pilot's seat in the **vehicle parts** category.

1. Lay airframe sections side by side in a rectangle **3 sections wide** and **3 to 6 long**. The 3 is the wingspan; the long side runs from nose to tail.
2. Stand the parts on the sections: an engine and a fuel tank (on the middle of the front row, so the engine turns the propeller), the **pilot's seat** on the middle of the second row, a wheel on each of the four corners, and passenger seats anywhere behind. A car's driver's seat does not fly a plane.
3. Put the airframe controls on any section and click them. The survey says what the airframe still needs. Choose **Assemble it**, then pick which way the nose points: along the long side.

Every section takes the shape its place calls for: the front row becomes the nose, the second row the wings with the cockpit under the pilot, the last row the tail and anything between the fuselage. A **3 by 3** plane is a short trainer, **3 by 4** has the light plane's shape, and **3 by 5** or **3 by 6** is a long transport. Planes longer than the light plane need a longer take-off roll, one more runway tile for every two extra sections, and a 5-long plane does not fit a 4x4 hangar bay. Parts stood on it later are fitted with **Fit new parts**, and its builders can **Take it apart** again. Fill the tank and it flies like any other plane.

An airframe **5 wide** and **4 to 6 long** becomes a **twin-engine plane** instead, with the 5 sections as the wingspan: point the nose along the 5-section side. Stand an engine and a fuel tank on the second and fourth sections of the front row, the pilot's seat in the middle of the second row, and wheels on the four corners. The front corners and the outer sections of the third row are open air beside the nose and cabin. A 5 by 4 twin takes the same 12 and 24 tile rolls as the hangar's twin. A 5 or 6 long one has one or two more cabin rows between the wings and the tail, carries more, and needs a longer roll. Taking it apart loosens both engines.

## Laying a runway

Runway is a road cover crafted from **1 stone per tile** in the roads category, like a modern road. A take-off needs **8 tiles** of straight runway in front of the plane at full throttle, against **16 tiles** of open ground without one, after pressing **Climb** to line up. A runway is not needed to land: flat, smooth ground works too. A long runway is just the easiest target. Lay it three tiles wide so the whole plane is over tarmac, and keep the ends clear of walls, trees and vehicles.

## Taxiing

On the ground a plane drives like a car. Start the engine with the control column, throttle up a gear, steer with the arrow keys and switch to reverse while stopped with the down arrow or the HUD button. You can taxi in any gear along roads, aprons and runways, into and out of a hangar bay, without lifting off by accident: away from a runway the plane only leaves the ground once you have lined it up for a takeoff (see below). The HUD shows **PARKED** or **TAXI** while you are on the ground, and **TAKEOFF** once you have lined up. A plane that has just landed rolls on at first gear, ready to taxi.

## Flying

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Use the control column | Throttle up one gear |
| Right-click the control column | Throttle down one gear |
| Arrow keys | Steer |
| **Climb** (verb on the control column, or the HUD button) | In the air, aim 5 height levels higher. On the ground, with the engine running, it lines the plane up for takeoff and tells you what is still needed |
| **Descend** (verb or HUD button) | Aim 5 height levels lower; just above the ground at low speed, land |

The HUD shows the plane's height while you are in the pilot's seat, such as **LOW 7** or **HIGH 18**. Examining the control column also shows how far above the ground you are and the height you are heading for.

- **Take-off:** on a runway, throttle to full and roll straight ahead: after 8 tiles of runway (for a light plane with a normal load) the plane takes off by itself. Off a runway, press **Climb** first to line up, then roll 16 tiles of open ground at full throttle. Without that, a full-throttle roll over open ground just keeps taxiing. The wheels leave the ground two levels up and the plane climbs on to height 6 in **low flight**. Coming to a stop against something stands the takeoff down again.
- **Height:** a plane's height uses the same 0 to 15 scale as the hills. It climbs 2 levels for every tile it flies (1 when heavily loaded) and sinks 2, toward the height you set with Climb and Descend.
- **Low flight (height 15 and below):** the plane flies among the hills. Anything ahead that reaches its height is flown into: a hillside, a tree or a wall (3 levels above the ground it stands on), or an upper storey. A **Terrain ahead** warning tells you the height of ground within 5 tiles in front of you that you would hit, so climb above it in time. A plane that flies into a building hits it with its loaded weight and speed: a light plane knocks a hole in a wooden house, a heavy one at speed goes through it, and stone mostly holds. The plane breaks up where it hit, and a fuelled plane sets wood and thatch alight. A plane that breaks up over a building comes down on its roof. Anyone can shoot at a plane in low flight.
- **Cruise (above 15):** past the top of the hill scale the plane cruises over every hill, tree and rooftop, and only heavy weapons reach it. It cannot sink back into low flight over an upper storey or the highest peaks, and you have to come down to low flight before you can land. The pilot sees the ground below from the cockpit, and people on the ground see the plane's shadow pass over them.
- **Stalling:** a plane that flies slower than **second gear** for 3 seconds, or whose engine stops, stalls and crash-lands on the nearest clear ground below. You get one warning to throttle up first.
- **Map edge:** the plane stops at the edge of the world and warns you. Turn back at once, because a plane that stays there stalls, or [fly on to another continent](#flying-to-another-continent).
- **Speed:** on the ground a plane taxis at a car's pace. In low flight it is a little faster than a car, and at cruise it flies **twice as fast as a car** at full throttle.
- **Fuel:** flying burns fuel faster than driving, low flight burns the most, and a heavier load burns more. A full tank lasts about four minutes of low flight. Watch the tank, because an engine that runs dry in the air is a stall.

## Flying to another continent

Every land can be reached by air: Gaia, North and South America, Europe, Africa, Asia, Oceania, Greenland and Antarctica (see [Regions of the World](../regions/Regions.md)).

1. Fly within 8 tiles of the edge of the map.
2. Use **Fly to another land** on the control column. It lists each continent you can reach, its direction and its distance in tiles.
3. Pick one. The flight burns about 20 seconds of cruise fuel for each tile, so fill the tank before a long flight. With too little fuel you are told so and keep flying where you are.

Everyone aboard flies with you, with what they carry and what is stowed in the hold. Animals cannot make the flight, and a plane too big for a hangar, or with something loose aboard, is refused. You arrive in the air near the edge of the other continent, still flying the same way, with the controls in your hands. Your character lives in the land you fly to from then on; fly back to return.

## Weight

Everything aboard counts: passengers, cargo on the bays and loose items on the airframe. Each model is built to fly with one person in every seat and a little cargo. Above that the plane is **loaded down**:

- its take-off roll grows with the load, on a runway and on open ground alike;
- a **heavily loaded** plane (about a quarter over its normal load) climbs only 1 level a tile, burns more fuel and cruises more slowly;
- at almost twice its normal load it **will not lift off**: unload it first.

Examine any part of the plane to see how its load affects it, and press **Climb** on the ground to see the roll you need now.

## Landing

Descend until the plane skims just above the ground, then fly at **second gear or slower** and **Descend** once more. Too fast, and the plane stays in the air and tells you to slow down.

- **On a runway:** a smooth landing. At least half of the airframe has to be over runway for the wheels to find it. The flight deck of an [aircraft carrier](Guide_to_Aircraft_Carriers.md) is a runway too, and its arresting wires stop the plane on the spot.
- **On flat open ground:** with no runway below, the plane can set down on dry, open ground such as grass, dirt, sand, snow, roads or floors, with nothing standing in the way under any part of the airframe. Where that ground is **perfectly level** under the whole plane (no step in height between neighbouring tiles; built floors and roads count as level) and has no loose rock or gravel, the landing is as smooth as on a runway: no damage, and the plane rolls on at taxi speed.
- **On a gentle slope or rocky ground:** where the ground under the plane differs by one height level, or there is rock or gravel under it, the plane still lands, but in a **rough landing**: it bounces down, stops dead and the airframe takes damage, which a weld repairs. A plane whose airframe is already badly torn also breaks its landing gear. Steeper ground than that is no place to land.
- **Over water, trenches or obstacles:** the plane warns you and keeps flying so you can go round again.

Where you cannot land, **Descend twice within 5 seconds** to force a crash landing. The plane comes down on the nearest clear ground with its engine off and its landing gear broken, and the crew are bruised. With no clear ground within reach the airframe breaks up and everyone aboard falls.

## Arming the plane

A light plane can carry a gun and two bombs:

- **Nose gun:** take any light machine gun, such as a Madsen or a Type 99, and use it on the **nose** section of the plane to bolt it in. Use a magazine on the nose section to load it, and a **wrench** to take the gun out again. Examine the nose to see what is mounted and how many rounds are left.
- **Firing:** with the control column in one hand and the other free, choose **Fire guns** on the column. The burst goes straight along the plane's heading from the nose: down the runway on the ground, or onto the ground ahead of the nose in low flight, so you strafe by flying low over the target. From cruise height you are too high to aim.
- **Bombs:** craft an **aircraft bomb casing** from steel (in the **weapons** menu, after Powered Flight) and fill it with ten units of [nitroglycerin](Guide_to_Chemistry.md) from any container. Use the filled bomb on a **wing** section to hang it; each wing takes one. A wrench takes a bomb down again.
- **Bombing:** in the air, choose **Drop bomb** on the column. The bomb falls onto the ground directly under the middle of the plane and bursts there, so fly low and straight over the target. Bombs cannot be dropped on the ground.

Filled bombs go off when a heavy blast reaches them, on the plane or in a store, so keep them apart from anything else that explodes. An empty casing is harmless.

## Bailing out

Anyone aboard a plane in the air can right-click the floor they stand on and choose **Bail out**.

- It takes two seconds. A plane with a door opens it for you. A plane with no door, such as a small single-seater, jettisons its cockpit canopy.
- You drop to the ground straight below, or to the nearest open tile if something solid stands there.
- With a **parachute pack** on your back you float down and land on your feet.
- Without one, a jump just off the ground is a hard landing. From any higher you break both legs, and from cruise height you are badly hurt as well.
- A jettisoned canopy stays off. On the ground, use **2 sheets of glass** on the cockpit to fit a new one, which takes eight seconds. Examining the plane shows when the canopy is missing.

## Damage and repair

People on the ground can shoot at a plane as it passes over them:

- In **low flight** any gun that fires across the ground under the plane has a **40%** chance of hitting it.
- At **cruise** height only heavy weapons reach it, such as heavy machine guns, cannon and shells, with a **20%** chance.

Every hit tears the airframe and can break a wheel, and hits on the nose can stop the engine. When accumulated damage reaches the airframe's limit the plane fails and comes down as a crash landing. Examine the plane to read its damage, and **weld** any frame section on the ground to mend the airframe. A crash breaks the landing gear, and the plane will not roll until it is fixed. The landing gear counts as one part: once the airframe is whole, one more weld on any frame section, or welding any one wheel, fixes every wheel.

## Hangaring a plane

The aircraft hangar also stores up to **2 planes** off the map. A plane in the hangar is not on the airfield, so it cannot be shot, stolen or caught by a save while it is in the air.

1. Land, then taxi the plane into the 4x4 bay to the northeast of the hangar, where planes are built. The whole airframe has to be inside the bay. Runway under the bay is fine.
2. Switch the engine off, get everyone out and take loose items off it.
3. Click the hangar and choose **Hangar the parked aircraft**. Stay next to it for a few seconds.
4. To fly again, clear the bay and choose **Roll out a hangared aircraft**. The plane comes back where it was parked, with the same fuel and damage.

You need to hold the plane's key to hangar it and to roll it out. Examine the hangar to see which planes it holds. Cars go in a [vehicle garage](Guide_to_Crafting.md#vehicle-garage).

## Towing a plane

A plane on the ground can be towed like a car. Park it in line behind a truck or another vehicle, both facing the same way and standing still, within 2 tiles, and use a **rope** on the plane. It then rolls and turns wherever the tug goes, which is handy for pulling a plane out of a hangar bay or along a taxiway without starting its engine. The tug pulls the plane's weight too: a light plane is too heavy for a small car, so use a truck. A plane cannot take off while it is on a tow line, and a plane in the air cannot take one. Right-click either vehicle and choose **Cast Off Tow Line** to let go.

## Crewed airships

An airship is a deck you build yourself, on the ground or in the open air, held up by gas cells and steered from a helm. A crew walks around on it while it flies. It flies in the open air above the ground, so only where planes can fly. The planks, gas cells and helm are crafted in the **vehicle parts** category. In tech-tree games, research **Lighter-than-Air Flight** from the Early Modern (1903) age after **Wagons & Ships** and **Utilities**. A faction that already knows **Powered Flight** can also make the basic wooden kit. Lighter-than-Air Flight does not unlock powered aircraft, metal airship frames or the bomb hatch.

![An airship deck of wooden planks with a helm at the stern and two gas cells over it](../images/civ13-airship-deck.png)

1. Use **airship planks** on clear, dry, level ground beside you, with nothing built or standing on it. Each bundle lays one tile of deck. Keep laying planks edge to edge from the deck you already have. A deck can be up to 60 tiles. You can also start the deck in the air from the top of a **wooden ladder** stood on the ground under open air.
2. Use **gas cells** on the deck to hang them, one per tile. A cell hangs empty: fill it with hydrogen or helium (see [Lifting gas](#lifting-gas)). A full cell holds up 12: every tile of wooden deck counts as 1 and so does every person aboard (see [Airship materials](#airship-materials) for lighter and stronger parts).
3. Set up the **airship helm** on the deck, facing the way you want the bow to point, and use it to commission the deck as an airship and name her.
4. On the ground, choose **Lift off** at the helm. She needs enough gas cells and open sky over every tile of her deck, so walk her out from under any roof first: on the ground she creeps along over clear ground only.
5. At the helm, choose **Cast off**, pick a speed and steer north, east, south or west. Choose **Hold position** to stop her where she is.
6. To come down, hold position and choose **Land**. Every tile under her must be clear, level ground with no tree, building or water. She sinks gently a level at a time.

| Condition | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Not enough gas cells | She cannot cast off or move until more cells are hung or fewer people are aboard |
| No engine | She drifts along at slow ahead |
| A running [engine](Guide_to_Crafting.md) on the deck | Half or full ahead, depending on its power against her size. It burns its fuel as she goes. Sails and oars do nothing up here |
| Upper floors, ladders and the map edge | She stops in front of them |
| The edge of the deck | Step off and you fall to the ground |

She can also fly higher: build a floor in the open air, stand a ladder on it up to the open air above, and lay her deck from the top of that ladder. However high she flies, the crew sees the ground around her (or any floor or roof built in between), and her shadow falls there. From the deck you can shoot, throw and drop things onto the ground below by aiming at it through the open air, as from a [pedal airship](Guide_to_Crafting.md#fighting-from-the-basket). Board her from a ladder stood up beside her deck. To add planks or take a gas cell down with a wrench, hold position first, then use **Refit the deck** at the helm. **Strike the airship** turns her back into a deck that stays where it is. An airship saved in flight comes back holding her position.

### Airship materials

| Part | Made from | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Airship planks | 3 wood | Weigh 1 per tile and can catch fire |
| Aluminium airship frame | 3 aluminium | Weighs half as much as planks and does not burn |
| Steel airship frame | 3 steel | Weighs twice as much as planks, does not burn, and treetops never catch it |

Frames are laid like planks, and one deck can mix them. A wooden deck under a burning gas cell catches fire.

### Lifting gas

A gas cell takes 60 units of gas and lifts in proportion to how full it is. Hydrogen lifts the most but burns; helium lifts a tenth less and never catches fire. A cell holds one gas: take it down with a wrench to let the gas out before switching. All of these are crafted in the **production** category.

For a first wooden airship, use the **water-gas retort** unlocked by **Utilities**. Feed it coal or charcoal and water, fill a **leather gasbag** at the retort, then use the filled bag on an empty, hung gas cell. You do not need a powered compressor or gas cylinder for this route.

| Gas works | How it works |
| --- | --- |
| Hydrogen generator (wood) | Pour in sulphuric or hydrochloric acid and drop in iron or zinc. It gives off hydrogen until one runs out |
| Electrolysis tank (steel) | Cable it to power and give it water: pour it in, pipe it in, or set it beside running water. It splits the water into hydrogen and half as much oxygen |
| Water-gas retort (steel) | Stoke it with coal or charcoal and give it water. It gives off hydrogen |
| Helium separator (steel) | Set it beside a working oil well and cable it to power. It slowly draws off helium |

| Storage | How it works |
| --- | --- |
| Gasbag (leather) | Use it on a gas works or gas holder to fill it with 60 units, and on a gas cell, gas holder or gas compressor to empty it |
| Gas cylinder (steel) | Holds 240 units. Only a powered gas compressor fills it |
| Gas compressor (steel) | Put a cylinder in and cable it to power. It fills the cylinder from a gas holder beside it, from gasbags emptied into it, or from gas piped to it |
| Gas holder (steel) | Holds 3000 units of one gas. Empty gasbags and cylinders into it or pipe gas in. Couple a **gas hose** to it, then use the hose on gas cells within six tiles, even one level up |

Gas works, gas holders and compressors join [factory pipe networks](Guide_to_Factory_Automation.md) like other machines. Hold a bag or cylinder of oxygen to someone's face to ease their breathing.

### Airship damage and bombing

| Event | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Shot from the ground under her deck | About half of shots passing under her strike her when she flies one level up, and half as many for each level higher. A floor or roof between the shooter and her deck shields her. Some hit whoever stands on that tile; most hole the gas cell over it, and each hole costs a quarter of its lift |
| Fire arrow, fire bolt or incendiary shot | The cell bursts into flame, lifts nothing more, and sets anyone under it alight |
| Less lift than she needs | She stops and sinks a level at a time until cells are patched or replaced, people get off, or she comes to rest on the ground, a floor or a roof |
| Sinking onto a few trees | Her weight flattens trees under up to a quarter of her deck, but the branches tear the gas cells over them |
| Sinking onto a forest | Trees under more than a quarter of her deck catch her in the canopy and tear her cells; she hangs there, and breaks up if that leaves her under half her lift |
| Less than half the lift she needs | She breaks up and falls. Everyone and everything aboard drops to the ground, or to a floor in between, and is hurt, worse from higher up, and the deck, cells and helm are wrecked. Anyone falling onto a tree comes down beside its trunk a little less hurt |

Use **cloth** on a holed gas cell to patch one hole; this works in flight. A burnt cell must be cut away with a wrench and replaced. Your own crew's shots go down, never up into your cells.

The **airship bomb hatch** (steel, vehicle parts) is built on the deck. Load it with up to two filled [aircraft bombs](#arming-the-plane), then pull its ring to drop one straight onto the ground below. A wrench takes a bomb back out.

### Building an airship at the yard

Instead of laying her plank by plank, you can draw an airship on a ship's draught and have an **airship yard** (wood, **production** category, after Lighter-than-Air Flight) build her.

1. Hold a blank **ship's draught** and a pen or pencil, use the draught and choose **Draw an airship on the sheet** (see [Drawing a vehicle sheet](Guide_to_Crafting.md#drawing-a-vehicle-sheet)). The designer shows her deck plan: pick planks, frames, gas cells, a helm, an engine and its fuel tanks, and bomb hatches, and click or drag over the tiles. Choose the engine kind and size and how many people she should carry. The figures show her weight against her lift, her cost and her engine push, and the checks list anything that would stop her flying. Click a finding to see the tiles it means. **Save to the sheet** when she is ready. To copy an airship you already built, choose **Draw her lines** at her helm while holding a blank sheet of paper. Airship sheets export and import as design codes like every other plan (see [Sharing plans as design codes](Guide_to_Boats.md#sharing-plans-as-design-codes)).
2. Feed the yard its stock: wood, aluminium, steel, cloth, and plastic or rubber sheets for rubberised cells and sausage casings for goldbeater's skin cells, as the sheet needs. Cable it to a supply with 20 power to spare.
3. Clear the ground northeast of the yard, as many tiles east as she is wide and north as she is long. It must be dry, level and open, with nothing on it.
4. Use the sheet on the yard. It refuses a sheet whose checks fail, one your faction lacks the research for, ground that is not clear, or stock it does not have, and says why.

She is laid facing north, commissioned under the sheet's name and held in place. Her gas cells hang empty and her fuel tanks are empty, with each engine connected to the nearest tank. Fill the cells with [lifting gas](#lifting-gas) and the tanks with fuel, then cast off and lift off at her helm.

To break an airship down for parts, land her wholly on the ground northeast of the yard with nobody aboard, then use the yard and choose **Break down the (her name) for parts**. A window shows what goes back before anything starts. Everything she cost goes back into the yard's stock, less for damaged parts, and her cargo is set down by the yard. If she is registered, only her owners or her captain can do this. If she is registered to nobody, only a player with a verified Discord account can.

## Tips

- Hangar the plane when you are done flying. A hangared plane is kept safe across saves.
- Land before a save. A plane saved in the air is set down on the ground below when the world loads, and that is a hard landing with broken wheels unless the ground directly beneath it is clear.
- Pick a flat meadow or a road when there is no runway. Level, smooth ground is a free landing; a slope or rocky patch costs airframe damage every time, and a torn plane lands on broken wheels.
- Build the runway right out of the hangar bay. The plane is delivered facing south on the 4x4 square northeast of the hangar, so a strip running south from there is the shortest taxi.
