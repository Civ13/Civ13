# Your First Day

This guide takes a new character through the first day on a persistent world such as [North and South America](../maps/The_Americas.md), where the world starts in the Stone Age and knowledge is kept in books. It covers getting a research book, food, water, shelter, early tools and a safe place to sleep, in the order you will need them.

```admonish info
On persistent worlds **you can't craft anything you haven't studied**. A new character knows nothing, so the first thing to do is get a book. Round maps such as [Civilizations & Nomads](../gamemodes/Civilizations_and_Nomads.md) work differently: start with the [Starter Guide](../Starter_Guide.md) there.
```

## Your first night

Six things keep a new character alive until morning. Do them in this order; each links to the full section below.

1. **Fire.** Make a flint axe (see [Tools without a book](#tools-without-a-book)), chop a tree for 4 logs and use the logs in your hand to build a **campfire**. Click it with an empty hand to light it. It burns one log every 8 minutes, so about 5 last a whole night.
2. **Shelter.** Get under a roof before dark or bad weather. A cave is the quickest: roofed ground looks darker. Check that no animal lives there.
3. **Water.** Click fresh water with an empty hand on **Grab intent** to drink. Clear running water is the safest raw water, and salt water is never safe. Boil it once you have a pot.
4. **Food.** Pick berries from a bush on **Grab intent**, or kill a small animal such as a rabbit or a bird. Roast meat on the lit campfire before you eat it.
5. **Warmth.** Watch the temperature icon. If it turns blue, stand next to a lit campfire and keep on any coat you started with. Get out of the rain: wet clothes chill you faster.
6. **People.** Hold leather or cloth and click a lit campfire under the open sky to send a **smoke signal**. Everyone outdoors a long way off is told which way to walk to reach you.

More on each: [Water](#water), [Food](#food), [Shelter and warmth](#shelter-and-warmth), [Finding other people](#finding-other-people).

## What you start with

- **Full food and water bars.** They drain as you work, so plan water and food within your first hour. The bars are at the bottom centre of the screen (see [Customizing Your HUD](Guide_to_HUD.md)).
- **Simple clothes.** In the Stone Age that is a leather loincloth. If you spawn somewhere cold (tundra, taiga, or a temperate area in winter) you also get a fur coat and fur boots; keep them on. In the desert you get a robe.
- **No tools and no knowledge.** You don't know a single recipe yet.

A short [in-game tutorial](../New_Player_Guide.md#in-game-tutorial) runs on your first character and walks you through the controls.

## Get a research book

Every recipe belongs to a chapter, such as **Basic Toolmaking** or **Basic Furniture**. You learn a chapter by studying it from a book. There are three ways to get one:

1. **Found your own faction.** Open the **Faction** tab and use **Create Faction**. Pick a name, draw a symbol if you like, then press **Found the faction**. The founder immediately gets the **Stone Age master book**, which holds every Stone Age chapter, and a **writing slate**. You can do this alone; you don't need other players.
2. **Join someone who has a book.** A book isn't used up by studying, so one book can teach a whole tribe. Ask a faction to lend you theirs, or to let you study from it on their research bench. If they know a chapter, they can stand beside you while you study it to make it faster.
3. **Buy or trade for one.** Books and single chapter pages are ordinary items. Anyone can rip a chapter out of a book and sell, swap or give it away.

To study, hold the book, use it in your hand and pick **Study: chapter**. The menu lists the chapters you can study now first, then the ones that need groundwork, then the ones you already know. A Stone Age chapter takes **1 minute**. Stand still until it finishes; you keep the chapter until you die.

In a world that has advanced a long way, you may already know the oldest chapters when you arrive: the game tells you so when you start, and the tech tree marks them as common knowledge. See [How knowledge spreads](Guide_to_Books_and_Scholars.md#how-knowledge-spreads).

```admonish tip
Writing supplies need no chapter: once you have **wooden planks**, anyone can make a **pencil** (1 plank), a **sheet of paper** (1 plank), a **blank book** (4 planks) and a **research bench** (8 planks), and a **writing slate** from 2 stone. Planks need no chapter either (see [Tools without a book](#tools-without-a-book)), so a lone player can also start from scratch: make planks, build a bench, put a blank book on it and research Basic Toolmaking yourself. See [Books and Scholars](Guide_to_Books_and_Scholars.md) for research benches, copying books and scholarship.
```

## What to study first

The Stone Age master book has twelve chapters. None of them needs another chapter first, except **Regalia & Heraldry**, which needs Weaving. For a first day, study these in order:

| Chapter | What it gives you on day one |
| --- | --- |
| **Basic Toolmaking** | The **stone hatchet** (2 stone and a wood handle), **wood handle**, **wooden spade** and bone shovel, **wooden ladder**, knives, the **tanning rack** and leather, the **dehydrator** for drying meat, fishing rods and nets, rope |
| **Basic Furniture** | **Bed** (4 planks), **brazier** (stone), tables, chairs and the writing desk |
| **Pottery & Cookware** | **Wooden mug** (1 log), **bucket** (2 planks), clay pots and cups, the clay **cooking pot**, waterskin, wooden bowl |
| **Primitive Construction** | **Log walls** (4 logs, built in steps), **roof supports** (2 logs), the **wood roof builder** (1 plank), doors, fences, wooden floors and stairs, **mine supports**, **torches**, the **travois** |
| **Weaving** | Fur coat, fur boots and pelt hats, the **leather bedroll** (5 leather), tents, hammocks, loincloths and leaf clothing, and your first bags: the **grass carry net**, **hide sling bag** and **pack basket** |

Later, add **Stone Arms** (spears, bone knife, bone and stone pickaxes, chipping flint on a rock), **Archery** (bows and arrows), **Herbalism** (bandages, splints, the drying rack), **Primitive Armor**, **Field Defenses** and **Ritual & Worship**. The [Research](Guide_to_Research.md#full-node-reference) node reference lists every chapter.

If a craft or action fails with "You don't know how to do this yet. Study the ... chapter first", the message names the chapter you need. The **Crafting Atlas** in the IC tab shows what each recipe needs.

## Tools without a book

You can get your first tools before you have studied anything:

1. **Flint.** Click a large rock with an empty hand on **Grab intent** to find a flint. Rocks refill slowly.
2. **Sharpen it by knapping.** Hold a stone or a second, unsharpened flint, and use it on the flint (in your other hand or on the ground beside you). Choose **Sharp flake**. Knapping can also make a **flint knife**, a **flint spearhead** or three **flint arrowheads**. See [Flint](Guide_to_Flint.md).
3. **A stick.** Click a tree with an empty hand on **Harm intent** to break off a branch. Palm trees give branches too. Each tree holds only a few and they grow back, so move to another tree when one runs out; examining a tree (Shift-click) tells you how many it has left and what else you can do with it. Click the branch on **Grab intent** to pick the leaves off, then on **Harm intent** to clear the twigs. Use the sharpened flint on the stick to sharpen it.
4. **A binding.** Use the sharpened flint or knife on grass or tall grass to cut **grass cuttings**, then use two cuttings in hand to twist **crude twine**. Butchered animals also give **sinew**, which is stronger.
5. **Flint axe.** With the twine or sinew in your inventory or on the ground under you, use the sharpened flint on the sharpened stick. A twine binding wears out quickly; sinew or rope lasts much longer.

With the axe you can chop trees for **wood logs**. A flint spearhead used on a stick makes a **flint spear** for hunting. Use the axe on a stack of logs to cut **2 logs into 1 plank**; this needs no chapter. A **campfire** (4 logs) needs no chapter either. Without an axe, [Woodworking without an axe](../Miscellaneous_Crafting.md#woodworking-without-an-axe) shows the hand route with flint, wedge and mallet.

## Water

- **Drink straight from fresh water.** Click a river, lake or pond tile with an empty hand on **Grab intent**. Never drink salt water. Raw water can give you **cholera**: about a 15% chance per drink from still water, half that from a river with a current (15% from a flowing jungle river), more from still water in summer heat, 30% from flooded ground and 45% from swamps. Look closely at the water to tell which it is: "The water runs clear and quick" is the best you will find.
- **Make it safe.** With Pottery & Cookware, make a **wooden mug** to drink from and a **bucket** to carry water. Boil water in a clay **cooking pot** on a campfire before drinking it. The [Starter Guide](../Starter_Guide.md#water) explains clay and boiling step by step.
- **Milk** never needs boiling. Use a bucket on a cow, ewe or goat on **Help intent**.
- **Wells** give clean water as long as nobody leaves dung within four tiles. You can dig your own: see below.

### Digging a well

You do not need to find a puddle to have a well. Stand where you want it, use a **shovel** in your hand and choose **Well**. Each time you do, the hole goes one spit deeper and leaves a dirt pile, and you are told what the earth at the bottom is like: **dry** (water is a long way down, try lower ground), **damp** (a few spits more), **wet** (one more spit) or **water seeps in**.

- **Where to dig.** Water lies about a spit under low ground beside rivers and lakes, and deeper the higher you climb: about one spit more for every two levels of hill. Desert and other dry country hold it deeper, jungle a little nearer the surface. Hilltops have none within reach.
- **Waterhole.** A bare hole stands only **2 spits** deep. Its water is muddy, like a puddle's: boil it. It fills slowly.
- **Timber-lined well.** Stand on the hole and build a **timber well lining** (4 wood, Primitive Construction). The water is clean, it fills twice as fast, and it can be dug to **5 spits**.
- **Stone well.** Build a stone, sandstone or marble **well** on a waterhole or a timber well. It can be dug to **8 spits**.
- **Deeper is safer.** A well holds more and refills faster the more water stands in it. A river in flood lifts the water in the wells near it and a low river drops it. A **dry season** or a **heat wave** draws the water down a spit, so a well that only just reaches water goes dry then; one dug a spit or two past the water keeps going. Examine a well to see how deep it is and how much water stands in it.
- **Deep water.** When the water is more than 2 spits down you cannot drink from your hands: lower a mug or a bucket, which takes longer the deeper the water is.
- **Windlass and pail.** Stand on a lined well and build a **windlass and pail** (4 wood and 2 rope, Cooperage & Containers). One winding fills your whole bucket whatever the depth, and you can drink at the well again.
- **Hand pump.** A **cast iron hand pump** (6 iron, Iron Tools) built on a lined well does the same in half the time. It takes the place of a windlass.
- Use the shovel on a dug well to dig it deeper.
- **Removing a well.** Use a **hammer** on any well to take down its windlass or pump, then again to break up the lining; you get about half the materials back. A dug well is left as a bare waterhole, which a shovel fills in. A well built over a spring leaves its puddle behind.

If you catch cholera, see [Medical](Guide_to_Medical.md#diseases).

## Food

- **Berries.** Click a berry bush on **Grab intent** to pick some. Each kind of berry has its own effect, and the effects differ from world to world: some are tasty, some are bad for you. Try a new kind carefully.
- **Hunting.** Kill small, harmless animals such as deer, rabbits or birds; even fists work. Stay away from bears, wolves, big cats, alligators and snakes. A predator that has eaten leaves you alone unless you come within a few steps of it, and lets you back away; a starving one hunts you on sight, and one you hurt always turns on you and won't let you go. If a starving one you haven't hurt is chasing or watching you, throw meat or fish where it can see it; it goes to eat that instead, which gives you time to get away. Predators only go after other animals when they are hungry. One that brings down its prey stays to eat it until it is full, then leaves the rest of the carcass and won't hunt again until it is hungry again. Hurting it while it eats makes it turn on you. Butcher a carcass with any sharp tool, including sharpened flint, on **Harm intent**; on **Grab intent** you skin it too, for pelts and hides. Carcasses in or beside rivers can drift away, so drag them ashore first.
- **Cook your meat.** Raw meat gives food poisoning. Build a **campfire** (4 logs, no chapter needed), put the meat on it and click it to light it. Basic Toolmaking's **dehydrator** dries cutlets for food that keeps.
- **Bird eggs** are safe raw.
- **Foraging.** Use **Forage nearby** in the **Crafting** tab while standing on open grass or dirt. A 20 to 45 second search can find a stone, a small wood piece, wild seeds or, outside winter and the dry season, a bunch of edible mushrooms. Each patch of ground holds only a few finds and refills slowly, so keep moving.
- **Wild crops.** Ripe wild plants grow in open countryside away from settlements. Harvest them with a knife or hatchet for produce and seeds. See [Farming](Guide_to_Farming.md#wild-plants).
- **Fishing.** Basic Toolmaking unlocks fishing rods and nets. See [Fishing](Guide_to_Fishing.md).

Eat a mix of food. Living on one kind of food for a long time slows your stamina recovery; see [Balanced diet](Guide_to_Cooking.md#balanced-diet). For a lasting food supply, move on to [Farming](Guide_to_Farming.md) and [Ranching](Guide_to_Ranching.md).

## Shelter and warmth

Any roofed area is shelter from rain, snow, cold and the desert sun. Watch the temperature icon on your HUD.

- **Caves.** Roofed areas are darker inside. A cave is the fastest shelter, but check that nothing lives in it.
- **Build a hut.** With Primitive Construction, put up **log walls** or **roof supports**, then hold a **wood roof builder** and use it in hand while facing each tile you want to roof. See [Construction](Guide_to_Construction.md).
- **Dig down.** With Basic Toolmaking, make a **wooden spade** (3 logs), or a bone shovel from a wood handle and bone. Use it in hand and choose **Tunnel** to dig a hole to the level below. Use a **wooden ladder** (4 logs) on the hole so people can climb in and out instead of falling in. Underground, put up **mine supports** as you dig or the tunnel can cave in.
- **Fire.** A **campfire** warms you within one tile, a **brazier** (Basic Furniture, stone) within three. Feed them wood, coal or charcoal.
- **Clothes.** Skin animals for pelts, then study Weaving to make a fur coat, boots and hat. A fur coat helps in winter but won't save you in a blizzard: get under a roof.

More on cold weather is in [Winter & Long Rounds](Guide_to_Survival.md) and the [Starter Guide](../Starter_Guide.md#temperature).

## Light

Nights are dark, and a research bench makes no progress in the dark. Make a **torch** from a wood handle and animal fat (Basic Toolmaking for the handle, Primitive Construction for the torch). Use it in hand to light it; it burns for about five minutes and can be topped up with more fat or oil. A campfire also gives light. See [Night time](../Starter_Guide.md#night-time).

## Carrying more

You start with only your hands and pockets. Each of these holds more than the one before it:

| Gear | Chapter | Made from | Worn on | Holds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Grass carry net** | Weaving | 3 rope | Belt | 6 small things |
| **Hide sling bag** | Weaving | 2 pelts of almost any animal, or 2 leather | Back | 3 normal-sized or 7 small things |
| **Pack basket** | Weaving | 4 logs, soft wood or bamboo | Back | 4 normal-sized or 9 small things |
| **Leather backpack** | Cooperage & Containers (Bronze Age) | 4 leather | Back | 2 large things, or more smaller ones |
| **Pack frame** | Cooperage & Containers (Bronze Age) | 6 logs or soft wood | Back | 3 large things, but it slows your walk a little |

Rope comes from the crude twine you twist from grass cuttings or sinew. Make the carry net from a stack of rope.

To move a bigger load, build a **travois** (Primitive Construction, 8 logs, soft wood or bamboo): two poles lashed into a drag with a hide platform. It works like a crate: open it, put up to 15 things on its tile, close it, then **Ctrl-click** it to pull it behind you.

## Storing things

You don't need Cooperage to keep things safe at home. Click one of these containers with an empty hand to open it, or click it with an item to put the item straight in. Examine a container, or a bag you carry, to read how full it is.

| Container | Chapter | Made from | Holds | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Woven basket** | Weaving | 3 rope, 6 leaves, 4 bamboo or 4 planks | 7 things, up to normal size | Everyday odds and ends. It is light enough to drag about. |
| **Clay storage jar** | Pottery & Cookware | 3 clay, fired in a kiln or oven | 6 small things, such as grain, seeds, food and beads | Food keeps twice as long, and fire can't touch it. The fired jar is set down beside the oven. |
| **Hewn log chest** | Basic Furniture | 6 logs | 10 things, up to large tools and weapons | Gear too long for a basket |
| **Storage pit** | Primitive Construction | A **pit lining** (4 leaves or 3 leather) | 14 things, up to normal size | The most room of any early store. The cool earth keeps food a little longer. |

To dig a storage pit, hold a shovel or digging stick in one hand and use the pit lining on bare ground next to you (grass, dirt, sand or snow). A pit can't be moved. Fill it back in with a shovel to remove it, and whatever was inside comes out.

**Racks** hold one kind of thing each and show what hangs on them. They open like any other container, but take no lock.

| Rack | Chapter | Made from | Holds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Peg post** | Primitive Construction | 3 logs | 4 hats, clothes, bags or tools |
| **Tool rack** | Basic Furniture | 4 logs | 5 tools |
| **Weapon rack** | Basic Furniture | 5 logs | 4 weapons, bows, guns or shields |
| **Coat stand** | Fine Furniture | 4 logs | 4 coats, cloaks, hats or bags |
| **Clothes rail** | Fine Furniture | 5 logs | 5 pieces of clothing |

Right-click a container and choose **Dump Out**, or **Alt-click** it, to tip everything onto the floor at once. A broken container spills what it held.

### The storage strip

Clicking a bag, basket or other container shows its things in a row of slots along the bottom of the screen, the classic storage strip. Click the container again to close the strip. Click a thing to take it into your hand, drag it onto a hand, a slot or another bag, or click the strip with something in your hand to put it in. Taking something out takes a moment: bigger things take longer, and taking from someone else's bags is slower.

You always know what is in your own worn gear, and whatever you or your faction put into a container. Anything else shows as a grey **?** tile of its size. Stay put with the strip open and you look through the container one thing at a time, and each **?** turns into the real thing once you have seen it.

A shut crate, chest or closet tips its things onto the floor when you open it. If someone else filled it, you search it first, which takes as long as looking at each thing you haven't seen.

### The storage window

Double-click a bag or container to open the storage window instead, or right-click it and choose **Open Storage Window**. To open it without a bag, for example to pick things up off the floor, use **Open Storage Window Here** in the IC tab. To open the window every time, set **Bags and containers open in** to **Storage window** in **Character Setup > Settings**, or use **Storage Window** in the OOC tab. Use either again to switch back.

The storage window shows every bag and container you can reach at once. The left side lists **you**: both hands, the bags and belt you wear, and your pockets. The right side lists what is **nearby**: containers on the tiles around you, bags lying on the floor, the bags of anyone unconscious or tied up next to you, and loose things on the floor.

- **Drag** a thing onto a container to move it there. Or click several things to select them, then press **Move here** on the container they should go to. Enter moves your selection to the container you used last.
- **Take all** puts everything from a nearby container away: into the first bag you wear that has room, then your pockets, then your hands.
- **Dump out** asks first, then tips the container's things onto the floor.
- The **find** box at the top shows only things whose name matches.

Moving things takes a moment. Bigger things take longer, things going to or from your hands, pockets and belt are quicker, and taking from someone else's bags is slower. Moves wait their turn along the bottom of the window, and **Cancel all** stops them. Walking away, or being stunned or tied up, also stops them.

**Searching.** You always know what is in your hands, pockets and worn gear, and what lies on the floor. You also know whatever members of your own faction put into a container. In a container anyone else filled, though, the things show as grey **?** boxes of their size until you have looked at them. Opening the container (or pressing **Search** on it) looks through it one thing at a time, and bigger things take longer to make out. You can't take, find or dump anything before you have seen it. Whatever someone else puts back into a shared store has to be looked at again, and after the server restarts you have to search other people's containers again.

**False bottoms.** Hold an empty box, briefcase, toolbox or backpack in one hand and a wooden plank in the other, then use **Fit False Bottom** (IC tab). It takes about 15 seconds, uses the plank and a little of the container's room. The false bottom holds two small things that don't show when the container is opened or searched. Use **Hidden Compartment** while holding the container to hide the thing in your other hand, take things out, or pull the false bottom out again. Anyone who suspects one can hold the container and use **Search Thoroughly**, which takes about 10 seconds and finds it if it is there.

A container that is too small or too full refuses what you drop on it and says why. A locked container shows nothing of what is inside.

**Crates, chests and closets.** With the window on, click a crate, chest, closet, cabinet or fridge to open its lid and see it in the storage window: nothing spills out, and closing the lid again leaves the floor alone. Right-click one and choose **Gather** to pack the loose things on its tile into it, or **Dump Out** to tip everything out. A crate holds a set number of things, whatever their size. Mailboxes, coffins, body bags, sandbags and dumpsters work as before.

**Why bother?** A room with a basket, jar or chest and little left on the floor counts as a tidy room toward [home comfort](Guide_to_Homes.md). A basket, wooden chest or crate that catches fire burns away with everything flammable inside; metal and stone things drop out. Clay jars don't burn.

## Sleeping and logging off

**Don't just log off.** Your character stays in the world, asleep on the ground, where it can be robbed or killed and still slowly gets hungry.

1. Make a **bed** (Basic Furniture, 4 planks) or a **leather bedroll** (Weaving, 5 leather) and stand on it. Sleeping in a free bed makes it yours.
2. Put away anything in your hands.
3. Use **Sleep** in the IC tab and stay connected for the two minutes it takes.
4. Use **Wake Up** when you come back.

Outside Gaia, a faction member who slept properly in a province their faction holds and then logged off is safe until they log back in: nobody can hurt, rob or move them. Everywhere else sleepers can still be attacked, so sleep somewhere safe. While you sleep properly your hunger and thirst don't drop. You can't sleep while you have fleas in your clothes or are too dirty. Being ill does not stop you: a severe illness waits while you sleep, and flu passes twice as fast in a bed. The full rules are in [Saving your character](../Starter_Guide.md#saving-your-character).

## Finding other people

If you are alone and want company, light a campfire or fire pit under the open sky, hold leather or cloth and click the fire. A column of smoke rises, and everyone outdoors a long way off is told which way to walk to reach you. If you see someone else's smoke, walk toward it or answer with a signal of your own. Examining a lit fire reminds you how. The details are in [Smoke signals](Guide_to_Survival.md#smoke-signals).

During your first two days your characters start a short walk from where the most players are gathered outdoors, so other people are usually within reach from the first minute.

When the game asks where your new character should start, look for **Near NAME's signal fire**. Someone at that fire has invited newcomers: choose it and you start a short walk from the fire, and you are told which way it lies. You arrive on your own, not as a member of anything; joining a faction still happens face to face.

## If you die

**Death clears what your character knew**, but for one real day it can be recovered. Your next character keeps the subjects, scholarship and body skills of the last one if it starts within 150 tiles of where you died, in one of two ways:

- **With your faction**, beside its recruitment station. Choose the faction when the game asks where to start.
- **At your own bed**, if it had been your bed for at least a day before you died. A bed you claim afterwards does not count, and a destroyed bed gives no cover.

Start anywhere else, in another land, or more than a day later, and the new character knows nothing, so keep your books somewhere safe. The message you get when you die, and the starting place question, tell you what you would keep.

Coming back has a cost that depends on how you died:

| How you died | What the next character loses |
| --- | --- |
| Starved, thirst, cold, heat, illness or poison | A fifth of what each body skill had gained |
| Wounds, an animal, a fall | A tenth of what each body skill had gained |
| Drowned or suffocated | A fifth of scholarship |
| Burned | A fifth of what crafting and dexterity had gained |
| Killed by another player | The three subjects learned last and a fifth of scholarship |
| Body destroyed, or by your own hand | Everything |

A bed only partly protects you: **starting at your bed costs double**, while starting beside your faction's recruitment station costs the amount above. Inside a Town or City your side holds, a bed start costs less (see [Settlements](../gamemodes/Civilizations_and_Nomads.md#settlements-camp-to-city)). A second return within a day of the first also costs double, and the two add up.

After any death your next character is **shaken**: for 30 minutes of play it deals half damage to other people. Animals are not affected, so you can still hunt. The Status tab shows the time left.

If you had a bed of your own, your next character starts beside it; otherwise you start again on the mainland (see [Your home land](Guide_to_Travel.md#your-home-land)).

## First-day checklist

1. Get a book: found a faction or study from a friend's book.
2. Study Basic Toolmaking, Basic Furniture, Pottery & Cookware, Primitive Construction and Weaving.
3. Make a flint axe and chop some logs.
4. Drink, and make a mug, bucket and cooking pot for clean water.
5. Make a carry net or sling bag so you can carry what you gather.
6. Hunt or forage, and cook on a campfire.
7. Find a cave or build a roofed hut, and light it.
8. Make a bed, and sleep in it before you log off.
