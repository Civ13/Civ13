# Pottery, Bricks & Lime

Six workshop stations make clay, glass, stone and furnace work faster. Everything they make can still be made by hand; the stations save time, clay or fuel.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| <img src="../images/Brick_mould_bench.png"> | **Brick mould bench** | 6 wood | Brickmaking |

| <img src="../images/Lime_kiln.png"> | **Lime kiln** | 20 stone | Stone Masonry |

Fasten or unfasten a station with a wrench. To take a wheel, bench, bellows or saw apart, hit it with a hammer on **Harm intent**.

## Making your first clay pot

1. Use a **shovel** on exposed dirt to get a **dirt pile**. If grass or snow is in the way, clear it and try the exposed dirt.
2. Fill a **bucket** with water, then use it on the dirt pile. The game uses **10 units of water** and drops **2 clay** at your feet.
3. Use the clay in your active hand and choose **unfired clay cooking pot**. In the default crafting catalog this costs 2 clay; check the recipe on your map.
4. Use the unfired pot **on** a campfire or oven to load it. Add fuel if needed, light the fire with an empty hand and, once the pot is fired, take it out. Leaving the pot on the ground beside the fire won't fire it.

## Potter's wheel

Use **clay** on the wheel and choose what to throw: pots, cups, bowls, jugs, pitchers, vases, cooking pots, crocks and vessels of your own design (see **[Making a Home](Guide_to_Homes.md#pottery-of-your-own-design)**). You only see the wares you already know how to make.

Each piece costs the same clay as shaping it by hand but takes a third of the time. The wheel keeps turning out the same ware until your clay or its water runs out, or you step away. The pieces come off unfired, so put them in a fire as usual to finish them.

## Brick mould bench

Use **4 clay** on the bench and choose a brick. Ten seconds later you have **eight** unfired bricks, and the bench keeps going while you have clay and it has water.

- **Clay blocks** and **bricks** cost the same clay as by hand, but come eight at a time.
- **Cement bricks** need <img src="../images/Quicklime.png"> **quicklime**. Use quicklime on the bench to fill its tub (it holds 20). Each batch of eight cement bricks then takes 4 clay and 2 quicklime. By hand, a single cement brick takes 4 clay.
- **By hand**, clay bricks and cement bricks are mixed wet: each one also takes 10 units of water from a bucket or bowl you hold or stand on. It's free while you work beside running water: a river with a current, an aqueduct, a fountain, a tap or a sink. Paper sheets take water the same way.

Fire the bricks to finish them.

## Water for the wheel and the bench

The potter's wheel and the brick mould bench each have a water tub. The wheel uses 10 units per piece and holds 150; the bench uses 20 per batch and holds 100. Pour water in with a bucket, pipe it in, or build the station beside running water (a river with a current, an aqueduct channel, a fountain, a tap or a sink) so it never runs dry. Examine a station to see how much water it holds.

## Bellows

Build the bellows on a tile next to a bloomery, blast furnace, kiln or lime kiln. Click them with an **empty hand** to pump for three seconds.

For the next minute and a half, a firing you start in a furnace beside the bellows takes a quarter less time and a quarter less fuel. Pump first, then light the furnace. One set of bellows blows on every furnace next to it.

Bellows that a treadwheel, horse gin, mill wheel or windmill drives, beside it or along a [line shaft](#mechanical-power), blow on their own: every firing started beside them gets the blast without anyone pumping.

## Lime kiln

1. Use **stone**, **marble** or **crushed shell** on the kiln. It holds 20 in all.
2. Add fuel: wood, planks, charcoal or coal.
3. Click the kiln with an empty hand to light it.

After about 40 seconds it leaves one quicklime for each block or handful of shell. Crushed shell comes from [crab chitin](Guide_to_Ranching.md#chitin-from-crabs-and-beetles). Right-click the kiln and choose **Empty** to take an unburnt charge back out.

Quicklime goes into cement bricks at the brick mould bench, and into concrete at a [cement mixer](Guide_to_Construction.md#concrete).

## Glassblowing bench

Build the bench on a tile next to a bloomery, blast furnace or kiln that has fuel in it. The fire does not need to be lit for a smelt; the bench reheats the glass in it.

Use **glass** on the bench and choose what to blow: drinking glasses, mugs, teacups and teapots, bottles, jars, vials, beakers, droppers and lightbulbs. Each go costs the same glass and time as making one piece by hand, but you get **two** pieces. It also burns a little fuel from the fire, so keep the furnace stocked. Reinforced glass cannot be blown.

## Mason's saw

Use **stone**, **marble** or **sandstone** blocks on the saw. Every two seconds it cuts:

- one stone block into two stone bricks,
- one marble block into two marble bricks,
- three sandstone blocks into two stone bricks.

By hand each of those cuts gives a single brick. The saw keeps working through the stack in your hand until it runs out or you step away. You need to know **Brickmaking** to cut bricks, the same as by hand.

## Mechanical power

Four prime movers turn the workshop machines standing next to them, so each step of the work takes less time. A mover drives every fastened **potter's wheel**, **mason's saw**, **cement mixer**, **shear legs** and **treadwheel crane** within one tile of it. You still work the machine yourself; it just goes faster.

Each mover is built from wood plus [machine parts](Guide_to_Tool_Materials.md) of any material, held or at your feet.

| | Mover | Made from | Research | Turns when | Work takes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <img src="../images/Treadwheel.png"> | **Treadwheel** | 12 wood, 1 shaft, 2 bearings | Stone Masonry | someone stands inside the wheel | 65% of the time |
| <img src="../images/Horse_gin.png"> | **Horse gin** | 10 wood, 1 gear set, 1 shaft, 1 bearing | Resource Processing | a living horse or head of cattle stands next to it | 50% |
| <img src="../images/Mill_wheel.png"> | **Mill wheel** | 14 wood, 1 gear set, 1 shaft, 2 bearings | Watermills | it stands next to flowing river water | 40% |
| <img src="../images/Windmill.png"> | **Windmill** | 16 wood, 1 gear set, 1 shaft, 2 bearings | Watermills | it stands under open sky and the wind blows | 70% in a light wind, 50% in a moderate one, 40% in a strong wind or gale |

- A mover must be fastened (use a wrench), and so must the machine it drives.
- Click a mover with an empty hand, or examine it, to see whether it is turning and why not.
- The treadwheel needs a second person: one treads inside the wheel while the other works the machine.
- Still water and ice do not turn a mill wheel, and a windmill stands idle in a calm.
- Each mover has a **strength**: treadwheel 1, horse gin 2, mill wheel 3 (4 when fed by a mill race), windmill 1, 2 or 3 as the wind rises. Most machines draw 1; a trip hammer, rolling mill, stone crusher, water stamp mill or blast furnace draws 2. When the machines on a drive draw more than its movers' strength, they all slow down in proportion. Examine a mover to see its strength, its draw and the speed it gives.
- Movers standing next to one another, or joined by shafts, add their strength together, and the fastest one sets the pace.

Hit a mover with a hammer on **Harm intent** to take it apart. Movers are too heavy to pull up a ladder.

### Line shafts, gear boxes and clutches

Transmission parts carry a mover's drive to machines further away. Every fastened machine within one tile of any part is driven, and parts join when they stand within one tile of each other, diagonals included. Fasten each part with a wrench. Click an unfastened shaft with an empty hand to turn it round.

| | Part | Made from | Research | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <img src="../images/Line_shaft.png"> | **Wooden line shaft** | 4 wood, 1 bearing | Watermills | Carries the drive. A run with any wooden length turns at most 8 lengths |
| <img src="../images/Shaft_clutch.png"> | **Shaft clutch** | 5 wood, 1 gear set, 1 bearing | Watermills | A length of shaft you throw in or out by hand. Thrown out, everything past it stops |
| <img src="../images/Gear_box.png"> | **Gear box** | 8 wood, 2 gear sets, 1 shaft | Watermills | Joins the shafts around it to a gear box directly above or below, so a mill wheel can drive the floor above |
| <img src="../images/Iron_line_shaft.png"> | **Iron line shaft** | 3 iron, 1 bearing | Machined Tools | An all-iron run turns up to 16 lengths |
| <img src="../images/Rope_drive_wheel.png"> | **Rope drive wheel** | 4 iron, 1 bearing, 2 rope | Machined Tools | Use rope on it to tie it to another free wheel up to 7 tiles away on the same level; the drive passes between them over walls, roads or water. Use rope on it again to take the rope off |

- A run that is too long binds and carries nothing. Examine a part to see whether it turns.
- Tie a [lever's cord](Guide_to_Levers_and_Mechanisms.md) to a shaft clutch to throw it in and out from a distance.

### Counterweight drum and steam engine

Two more movers drive machines the same way as the four above.

| | Mover | Made from | Research | Turns when | Work takes | Strength |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <img src="../images/Counterweight_drum.png"> | **Counterweight drum** | 10 wood, 1 gear set, 1 shaft, 2 rope | Watermills | its stone weight is falling | 60% | 1 |
| <img src="../images/Stationary_steam_engine.png"> | **Stationary steam engine** | 16 iron, 1 gear set, 1 shaft, 2 bearings | Steam Power | it has fuel in its firebox | 35% | 6 |

- **Counterweight drum:** click it with an empty hand on Help intent to crank the weight up. Each 10 seconds of cranking stores about 5 minutes of fall, up to 15 minutes. Click it on any other intent to set or let off its brake. A [lever's cord](Guide_to_Levers_and_Mechanisms.md) can also work the brake: thrown over, it lets the weight fall; thrown back, it holds it.
- **Stationary steam engine:** stoke it with coal, charcoal or logs. It burns a minute of fuel every minute whether or not anything is working, and holds up to an hour. Coal lasts 4 minutes a piece, charcoal 3, wood 1.

### Hopper machines

These machines work on their own while they are driven. Fill the hopper, and nobody has to stand at the machine. They are built under Watermills unless the table says otherwise, and each draws 2 from its drive.

| | Machine | Made from | Feed | Each step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <img src="../images/Millstones.png"> | **Millstones** | 12 stone, 1 gear set, 1 shaft | Wheat, oats, barley or rice, one at a time or a whole sack at once | Grinds 2 grain into flour (rice comes out as rice) |
| <img src="../images/Sash_saw.png"> | **Sash saw** | 10 wood, 1 gear set, 1 shaft, 2 iron | Logs | Saws 1 log into 2 planks and a piece of bark |
| <img src="../images/Fulling_stocks.png"> | **Fulling stocks** | 10 wood, 1 shaft | Wool piles | Beats 2 wool into 2 wool cloth |
| <img src="../images/Bark_stamps.png"> | **Bark stamps** | 8 wood, 1 shaft, 2 iron | Tree bark | Crushes 2 bark into 4 handfuls of <img src="../images/Tanbark.png"> **tanbark** |
| <img src="../images/Power_loom.png"> | **Power loom** | 12 iron, 1 gear set, 1 shaft, 1 bearing (**Textile Mills**) | Cotton, retted fibre or silk thread | Weaves 4 into cloth or silk (its hopper holds 40) |

- The hopper holds 20. Click the machine with an empty hand to see how full it is and whether anything is stopping it.
- A step takes about a minute behind a horse gin, a little less behind a mill wheel, and longer behind a treadwheel or an overloaded drive.
- It stops once 20 products lie on its tile, and carries on when you clear them away.
- Take it apart with a hammer on Harm intent and you get back whatever was still in the hopper.
- Tanbark soaks out tannin just like bark (10 water for 10 tannin a handful), and a tanning drum takes it as bark, so stamping doubles the tannin from your bark.
- Every hopper has a gate that a [lever's cord](Guide_to_Levers_and_Mechanisms.md) can shut and open. A shut gate holds the feed back until it is opened again.
- [Factory belts and arms](Guide_to_Factory_Automation.md) can fill the hopper and take the products away. A belt beside the machine that runs away from it carries off each product as it is made.

The same movers drive a [trip hammer](Guide_to_Metallurgy.md#trip-hammer) beside an anvil.
