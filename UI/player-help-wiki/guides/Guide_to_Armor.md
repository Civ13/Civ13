# Guide to Armor

Armor protects by how it is built and what it is made of. It does not matter which culture a piece looks like: every look of the same kind and material protects the same, so wear whatever style you like. For bullets, see the armor notes in the [Guide to Weapons](Guide_to_Weapons.md).

## Three kinds of hit

Every hand weapon, thrown object, punch and bite hits in one of three ways:

| Kind | Weapons | What it does to armor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cut | Swords, axes, knives, claws, halberds | Best against unarmored and lightly armored targets |
| Thrust | Spears, pikes, rapiers, bayonets, javelins | Finds the gaps in mail and lamellar |
| Blow | Maces, clubs, hammers, punches, kicks | Bruises through armor that stops it and wears armor down three times as fast |

A blow your armor stops still bruises you for part of its force. A thrust that slips through mail or lamellar tells you it found a gap.

## Kinds of armor

| Kind | Good against | Weak against | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Padded (linothorax, padded caps and turbans) | Blows, arrows | Cuts, thrusts | Light |
| Leather (leather armor, leather helmets) | Cuts | Thrusts, blows | Light |
| Lamellar and scale (samurai, chinese and varangian lamellar, scale armor) | Arrows, cuts | Thrusts find gaps | Medium |
| Mail (chainmail, hauberks, coifs) | Cuts | Blows, thrusts find gaps | Medium |
| Plate (plated armor, chestplates, samurai lord armor, most european metal helmets, gauntlets) | Cuts, thrusts, arrows; fair against blows | Its weight | Heavy |

Examine any piece of armor to see what it is good and bad against and what it is made of.

## Materials

Better material makes the same kind of armor stronger. From weakest to strongest: hide or cloth, laced leather, chitin, bronze, iron, steel. Armor forged at an anvil takes the metal you forge it from, so steel plate beats iron plate.

## Layering

A **padded jack** is a quilted cloth jacket worn on your clothes under other armor. Craft it from 5 cloth once you know padded armor. It softens blows and arrows but does nothing against blades or points on its own. Wear it under mail to cover mail's weakness to maces and clubs. Chainmail can also be worn on your clothes under other armor.

Plate over mail closes the gaps that thrusts would otherwise find.

## Armor against guns

From the Imperial age, a steel anvil can forge a **proof cuirass** or a **proof do** (the same armor in the japanese style). It is steel plate on the chest and belly that turns many musket balls, but it is heavy.

From the First World War, the armor assembly workbench makes a **resin body shield** from 8 cloth and 4 resin once you know trench warfare. It is a light vest that stops shrapnel and pistol rounds, but not a rifle bullet.

The same workbench makes the **Dayfield body armor** from 4 resin laminate and 6 steel: steel plates between resin-hardened layers. It stops more than the resin shield and is not too heavy. The **heavy Dayfield body armor** takes 6 resin laminate and 14 steel. It can stop a rifle bullet, but it is very heavy. Make resin laminate at a hot press.

## Armor never goes out of date

Every armor recipe stays craftable from the age it unlocks until the end. Iron, steel and bronze anvils keep offering armor in later ages.

## Choosing armor

- Facing swords and axes: mail or plate.
- Facing spears and pikes: plate, or lamellar if you need to stay lighter.
- Facing maces and clubs: padding, or plate, or mail with a padded jack underneath.
- Facing archers: lamellar or plate.
- Moving fast: padded or leather.
