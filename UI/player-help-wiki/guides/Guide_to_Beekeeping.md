# Guide to Beekeeping

Bees give honey for food, jam and mead, and beeswax for candles and sealing wax. Kept near your fields, they also help many crops give more at harvest. You start by robbing wild nests in the Stone Age, keep bees in log, clay and straw hives once you know Basic Agriculture, and move on to frame hives and an extractor with Advanced Agriculture.

```admonish info
Wild nests live in temperate, taiga, jungle and savanna lands. Bees store honey in spring and summer, or in the wet season, and live on their stores through autumn and winter, or the dry season.
```

## Wild bees and honey

Wild honeybees nest in hollows in the trunks of living trees in temperate, taiga, jungle and savanna lands. Look for a dark hole low on a trunk with bees streaming in and out. A nest holds up to four combs. They fill again slowly in spring and summer, or in the wet season, but not in autumn, winter or the dry season.

1. **Make smoke.** Craft a **smoke bundle** from 2 leaves (tools category) and light it from any flame, such as a campfire, torch or candle. It smoulders for about three minutes. A lit torch works too.
2. **Smoke the nest.** Click the nest with the smouldering bundle or torch. The bees stay calm for about 45 seconds.
3. **Take a comb.** Click the nest with an empty hand, or faster with a knife or axe. Each try gives one **honeycomb**, and sometimes a piece of **brood comb**, which is good food in its own right. Take the last comb and the colony leaves. A new swarm may move into the empty hollow after a few hours of good weather, so leave a comb behind if you want to come back.

If you reach in without smoke, the bees chase you and sting. Only the person who disturbed them is chased. Clothes on your head, face, body and hands keep many stings off. Run far enough away, or jump into water, and they give up. A lot of stings at once can knock you down, but stings never kill. See the sting traits in the [Guide to Character](Guide_to_Character.md#traits).

Felling a tree that holds a nest drops its combs on the ground, and the colony leaves.

## Keeping bees

Once your people know **Basic Agriculture**, you can keep bees in a hive near your fields. There are three kinds, all in the tools category:

- **Log hive**: 6 soft wood. Holds up to 6 combs.
- **Clay hive**: 6 clay, dried in the sun. It holds up to 8 combs and won't burn.
- **Skep**: 8 leaves, woven into a straw dome. Holds up to 5 combs.

A new hive is empty. To get bees, wait for a **swarm**. In spring, or at the start of the wet season, strong colonies split. Half the bees leave and hang in a humming cluster near their old home for about 20 minutes. Both kept hives and wild nests swarm. Pull an empty hive right next to the cluster, then click the swarm to shake it in. Swarming bees are calm and don't sting. Once bees move in, the hive is fixed in place.

A colony grows and fills its combs while it has flowers, crops and trees to forage within a few tiles, in spring and summer or the wet season. Without forage it dwindles. Through autumn and winter, or the dry season, it lives on its stored combs. If it runs out, it starves and dies. Examine a hive to see how it is doing. Skilled farmers can read its strength and stores exactly.

To harvest, smoke the hive with a smoke bundle or torch, then cut a comb out with a knife (or slowly by hand). The combs are built into the hive, so every comb you cut also kills some of the young bees and weakens the colony. Cut without smoke and the bees chase you. Leave enough combs in autumn for the colony to last until spring.

## Frame hives

With **Advanced Agriculture**, you can keep bees the modern way, in a painted wooden **frame hive**. The bees build their comb on removable frames, so you can take honey without cutting the comb out. The kit is all in the tools category:

- **Frame hive**: 8 soft wood. Holds up to 10 frames.
- **Hive frame**: 1 soft wood.
- **Hive tool**: 1 iron. Prises the lid off or on, and takes an empty hive apart. A crowbar also works on the lid.
- **Bee smoker**: 2 iron. Pack it with leaves, light it from a flame, and use it on any hive or wild nest. Each load of leaves smoulders for about three minutes, and you can snuff it in hand.
- **Honey extractor**: 8 iron.
- **Nucleus box**: 3 soft wood.

To stock a frame hive, shake a swarm into it like any other hive. You can also catch a swarm in an empty nucleus box and tip it into the hive later with the lid off. Put frames in with the lid off, then close it again. The bees only store honey on frames, and an open hive barely fills. Frames that already have comb on them fill about twice as fast as new ones.

To harvest, smoke the hive, take the lid off, then click it to lift out each full frame. Reaching into a hive without smoke brings the bees out at you. Load up to four full frames into the honey extractor and click it to crank. Each frame gives 20 honey and comes back as a drawn frame, ready to go straight back in the hive. Draw the honey off by clicking the extractor with an open bucket, pot or bowl. You can also cut the comb off a full frame with a knife, which gives a honeycomb and a bare frame.

A colony at 60% strength or more can be split. Smoke it, take the lid off and click it with an empty nucleus box to take half the bees. Tip the box into an empty frame hive to start a second colony. Plenty of empty frames also keeps a strong colony from swarming away in spring.

## Pollination

Many crops need insects to set their best crop: apples, cherries, apricots, oranges, limes, lemons, watermelons, pumpkins, zucchini, beans, tomatoes, red peppers, coffee, cocoa, cotton and flax. When you harvest one of these with a bee colony within five tiles, there is a good chance of one extra fruit, or one extra bundle of cotton or flax. The stronger the colony, the better the chance. A busy kept hive does the most, and a wild nest in a nearby tree helps too. Grains, corn, rice, grapes and olives pollinate by wind or by themselves, so bees don't change their harvest. Experienced farmers can tell from examining a flowering crop whether bees are working it.

## Honey, beeswax, candles and mead

- **Honey**: click an open bowl, pot or bucket with a honeycomb to squeeze 10 honey into it. Honey goes into jam and mead, and counts toward the sweet part of your diet. A **crushed comb** is left in your hand.
- **Beeswax**: hold the crushed comb over a flame (click a campfire, torch or candle with it) to melt it into a cake of **beeswax**.
- **Candles**: click the beeswax with rope to dip a **beeswax candle**. It burns about 40% longer than an ordinary candle.
- **Sealing wax**: click the beeswax with a bucket, pot or bowl holding at least 5 pitch from a tar kiln, then pick a pigment (red, black, blue, green, pink or white). You get a stick that is good for about 10 seals. Wax seals on letters and papers need a stick in your other hand (see the [Guide to Paperwork](Guide_to_Paperwork.md)).
- **Mead**: honey stirred into water makes **mead must**, not mead. Pour the must into a wooden barrel and put the lid on. It ferments a little every couple of minutes. Add some yeast from a fermentation starter jar to the barrel and it ferments about three times faster. Mead left in a closed crock sours into vinegar, so store it in the barrel or in bottles.

## Beekeeping clothes

- **Bee veil** (3 cloth, hats, **Tailoring**): a broad hat with fine gauze hanging all round, so bees can't reach your face or neck.
- **Beekeeper suit** (6 cloth, jackets & vests, **Textile Mills**): a loose white suit closed at the wrists and ankles.

A veil or a suit each cuts your chance of being stung far more than an ordinary hat or coat. Wear both, plus gloves, and stings become rare. They can't be avoided completely.
