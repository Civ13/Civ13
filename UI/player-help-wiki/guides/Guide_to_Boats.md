# Boats & Ships

Anything that floats is built from the crafting menu's **vehicle parts** section. Pick the boat that fits your needs:

| Boat | Best for | Crew | Propulsion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Dugout canoe](#dugout-canoe) | Rivers, lakes and the shallows from the first age | 2 + 1 cargo | Paddling (direction keys) |
| [Raft](#raft) | Crossing a river or lake early on | 1 | Paddling (direction keys) |
| [Outrigger canoe](#outrigger-canoe) | Coasts and open sea crossings | 2 + 1 cargo | Paddling, then a sail or an engine if you add one |
| [Rigid hull inflatable boat (RHIB)](#rigid-hull-inflatable-boat) | Sturdier motorboat with a bigger engine | 2 + 1 cargo | Engine |
| [Modular ship](#modular-ships) | Carrying a crew, cargo and guns | As many as fit | Masts and sails, or an engine |
| [Submarine](#submarines) | Travelling unseen under the open ocean (Gaia) | As many as fit | Diesel engine |
| [Slipway ships](#slipway-ships) | A big ship built piece by piece on the shore | As many as fit | Sails (schooner) or a diesel engine (freighter) |

## Where to build

- Boats only move onto **water**: shallow or deep water, swamp water and flooded trenches. Dry land and thick ice stop them. Rafts, canoes and small boats crunch through **thin ice** and open it. Break a channel through thick ice with picks or axes on harm intent, and keep using it so it stays open (see [Breaking and melting ice](Guide_to_Survival.md#breaking-and-melting-ice)).
- You **can't craft while standing in deep water**, so build at the shoreline or in the shallows, facing open water.
- **Rivers flow** on North America, South America, Gaia, Europe, Jungle and Asia. Every few seconds the current carries loose items and swimmers one tile downstream, but not boats or anyone aboard one. Animal carcasses stay where they fell, so you can butcher a kill in the shallows. Lakes and ponds are still. Faint streaks slide along flowing water to show which way it runs, and examining the water tells you too.
- **The sea has currents** on North America, South America, Gaia, Europe and Asia. A belt of water a few tiles off the coast flows along the shore. See [Sea currents and swimming](#sea-currents-and-swimming).
- Small boats are built on land and dragged to the water. See [Hauling a boat over land](#hauling-a-boat-over-land).
- A flooded trench is navigable, but a boat can't cross a step where a canal goes up or down without a [pound lock](Guide_to_Trenches.md#pound-locks). Dig a channel from a lake or the sea to a sheltered building spot, or around a ship to give it room to turn.

## Hauling a boat over land

Rafts, canoes, outriggers and RHIBs can be dragged like a crate: pull the boat and walk. Use this to build away from the tide line, to launch, and to beach a boat where the current can't take it.

- **Empty her first.** On land a boat won't budge with anyone or anything aboard.
- A **raft** or **dugout canoe** is light. One person drags it at a slow walk.
- An **outrigger canoe** or a **RHIB** is heavy. Alone you crawl. Anyone standing beside her lends a hand: one helper makes her drag like a light boat, and two make her drag like a crate. An engine on her counts as one helper fewer.
- <img src="../images/Log_rollers.png"> **Log rollers** (3 wood, vehicle parts) do the work of a crew. Use them on an empty beached boat and one person can drag her easily. When she reaches the water she floats off them and they stay on the beach. To get them back sooner, right-click the boat and choose **Take Out Rollers**.
- Drag her into the shallows and she floats. Afloat, any small boat tows easily, loaded or not.

Looking at a beached boat tells you how she drags.

## Lighthouses

A **lighthouse beacon** (4 iron, needs **Iron Smithing**) is a fire basket for the top of a tower. Feed it wood (10 minutes each) or coal and charcoal (20 minutes each), up to two hours, and light it with any open flame. At night everyone on water within 120 tiles is told which way the light lies, at most once every five minutes. A **reflector lamp** (6 bronze, needs **Optics and Instruments**) does the same out to 200 tiles. Wall it in with **lantern room glazing** (4 glass, needs **Glassworking**), a pane you can see through.

## Sea currents and swimming

**Currents.** From about 4 to 16 tiles off the coast the sea flows along the shore, round bays and islands. The water right by the beach and the open sea further out are still. Faint streaks on the water show the direction, and examining the water says which way the current sets. Every few seconds the current carries loose items, swimmers and **empty rafts** one tile along the coast. It never pushes anything ashore, so to get out of it head for the beach or straight out to sea. A raft with anyone or anything aboard stays put, and so do boats and ships. Pull an empty raft into the shallows if you want to find it again. Nothing drifts on sea ice.

**Swimming.** On the open-world maps you can walk out of your depth and swim in deep water. Swimming is tiring:

- In calm water an untrained swimmer lasts about 50 seconds. In rain a little less, and in a storm about half as long. Heavy armour or a bulky suit tires you faster.
- You are warned when you are getting tired and again when you are nearly spent. After that you slip under and hold your breath: about 10 seconds for a beginner. Reach the shallows, a boat or a lifejacket in that time or you start to drown.
- Standing in the shallows, on land, or on a deck or raft gets your strength and breath back.
- If you lose your connection while swimming, your character treads water for two minutes without tiring. If you are not back by then, the sea washes them up on the nearest shore, exhausted but alive. This happens at most once every ten minutes.
- A **lifejacket** keeps you afloat for as long as you like.
- Anyone unconscious in deep water drowns straight away, so pull the wounded out.

**The swimming skill.** Swimming is a skill like the others, shown in your Stats. It goes from 100 up to 250. A better swimmer tires more slowly (about 100 seconds afloat at 200), swims faster, gets their strength back sooner and holds their breath longer (up to a minute). You learn by swimming through new water while you play: floating on the spot or paddling up and down a pond teaches nothing. The first swim in a while in each kind of water (a river, the sea, a flood, a storm sea, the seabed) teaches a little extra, and swimming beside a better swimmer teaches twice as fast. It takes about an hour of real swimming to master, and becoming skilled, expert and a master at it each teach you Natural Science.

Deep water is slow to cross, so that is not far. Swim to a nearby boat or across a channel, not across the sea. On battle maps deep water can't be entered at all.

## Dugout canoe

<img src="../images/Dugout_canoe.png"> A **dugout canoe** is a single log hollowed out with fire and an adze. It is the first boat you can make, long before rafts.

1. Craft a **dugout log** from 12 wood (or soft wood), Basic Toolmaking research. Drag it where you want to work, close to the water.
2. **Char** the top of the log with any open flame, such as a lit torch.
3. **Hollow** out the charred wood with a **stone adze**. Any hatchet works too, but more slowly. Raw wood is too hard to scoop, so always char first.
4. Repeat char and hollow three times. After the third time the log is a canoe.
5. Drag it into the water and climb in. It carries two people and one crate.

<img src="../images/Paddle.png"> Craft a **paddle** from 2 wood. If the person steering holds a paddle, the canoe moves much faster. Steer the canoe like a [raft](#raft): hold a direction key to paddle, and let go to drift to a stop.

A canoe is for rivers, lakes and the shallows along a coast. It won't go out onto the open sea, and on salt water it stays ashore in a strong wind until the wind drops. For the sea, lash an outrigger to it: see [Outrigger canoe](#outrigger-canoe).

## Raft

1. Craft a **raft** from 10 wood logs, at the water's edge or anywhere you can [drag it](#hauling-a-boat-over-land) from.
2. Drag yourself onto the raft to climb aboard. A raft carries only you.
3. Hold a **direction key** to paddle that way; the raft glides smoothly while you hold it and drifts to a stop within about half a tile when you let go. It only goes onto water.
4. Click the raft to get off.

On some maps the open sea is closed to rafts, and you will be told you can't go further. To recover the wood, hit the raft with a hammer: you get 5 planks and a rope back.

## Outrigger canoe

<img src="../images/Outrigger_canoe.png"> An **outrigger canoe** is a dugout with a float lashed out to one side on two booms. The float keeps her steady, so she can cross the open sea, including the ocean where rafts are turned back, and ride out a strong wind that keeps a plain dugout on the beach. Only a gale on salt water stops her.

Build her on a finished [dugout canoe](#dugout-canoe). The canoe has to be empty, and either ashore or in the shallows:

1. Use **4 wood** on the dugout. The two booms and the float are laid on her. She won't move like this.
2. Use **3 rope** on her to lash the booms to the hull and the float to the booms. She is now an outrigger canoe.

A hammer knocks unlashed booms off again and gives the wood back.

She carries two people and one crate and is paddled like a dugout: hold a direction key, and hold a [paddle](#dugout-canoe) to go faster. Steady or not, she is still a small boat and can be [damaged and swamped](#damaged-small-boats).

To take a dugout or an outrigger canoe apart, empty her and hit her with a hammer. You get about half the wood back, and all the rope, the sail and the engine.

### Adding a sail

Use a **small sail** on an empty outrigger canoe to step a mast. She can't carry a sail and an engine at once.

- From the steering seat, right-click the canoe and choose **Set or Furl Sail**.
- With the sail set, steer with the direction keys as usual. The wind drives her whenever that is faster than paddling, so a sail never slows you down. See [Sailing and the wind](#sailing-and-the-wind).
- In a calm you are back to the paddle.
- **Strike Mast** on the right-click menu takes the mast down and gives the sail back.

### Adding an engine

1. Build an **engine** of **100 to 400 cc** with the engine maker. Smaller or larger engines are refused. See [Boat engines](#boat-engines) for which types fit.
2. Drag the engine onto an empty outrigger canoe that has no mast.
3. Use a **fuel tank of up to 150 units** on her. The 120u small fuel tank and the bike tanks fit.
4. Fill the tank by using a barrel or other container of the fuel your engine burns on her. Examine her to see how much fuel is left.
5. From the steering seat, right-click the canoe and choose **Start or Stop Engine**, then hold a direction key to drive. A bigger engine is faster and burns more fuel for every tile. If the tank runs dry the engine stops and you paddle home.

A **wrench** takes the engine and tank off again.

## Rigid hull inflatable boat

The **RHIB** is a sturdy modern motorboat. Craft a **rigid hull inflatable boat frame** (22 planks, Crafting skill 1.35; you name the boat when you craft it), drag an engine onto the frame, then click the frame with a fuel tank. The boat is finished as soon as the tank goes in. Fill the tank by clicking the boat with a container of fuel. It takes:

- An engine of **150 to 600 cc**. See [Boat engines](#boat-engines) for which types fit.
- A fuel tank of up to **200 units**, such as the 180u medium fuel tank.

## Damaged small boats

Rafts, dugout canoes, outriggers and RHIBs are tight for as long as they are sound. Knock one about and she starts to take water.

- **Damage:** a weapon swung at her in anger (harm intent) or a shot that hits her wears her down. Blows never break her up, but they can leave her a hulk.
- **Leaking:** below half her strength she ships water as she moves, faster the worse she is. You are told when the water starts coming in and when she is half full. Looking at her tells you how damaged she is and how much water is in her. She takes no water while she lies still.
- **Swamped:** at ten bucketfuls she lies awash and will not move. You stay aboard and nothing is lost, but she goes nowhere until she is bailed.
- **Bailing:** use an **empty bucket** on her. Every three seconds takes out three bucketfuls, and you keep going until she is dry or you stop.
- **Mending:** use **wood or planks** on her, or **cloth** on a RHIB. Each piece takes five seconds and gives back a quarter of her strength. Once she is back above half she stops leaking.

Bigger hulls that you build tile by tile have their own rules: see [Bilge water](#bilge-water) and [Holes and sinking](#holes-and-sinking).

## Small boat upkeep

Rafts, dugouts and outrigger canoes wear out as they are used, faster on salt water than on fresh. A boat left alone stays as she is. Look at a boat to see what she needs. All of this work is done with her empty, ashore or in the shallows.

- **Lashings** (rafts and outrigger canoes) work loose. Slack lashings slow her, and loose ones let water in. Use **1 rope** on her to haul them tight again. A new raft is bound with bark and vines, so rope her before a long trip.
- **A soaked hull** (dugouts and outrigger canoes): the wood takes up water and she gets heavy and slow. Drag her out of the water and she dries over a few minutes. Rub **animal fat** or **tarred oakum** into the hull while she is ashore and she is dry at once, and soaks up water half as fast for a good while after.
- **A torn sail** (outrigger canoes): a sail left set in a strong wind or a gale can tear, and then it drives nothing. Use **2 cloth** on her to mend it. Furl the sail when it blows hard and it is safe.

## Boat engines

Motorboats take any engine made with the [engine maker](Guide_to_Crafting.md#engines). The **steam engine doesn't fit**, because it needs a separate furnace. The type decides which fuel the boat burns. Some types have a minimum size, which rules out the smaller outrigger sizes:

| Engine type | Smallest you can build | Outrigger canoe (100-400 cc) | RHIB (150-600 cc) |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| 2-stroke gasoline | 49 cc | 100-400 cc | 150-600 cc |
| 4-stroke gasoline, high-efficiency 4-stroke, ethanol-gasoline | 80-100 cc | 100-400 cc | 150-600 cc |
| Hot bulb, Hesselman | 200 cc | 200-400 cc | 200-600 cc |
| Turbine | 250 cc | 250-400 cc | 250-600 cc |
| Diesel, biodiesel | 300 cc | 300-400 cc | 300-600 cc |

An engine of 300 cc or more gives the fastest boat, so a 300-400 cc engine is a good choice for either hull.

## Driving a motorboat

These controls are for the RHIB. An outrigger canoe with an engine is driven from her right-click menu instead: see [Adding an engine](#adding-an-engine). See [Hotkeys](../Hotkeys.md) if you use different keys.

| Action | How |
| --- | --- |
| Board | Drag yourself onto the boat. The first person aboard drives and takes the **boat rudder** in hand. A second person rides along. |
| Start the engine | Press **Z** with the rudder in hand. The tank must have fuel. |
| Go and speed up | Press **Z** again for first gear, and again to speed up (three speeds). |
| Slow down and stop | Press **C**. |
| Steer | The boat drives the way you face. Turn with the direction keys. |
| Turn off and get off | Click the boat with the rudder. The first click shuts the engine off and the next one gets you off. |

A bigger engine moves faster: engines of 300 cc or more are quickest, and engines of 200 cc or more beat the smallest ones. The boat stops when the next tile isn't water.

Boats glide smoothly rather than jumping from tile to tile, the same way you walk. When you turn, the boat swings round to its new heading and keeps its sideways place, so you can line it up anywhere. Out of gear, it drifts on for about half a tile before it stops.

**Cargo:** a RHIB can carry **one** loose structure, such as a crate or a small cannon. Drag it onto the boat to load it. Click the boat to unload it beside you.

## Modular ships

Ships are built tile by tile out of frames, like [modular tanks](Guide_to_Crafting.md#modular-tanks), but they float. A ship can be at most **5x5 tiles** (25 frames) and should be a **rectangle**. Every ship is assembled facing **north**, so the top row of frames is the bow.

| **1** | **2** | **3** | **4** |

### Building the hull

1. Lay out **wood ship frames** (4 planks each) or **steel ship frames** (2 steel each) in the shallows, one per tile. A new frame is fixed in place, so nobody can drag it out of line; use a wrench on an unlinked frame to release it if you need to move it.
2. Add the walls **before assembly**, because a frame stops taking walls once it joins a ship. Click a wood frame with **wood logs**, or a steel frame with **steel**. Then pick a side, a thickness from 1 to 50 mm and a type:
   - **Wall**: solid and impassable, with the most health.
   - **Crenelated wall**: a wall with a gap you can walk through. Use it where the crew boards and leaves.
   - **Open window**: a weaker wall section, mostly for looks.

   A wall on the left side costs 1 log or sheet per 5 mm of thickness. The other sides cost 1 per 10 mm.

### Fitting it out

3. Place the **ship rudder control** (the wheel) on one of the frames.
4. Give the ship a way to move:
   - **Sail:** place one or more **ship masts** on frames and click each mast with a **small sail**. The mast sprite is larger than one tile, so it looks offset; this is how it sits when the ship faces north:

     <img src="../images/ship31.png">

   - **Engine:** place an engine on a frame and connect a fuel tank to it by clicking the engine with the tank (see [Engines](Guide_to_Crafting.md#engines)). Steel ships in later ages usually run on engines.

   A ship can carry both masts and an engine.

### Assembling the ship

5. Build a **ship rudder axis** on **any frame** of the ship.
   - The **light axis** carries up to **1200** weight.
   - The **heavy axis** carries up to **2500** weight.

   Both axes are made of planks, even for a steel ship.
6. Click the axis. It shows a build sheet: the size of the hull, the parts it found, and what the ship still needs before she will sail (a ship's wheel, and a mast or an engine). It also warns when her fuel tank is empty or holds a fuel her engine cannot burn. If the frames cannot be a ship, it says why: they must fill a rectangle of 2x2 to 5x5 tiles, and every frame must lie on water. Choose **Assemble it**, pick which way the bow points, and name your ship. Every ship frame joined to the one under the axis becomes part of her, with the wheel, masts, engine and fuel tank standing on them.

If you forgot a mast, an engine or the wheel, stand it on one of the ship's frames afterwards, click the axis and choose **Fit new parts**. To change the hull, drop the anchor, click the axis and choose **Take it apart**: the frames and parts stay where they are, loose, and can be assembled again. A ship registered in her papers can only be taken apart by her owners.

### Weight

Examine any frame of the ship to see its **current weight**. Every frame, every object aboard and every person adds to it.

- Under sail, the ship won't move if it weighs more than the axis can carry.
- Under engine, it also stalls if it weighs more than **twice the engine's power**. An engine of 12,000 to 15,000 cc is enough for almost any ship.

### At the helm

Stand next to the wheel and click it to open the helm. A ship starts with its **anchor down**.

| Control | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Turn Left / Turn Right** | Turns the ship a quarter turn. This only works **while moving**, at most once every 3 seconds, and fails if another ship or obstacle is in the way. Ordered between tiles, the turn is made as she reaches the next tile, and she swings round smoothly. |
| **Reverse** | Sails backwards (astern) on the same heading. Click again to go forwards. |
| **Anchor** | Drops or lifts the anchor. A ship can't move with the anchor down, and dropping it stops the ship within a tile. |
| **Sails** | Hoists or takes in the sails on every mast. |
| **Engine** | Turns the engine on or off. |
| **Increase / Decrease Speed** | Engine speed: slow, half and full. Decreasing from slow stops the ship, and decreasing again turns the engine off. |

**To set sail:** hoist the sails, then lift the anchor.

**Under engine:** turn the engine on, lift the anchor, then increase speed.

To stop, drop the anchor or take in the sails. With an engine, decrease the speed to zero. Under engine, the light axis is quicker than the heavy one.

A modular ship glides smoothly over the water with everyone and everything aboard, and her crew can walk her deck as she goes. When she stops she drifts on to the end of the tile she is on.

### Hazards

- A ship stops when its bow meets land, ice, another ship or a solid obstacle it can't break. A ship with **belt armour** on the hull tile next to the ice breaks thin ice ahead of her, as icebreakers do. Thick ice still stops her.
- A ship weighing **400 or more** smashes through most solid objects in its path. At **800 or more** it runs over anyone in the way, and lighter ships still hit them hard. Keep swimmers clear of the bow.
- Explosions can break or destroy masts. A ship with a broken mast, or a mast without a sail, won't sail.
- A ship that cannot sail can be **towed** by another modular ship: line her up behind the towing ship, facing the same way and within 2 tiles, and use a **rope** on her. See [Towing](Guide_to_Tanks.md#towing).

## Submarines

A submarine is a steel boat that can dive under the open ocean. On the surface it handles like any engine-driven [modular ship](#modular-ships). Submerged, it travels on the dark water under the sea, where ships on the surface can't see it.

Diving only works on **Gaia**, which has open sea under its deep ocean. Anywhere else a submarine is just a surface boat.

![Render using Civ13 sprites: a Scout submarine above and an Explorer submarine below, each shown twice, once with the deck plan the crew see and once with the rounded steel hull seen from outside; the bows point down](../images/civ13-submarines-scout-explorer.png)

There are two boats:

| | Scout | Explorer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Size | 3 wide, 5 long | 3 wide, 7 long |
| Cost at the pen | 90 steel, 4 glass, 12 cable | 140 steel, 8 glass, 20 cable |
| Clear deep sea to launch | 5x5 | 7x7 |
| Extra room | - | Two bunks and three lockers |
| Speed | Faster | Slower |

### Building one

1. Research **Submarines** (needs Wagons & Ships, Combustion Engine and Electricity).
2. Craft a **submarine pen** (40 steel, in the production section) on the shore. The 5x5 square northeast of the pen must be **deep saltwater** with nothing in it, and the pen needs a power cable on or next to it, like the [automobile workshop](Guide_to_Crafting.md#automobile-workshop).
3. Feed the pen steel, glass and cable, then click it and choose **Scout submarine** or **Explorer submarine**. An Explorer needs the 7x7 square northeast of the pen clear.

Or build one [by hand](#building-one-by-hand) from hull sections, without a pen.

The boat is launched into the square with an **empty diesel tank** and a vehicle key. The hull is roofed, so from outside you see only its steel shell.

### Building one by hand

You can also build a submarine without a pen, the way you build a [modular ship](#modular-ships). Everything is crafted from steel in the vehicle parts section after **Submarines** research:

| Part | Steel |
| --- | --- |
| Submarine hull section | 6 |
| Submarine controls | 20 |
| Ballast and periscope station | 10 |
| Submarine battery bank | 8 |
| Submarine air bank | 8 |

1. Lay **submarine hull sections** on surface water, one per tile, in a rectangle **3 wide**. Make it **5 long** for a Scout or **7 long** for an Explorer. For any other length from 4 to 7, or your own layout, clip a [submarine sheet](#designing-a-submarine-on-a-sheet) to the controls first.
2. Stand the parts on the sections: a **ship rudder control** (the wheel), an engine with its fuel tank, and a **ballast and periscope station**. Without the station she sails but can't dive. Battery banks, air banks, lockers and bunks are optional, but every bank counts: see [Battery, floodlights and depth](#battery-floodlights-and-depth).
3. Place the **submarine controls** on any section and click them. Choose **Assemble it**, pick which way the bow points along the long side, and name her.

Each section becomes the part of the hull its place calls for, the same as a pen-built boat: the glass bow, the control room, the lock-out hatches amidships, the watertight doors and the engine room. Place the parts with that plan in mind, because the two stern corners become **sealed tanks** that nobody can reach.

A hand-built boat dives, surfaces and docks like one from the pen. To rebuild her, surface, stop the engine, click the controls and choose **Take it apart**. The sections keep their shapes and can be assembled again facing another way. Her station and gauges come loose too.

### Designing a submarine on a sheet

A [ship's draught](#ships-draughts) can hold a submarine instead of a ship, so you can plan her inside before you build her.

- **Drawing one:** hold a blank ship's draught with a pen, pencil or pigment in your other hand, use it and choose **Draw a submarine on the sheet**. The window shows her plan 3 sections across, bow at the top and starboard on the right:
    - pick her **length**, from 4 to 7 sections;
    - under **Sections**, make a section **Hull** or a **Lock-out hatch**. Hatches go only in her side sections, not in the bow row or the engine room;
    - under **Across the bow side**, put a **Bulkhead**, a watertight **Door** or nothing across the front edge of a section;
    - under **Fittings**, stand a ship's wheel, a ballast and periscope station, a battery bank, an air bank, a locker or a berth in a section. **Right-click** takes a fitting out;
    - **Start from** loads the Scout or the Explorer to change as you like, and **Undo** steps back;
    - picking a section, wall or fitting shows what it is, what it is for and how to use it. The **Checks** list marks what still stops her being built (**Fix**), what she can be built with but will miss (**Warning**: no battery bank or no air bank leaves her half a Scout's charge or air) and advice (**Note**). Point at a line to ring the sections it is about;
    - the summary shows what she costs and her banks and hatches. **Save to the sheet** keeps the drawing.
- **What every boat has:** the glass bow on her first row, steel hull down both sides (open where a hatch is), and the stern row as her engine room: the diesel and its fuel tank in the middle with sealed tanks either side. You don't draw those.
- **The rules:** she needs exactly one wheel, one ballast and periscope station and at least one lock-out hatch. A bulkhead right across her with no door is refused, because nobody could reach the rooms behind it.
- **Building her:** use the sheet on loose **submarine controls** to clip it to them. Lay hull sections 3 wide and as long as the sheet, stand her wheel, station, banks and other fittings where the sheet shows them, put the engine and fuel tank in the middle of the stern row, and assemble her as above. Each section takes the shape the sheet gives it.
- **Copying a boat afloat:** hold a blank sheet of paper, use her ballast and periscope station and choose **Draw her lines**.
- **Sharing it:** use the sheet and choose **Export its design code** for a code starting `C13SUB`, or press **Export code** in the drawing window. **Import a design code** (or **Import code** in the window) draws any shared plan onto the sheet, as described under [Sharing plans as design codes](#sharing-plans-as-design-codes).

A slipway only builds ships, so it refuses a submarine sheet.

### Finding your way around

Both boats are laid out the same way, from bow to stern:

1. **Helm.** The ship wheel stands under the glass bow.
2. **Control room.** The **ballast and periscope station** is on one side and a dive locker for suits and bottles on the other.
3. **Lock-out chambers.** The two side sections with the yellow hatches. On the surface they are the way on and off the boat. Submerged, they are the [lock-out hatches](#diving-on-the-seabed).
4. **Engine room.** The battery bank, the air bank and the diesel engine with its tank, behind a **watertight door**. Click the hull section the door stands on to open or shut it, from either side. A shut door blocks the way.

The Explorer adds a berth with two bunks between the control room and the lock-out chambers, behind a second watertight door, and a locker space aft of the chambers.

Examine the **battery bank** or the **air bank** to read the boat's battery or air without going to the station. The two corners beside the engine are sealed tanks and can't be entered.

### Naming and painting your boat

Click the **ballast and periscope station** and choose:

- **Name the boat** to give her a name of up to 39 letters. It is what people read when they look at her hull.
- **Paint the stripes** to repaint the two stripes across the hull in orange, red, yellow, green, blue, purple, white or black. It takes a few seconds and the boat has to be on the surface. Every boat leaves the pen in orange.

![Scout and Explorer submarines with their hull stripes in each of the eight paints](../images/civ13-submarine-stripe-paints.png)

### Diving and surfacing

Click the **ballast and periscope station** to dive or surface. Each takes a few seconds.

- **Stop the boat first.** You can't dive or surface while it's moving.
- **Dive in open ocean.** The water has to be deep enough under every tile of the hull. The sea floor rises about one tile out from every coast, island and shallow, so move a little way offshore.
- **Surface into open water.** You can't come up under another ship, a raft, a bridge or anything else floating above you.
- Everything aboard goes down with the boat: crew, crates, machinery and anyone standing on the deck. Nobody can step straight off a submerged boat; divers go out through the [lock-out hatches](#diving-on-the-seabed).

Submerged, you steer and set speed at the ship wheel as usual. If the sea floor rises ahead, the sonar warns you and the boat stops. Turn away, or surface and continue on the surface.

### Air aboard

Once the hatches close for a dive, the crew breathe the air sealed inside the hull. Blowers keep it fresh with compressed air from the boat's **air bank**. Choose **Status** at the ballast and periscope station to check the hull air, how full the bank is, the battery and the hull.

- A full bank lasts a long time with a small crew, but the more people aboard, the faster it empties. Refilling diving bottles and blowing the lock-out chamber dry also draw on it.
- If the bank runs dry, the air goes stale and everyone aboard starts to suffocate. Surface before that happens, or seal yourself into diving gear.
- On the surface the hatches air the boat out and the compressor refills the bank in a couple of minutes.

### Battery, floodlights and depth

**Battery.** Underwater, the motors, the floodlights and the sonar all run on the boat's battery. The diesel charges it whenever the boat is on the surface, and a Scout's flat battery charges in about two minutes. If the battery goes flat while you're submerged, the boat stops dead where it is, so blow the ballast and surface to charge it.

**Banks.** Her hull holds half a bank of battery and half a bank of air on its own. Each **battery bank** and **air bank** standing in her adds a full bank, so the Scout and Explorer, with one of each, hold a bank and a half. Fit more banks to stay down longer; a bigger battery also takes longer to charge.

**Floodlights.** The sea below is pitch dark. Switch the floodlights on or off at the ballast and periscope station. They throw a white beam from the helm at the bow while the boat is submerged and slowly drain the battery.

**Depth.** The farther from land, the deeper the water: the shelf, open water, the deep and the abyss (see [Diving on the seabed](#diving-on-the-seabed)). A steel submarine is built for open water. Go deeper and the hull groans and springs leaks. Each leak lets the air out, so the air bank empties faster.

- Use a **shoring kit** on the ballast and periscope station to plug one leak.
- **Deep hull plating** (30 steel, needs **Naval Aviation** research) rates the hull for the deep. Fit it on the surface by using it on the station.

**Sonar ping.** Choose **Sonar ping** at the station while submerged. It reports the depth here, how far the rock is in every direction within 20 tiles, and the contacts in range. Each ping costs a little battery.

### Periscope and contacts

While submerged, choose **Raise periscope** at the station to see the surface straight above the boat. The view follows the boat as it moves. Step away from the station, or choose **Lower periscope**, to put it down.

**Contacts** lists what's around you, with the range in tiles, the bearing and the heading:

- **Ships** on the surface within 30 tiles.
- **Aircraft** flying within 30 tiles.
- **Submerged boats** within 20 tiles, by sonar, only while you are submerged yourself. Sonar can't tell you whose boat it is.

### Loading and unloading

Surface next to a pier, a beach or another ship. Then walk off through the lock-out chambers amidships, which stand open on both sides on the surface, and carry or pull crates on and off. Cargo left on the deck stays aboard when you dive.

### Diving on the seabed

Under the open ocean lies the seabed: cold, dark water where nothing can breathe. You can walk it if you are sealed in diving gear. All three pieces need **Submarines** research:

- **Diving dress**: 12 cloth.
- **Diving helmet**: 8 brass. Use it in your hand to turn its lamp on or off. The seabed is pitch dark.
- **Diving air bottle**: 5 steel. Wear it on your back. Refill it from the submarine's air bank at the **ballast and periscope station**, at a habitat module's core or at a landed lander.

Wear the dress, the helmet and a bottle with air in it, and you are sealed.

![A diver in the canvas diving dress and brass helmet and a diver in the steel hard dive suit, each seen from four sides with twin air bottles on the back, above the five item icons](../images/civ13-diving-gear-sprites.png)

The top row is the diving dress with the diving helmet. The second row is the hard dive suit with its helmet. The air bottles show on your back.

**Leaving the boat.** When the boat is submerged, the two lock-out chambers amidships (the side sections with the yellow hatches) work as **lock-out hatches**. Stand next to one inside the boat, click it and choose **Lock out**. The chamber floods, and after a few seconds you step out onto the seabed beside the hull. To get back in, stand next to the same hatch on the outside and click it. Compressed air from the boat's air bank blows the chamber dry and you are back aboard. If the bank is empty you have to pump the chamber out by hand, which takes about 20 seconds. You can't use a lock-out hatch without sealed gear, or if rock or wreckage is pressed against it.

**Securing the hatch.** From inside, click a lock-out hatch and choose **Dog the outer hatch**. A dogged hatch can't be opened from the seabed, so divers outside can't get in. They can only hammer on it, and everyone aboard hears it. Choose **Free the outer hatch** to let them in again. Whoever goes out through a hatch undogs it on the way out, so dog it again behind them.

**Torpedo tubes.** A submarine can carry torpedo tubes in her bow row, drawn as the **torpedo tube** fitting on her sheet (25 steel each). A tube anywhere else is refused. A tube in a submarine always fires along her heading. Submerged, she fires from periscope depth: the torpedo runs along the surface above her bow, or below it when something is floating there, so it can strike another submerged boat.

**Locking out through a tube.** From inside a submerged boat, click a torpedo tube for its menu:

- **Lock out through the tube** puts a diver in sealed gear out onto the seabed ahead of her bow. It takes 20 seconds, and the tube must be empty.
- **Open or shut the outer door for divers** controls who can come back in. A diver outside can climb back in only through an open outer door and an empty tube. That takes 20 seconds with air in the bank, or 40 seconds pumped by hand. The door shuts behind them.
- At a shut outer door, a diver can only hammer for the crew.
- A tube can't fire or be loaded while a diver is inside it.

**Escaping a sunk boat.** Each lock-out chamber is also an escape trunk. Without any diving gear, click the hatch from inside and choose **Escape to the surface**. The chamber floods, and you make a free ascent to the open sea above the hatch. From the shelf you come up short of breath. From open water you come up gasping and hurt. From the deep or the abyss you can't escape this way, so wait for help or go out in a suit built for that depth. Nothing can be floating above the hatch.

**Depth.** The farther you are from land, the deeper the water gets. Examine the water to see how deep you are. The four depths are:

1. **The shelf**, close to coasts and islands.
2. **Open water**.
3. **The deep**.
4. **The abyss**, far from any land.

A diving dress is only built for the shelf. Deeper than that it groans, loses integrity every few seconds, and finally tears, and then you drown. Patch a worn suit with a suit patch kit.

To go deeper, build a **hard dive suit** (20 steel) and a **hard dive helmet** (10 steel). Both need **Naval Aviation** research. The suit is built for the deep and takes hits better, and the helmet has a brighter lamp, but the suit is heavy and slow. The abyss is still too deep for it.

**Air.** A full bottle lasts about 30 minutes. A badly worn suit leaks and empties the bottle three times faster.

**Getting out in trouble.** If you can't get back to your boat, use **Drop Weights and Ascend** (IC tab). After about 15 seconds you swim up to the open sea straight above, as long as nothing is floating there. The dress keeps you afloat and breathing while your air lasts, so swim for a boat or the shore.

Anything you drop on the seabed stays where it fell.

### Free diving

You don't need a suit to visit the shelf. Swimming in the open sea, use **Dive** (IC tab) or the HUD down arrow and after two seconds you are on the seabed straight below, holding your breath. You need to be rested: not nearly spent, and with your breath back from the last dive.

- Your breath lasts as long as your swimming skill allows: about 10 seconds for a beginner, up to a minute for a master. You are warned when your chest starts to burn.
- Down there you swim as fast as you do at the surface. Pick up whatever has sunk to the seabed: lost tools, cargo, weapons.
- Anyone can reach the shelf. **Open water** needs a swimming skill of 200. The deep and the abyss are only for sealed suits.
- Use **Surface** (IC tab) or the HUD up arrow to swim back up: about 3 seconds from the shelf and 6 from open water, and it uses breath on the way.
- If your breath runs out you claw your way up half drowned. Under a boat or raft there is no way up, so you drown where you are. Anyone who passes out down there drowns.

Diving teaches swimming too, open water more than the shelf.

### Scuba diving

**Scuba Diving** research (it needs Submarines) unlocks lighter gear for swimming the seabed instead of walking it:

- **Dive mask**: 2 glass.
- **Swim fins**: 3 plastic. They make swimming and wading much faster but walking on land clumsy, so take them off ashore.
- **Scuba set**: 6 steel. An air cylinder with a regulator, worn on your back. Refill it where you refill diving bottles.

![The diving dress, the hard dive suit and the new scuba kit (dive mask, scuba set and swim fins) each seen from four sides, above the eight item icons](../images/civ13-scuba-gear-sprites.png)

The third row is the scuba kit: dive mask, scuba set and swim fins.

Wear the mask and a scuba set with air in it, then **Dive** from the open sea as a free diver would. You breathe from the set, so there is no breath to hold, and you can reach **open water** at any skill. The deep still needs a hard dive suit. A full set lasts about 10 minutes, longer for a good swimmer who breathes calmly (about 14 minutes at swimming skill 200). If it runs dry you are back to holding your breath.

**The bends.** Breathing compressed air in open water builds up in your body. Coming straight up after a long dive out there gives you **the bends**: aching joints, failing strength, harm to the body, and after a bad case your legs give way. It can kill if you ignore it. Lying down helps it pass. To avoid it:

- Spend time on the shelf on your way back. The shelf lets your body clear (a safety stop), and you are told when you have waited long enough.
- Or use **Ascend Slowly** (IC tab) instead of Surface. You rise with a safety stop on the way, 20 seconds to a minute depending on how long you were down and how good a swimmer you are.

Short dives and dives on the shelf never give you the bends.

### Sea life

The seabed has its own animals. They live only in the water and keep to their own depths. None of them can get into a boat.

- **Silverfin shoals** (shelf and open water) and **reef darters** (shelf) swim in schools. They scatter from a diver who comes close, from a diver with a lit lamp farther off, from a boat's floodlights and from a sonar ping, then gather again. Butcher a dead shoal with a knife for raw fish.
- **Kelp turtles** graze slowly along the shelf and hurry away when they are hurt or startled. They give meat.
- **Reef eels** hide in crevices where the water meets rock. Swim within two tiles of one and it lunges out and bites, then goes back to its hole. A bite hurts and scores your suit, so keep an eye on its integrity.
- **Drift jellies** glow softly as they drift through open and deep water. Swimming into one gets you stung. The threads can't get through a hard dive suit.

## Slipway ships

Bigger ships are built on a **slipway**: a berth on the shore where the crew add the ship one piece at a time, then launch her into the sea. Two ships can be built this way:

| Ship | Research | Size | Pieces | Materials in all |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sailing schooner | Wagons & Ships | 14 x 5 tiles, one deck | 16 | 180 planks, 20 cloth, 4 rope |
| Motor freighter | Combustion Engine | 20 x 7 tiles, deck and hold | 20 | 214 steel, 20 cable, 10 glass |
| Light cruiser | Naval Mines | 20 x 7 tiles, deck and hold | 26 | 578 steel, 20 cable, 10 glass |

### Setting up the slipway

1. Craft a **slipway** (20 planks, in the vehicle parts section) on the shore, **facing the water**. The ground in front of it is the berth: the ship is built there with her stern at the slipway and her bow towards the sea. Use a **wrench** on an empty slipway to turn it.
2. The berth can be sand, grass, any other floor, shallow water or a dug trench. It must be clear of walls and solid objects.
3. Click the slipway and choose **Lay the keel** of the ship you want. It refuses with a reason if the berth is blocked, your faction lacks the research, or there is no **open deep water** straight ahead for the finished ship to float in. The freighter also needs a level below the sea for her hold, which Gaia has.

### Building her

The slipway tells you what the next piece needs, for example "the next keel timber (1 of 2) needs 10 wood". Hit the slipway, or any part of the ship on the berth, with that material. After a few seconds the stack pays what the piece still needs and you keep the rest. Anyone can help, and the pieces can be brought over as many trips and days as you like: the project is saved with the world.

The ship grows on the berth from stern to bow: the keel down the middle, then the ribs, the hull and the deck, then the masts, wheel or wheelhouse. You can walk over her while she is being built.

From bottom to top: half the keel laid, half the planking on the ribs, the deck finished, and a finished schooner with her masts and wheel.

| Schooner stage | Pieces | Each piece |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keel | 2 | 10 planks |
| Frames | 4 | 10 planks |
| Planking | 4 | 15 planks |
| Deck | 3 | 10 planks |
| Masts and rigging | 2 | 10 planks, 10 cloth, 2 rope |
| Wheel | 1 | 10 planks |

| Freighter stage | Pieces | Each piece |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keel | 2 | 10 steel |

To give up on a ship, click the slipway and choose **Abandon**. She is taken apart, and every material fitted so far, along with anything already paid towards the next piece, is stacked by the post. A ship built from a draught is different: her floors and fittings stay standing where they were built, and only what was paid towards the next piece comes back.

### Launching

When the last piece is fitted, click the slipway and choose **Launch**. The ship slides straight down the ways until she floats on open deep water, up to twelve tiles past the end of the berth. Everyone and everything standing on her goes with her. She floats with her anchor down and empty bunkers, and the slipway is ready for the next keel.

- Every tile she slides over must be clear: a wall, a tree, a wreck or another boat in the way stops the launch.
- **Dry dock:** instead of the open shore you can dig a trench for the berth. Build the ship in the dry trench, then dig through to the sea so the trench floods. A dry dock that is not flooded refuses the launch.

![A launched schooner and a launched freighter afloat off the shore, and a freighter half built on its slipway](../images/civ13-slipway-launched.png)

A freighter and a schooner afloat after their launch, with another freighter on the slipway between them.

### Sailing them

- **Sailing schooner:** she comes off the ways as a [hull of your own](#building-your-own-hull): 62 tiles of ship floor, two rigged masts and a **tiller** at the stern, named after her design. Sail her from the tiller like any built hull: she glides smoothly with her crew walking her deck, needs **no fuel**, and her two sails make half ahead on a reach (slower running before the wind or beating into it). Bilge, seams, anchors and rigging work as for any built hull. Register her in the papers at the tiller.
- **Motor freighter:** she also comes off the ways as a [hull of your own](#building-your-own-hull), with a steel deck, the wheelhouse at the stern with her **tiller** inside, and a ladder amidships down to her **cargo hold** below the waterline. The hold sails with her, along with everyone and everything in it. Through the door at the back of the hold is the **engine room** with a **diesel engine** and its fuel **barrel**. Pour **diesel** into the barrel (250 units) and click the engine to start it: with it running she makes **full ahead**, and with it stopped she can only be poled along at slow ahead. Sail her from the tiller like any built hull. She glides smoothly and burns fuel as an engine does on any hull. Register her in the papers at the tiller.
- **Light cruiser:** a warship on the freighter's hull, with the same hold, engine room and engine, and the same handling. She carries a [gun turret](#building-your-own-hull) fore and aft. Each turret stands over a **shell rack** in her hold, so stock the racks with shells and Alt-click a turret to hoist one into the breech. Her sides are lined with **belt armour** plates. The racks come empty.

### Hauling a ship out for repairs

A [hull you built](#building-your-own-hull) can come back up an empty slipway for work on her bottom.

1. Bring her to a stop no more than 12 tiles off the foot of the ways, with her **bow pointing to the open water** the way the slipway faces. Her stern must line up with the post.
2. Get enough people beside the post, standing on the shore and not aboard her: one person for every 20 tiles of hull. A horse or an ox counts as two.
3. Click the post and choose **Haul the (her name) out**. After about three seconds for each tile she moves, she is drawn up stern first until her stern sits in front of the post. Anyone aboard rides up with her.

Everywhere she will rest must be dry open ground, and nothing solid can be in the way up the ways.

Hauled out, she lies **dry**. Her bilge drains, and nothing seeps, wears or grows on her. You can [caulk her tight](#caulking), [scrape and copper her bottom](#a-foul-bottom) and [replace her patches](#working-on-a-boat-in-the-boathouse-slip) just as in a boathouse slip. The post offers **Survey her condition**, and **Launch the (her name) again** to slide her back down the ways into the water. No keel can be laid on the berth while she is on it.

### Drydock

A **drydock** is a basin you dig at the shore, closed off from the sea by a **dock gate**. A [hull you built](#building-your-own-hull) can be floated in, pumped dry and worked on.

1. Dig a **trench** basin inland from the water, big enough for your ship, with one mouth onto open water. It can hold up to 400 tiles. Apart from its mouth, it must not touch any other open water, diagonals included.
2. Craft a **dock gate** (24 stone, construction, Wagons & Ships) and build it on the flooded trench tile at the mouth. For a wide mouth, set several gates side by side; they work together.
3. With the gate open, sail her in until **every tile of her lies inside the basin**, then stop her.
4. Click the gate and choose **Shut the gate**, then **Pump out the dock**. Pumping takes about two seconds for each tile of the basin. Hand bilge pumps within 3 tiles of a gate speed it up a little, and steam or sump pumps a lot.

Once the basin is dry she lies **dry**, just as when she is [hauled out on a slipway](#hauling-a-ship-out-for-repairs). You can caulk her, scrape and copper her bottom and replace her patches. The gate offers **Survey her condition**.

**Keel blocks** (6 wood, construction, Wagons & Ships) can be set on the dock floor or a slipway berth for a ship to rest on. They are for show: she lies dry without them.

To leave, choose **Open the sluices** at the gate. After ten seconds the basin floods again, then choose **Open the gate** and sail out. Even while the basin is flooded, a hull lying wholly inside it counts as at a berth, as in a [boathouse slip](#working-on-a-boat-in-the-boathouse-slip).

### Planning a harbor on paper

A [ship's draught](#ships-draughts) can hold a **harbor plan** to help you lay out a pier, slipway, boathouse slip or drydock before you start work. Hold a blank draught with a pen or pencil in your other hand, use it and choose **Draw a harbor on the sheet**.

1. Pick the project: a **timber pier**, a **steel pier**, a **slipway** (for a schooner or a freighter), a **boathouse slip** or a **drydock**. Piers and drydocks can be made longer or shorter, and a drydock wider.
2. Stand where the work starts, at the pier's start, the slipway post, the boathouse or the drydock's gate, and face the way it should run. Press **Survey from here**, and use **Rotate** to turn the plan.
3. The plan colours each tile by what is there now: deep or shallow water, flooded or dry trench, ice, land or solid ground. A red **!** marks a tile to look at, and **?** a tile you can't see from where you stand. Only tiles your character can see in good light are surveyed, so walk around the site and survey again if parts are unseen. For a slipway the dashed rows ahead show the run she will slide down at launch.
4. The checks under the plan list what is likely to go wrong, and the sheet lists the main materials.

The plan never builds, digs or launches anything, and a clean survey is not a promise: the real slipway post, dock gate or pile driver makes its own checks when you build. **Save on sheet**, **Export** and **Import** work as for any draught. The design code (starting `C13HARBOR`) holds only the project and its size, not the place, so someone else can use it at another shore.

### Building and refitting at a berth

A [hull of your own](#building-your-own-hull) can be laid down, and refitted, out of the water at a berth:

- **On a slipway** with no keel laid, lay her floors on the dry ground in front of the post. Her **stern must be on the tile in front of the post** and her bow must point the way the slipway faces. Commission her at the tiller and she lies hauled out. When she is ready, choose **Launch the (her name) again** at the post.
- **In a drydock**, lay her floors anywhere in the basin, dry or flooded. When the dock floods, she floats.

A hull lying on either berth can have floors added or taken up, then be surveyed again at the tiller with **Refit the hull**, just as when she is afloat. Floors that run off the berth onto the shore join her to the land, so leave a gap.

**Refitting to a draught.** A hull at any berth (a boathouse slip, a slipway or a drydock) can be rebuilt to a [ship's draught](#ships-draughts). Hold the draught at her tiller and choose **Mark out a refit**. The draught is laid over her, with its tiller on hers and its bow pointing her way, and every tile of her main deck that differs is chalked:

| Mark | Work |
| --- | --- |
| Green | Lay a floor here, with the fitting the draught shows |
| Red | Lift the floor and anything on it |
| Amber | Change the floor or fitting for another |
| Faint amber | Turn the fitting to face another way |

Do the work by hand, at the usual cost. A floor lifted at a berth is not a leak. Choose **Refit the hull** at the tiller to survey her new lines, and the marks are checked again. **Check the refit** lists what is left, and **Clear the refit marks** rubs them out. Once she matches her draught, the marks are gone. Only her main deck is chalked, and the marks do not survive a server restart.

**Adding a cargo hold.** A ship already afloat can be given a **hold** below the waterline, where the world has a level below the sea (Gaia has one). Use **Draw her lines** at her tiller, open the draught, switch to the **Hold** deck and draw the hold under her hull, with a **ladder** down to it. Bring her to a berth and **Mark out a refit** with that draught. The tiller then counts the hold still to dig, and offers **Dig out the hold**. Choose it while holding the materials: each tile of hold takes **1 steel** and about 3 seconds, and each ladder takes **4 wood** once the floor under it is down. The crew keeps digging while the stack in your hand lasts, and a [yard office](#yard-office) nearby speeds the work. A hold goes only under tiles of her deck, in rock or deep water, and her deck, hold and decks above together may be at most **300 tiles**. Whatever you stow down there sails with her and counts toward her load. Hold floors the draught does not show are left as they are.

### Yard office

A **yard office** (30 wood, construction, Workshops) turns the slipways, boathouses and dock gates within **15 tiles** of it into a shipyard:

- **Faster work.** Work in its reach takes two thirds of the usual time: fitting pieces on a slipway, hauling a ship up the ways, replacing patches, and pumping out or flooding a dock.
- **Shared stock.** Hit the office with wood, steel, iron, planks, aluminium, glass, rope, cloth or cable to store it. At a slipway post in reach, **Fit from the yard stock** fits piece after piece from what the office holds. Take any material back out from the office's menu.
- **Draught shelf.** Hit the office with a ship's draught to file it (up to 20). Take any one back down from its menu.
- **Survey the yard** lists the berths in reach and the ships they hold.

The yard office is not the shipyard office that lays down an aircraft carrier.

### Breaking a ship up for parts

A ship you built can be taken apart again at a berth: hauled out on a slipway, in a drydock basin, or in a boathouse slip.

1. Bring her to a stop at the berth and get everyone off her. Drive off any vehicle parked aboard.
2. Use the slipway post, the dock gate or the boathouse and choose **Break up the (her name) for parts**.
3. A window says what comes back and how long it takes. Nothing starts until you choose **Yes**.
4. Stay by the berth while you work. Others can join in to finish sooner, and a [yard office](#yard-office) in reach speeds it up too. If you stop, the work done so far is kept and she stays whole until the end.

- **What comes back:** everything her floors, walls, fittings and gunwales cost to build, stacked by the berth. Damaged parts give back less, and a patched plank gives back half.
- **Cargo:** everything aboard that is not part of her is set down whole by the berth, including what is in her chests, her engines and any machines.
- **Time:** about half as long as building her on a slipway: a second and a half for each floor and two and a half for each fitting.
- **Who may:** if she is registered at her helm, only members of her owning group or her listed captain. A ship registered to nobody can be broken up by any player with a verified Discord account, so register her to keep her safe.

## Building your own hull

You can also design a ship yourself. A hull is nothing more than **floors laid over water**, in any shape you like, with a **tiller** on one of them.

1. Craft **floor covers** (2 wood each, construction) and lay them over water: a river, a lake or the shallows. You can also lay them on a slipway or in a drydock, see [Building and refitting at a berth](#building-and-refitting-at-a-berth). Floors cannot be laid on deep salt water, so build near the shore and sail out.
2. Keep the hull **free of the shore**. A floor that touches a floor on land makes it a jetty, and a jetty cannot be commissioned. Leave a one-tile gap and step across, or cut the last plank when you are ready.
3. Craft a **tiller** (10 planks, vehicle parts) and set it on one of the floors. From the Classical Age a **steering oar** (6 planks, Oared Ships) does the same job. The tiller is the point the ship turns about, so somewhere near the middle is best.
4. **Close her sides.** A hull of more than 12 tiles is a boat or ship, and every outer edge of her deck needs a **gunwale** before she can be commissioned. Craft a **wooden gunwale** (2 wood, railings) or, on a steel deck, a **steel bulwark** (2 steel, railings, with Steelmaking) and build it along the edge like a railing. A wall, window or door standing on an edge tile closes that tile too, and any other railing or railing gate closes the side it runs along. The quick way is to hold wood or steel and choose **Rail her sides** at the tiller: it fits one on every open side, 2 a side, gunwales on plank deck from wood and bulwarks on steel deck from steel. Rafts and other small craft of up to 12 tiles need no gunwales.
5. Click the tiller, choose where the **bow** points and **name her**. Every floor joined edge to edge with the tiller's tile becomes the ship, up to **200 tiles**. If a side is still open she will not be commissioned: the open sides are marked on her deck for a few seconds.

Click the tiller to sail:

| Choice | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Weigh anchor / Drop anchor** | A new hull lies stopped until her anchor is weighed. See [Anchors and mooring](#anchors-and-mooring) for what holds her |
| **Slow ahead**, **Half ahead**, **Full ahead**, **All stop** | Under way she creeps smoothly along her heading with every floor, wall, crate and person aboard, and her crew can keep walking her deck as she goes: a tile every **15 seconds** at slow ahead, every **7.5** at half and every **5** at full. Half ahead needs sails, oars or an engine, full ahead needs engines or oars (below) |
| **Head north / east / south / west** | She comes about around the tiller five seconds later, half a second more for every tile of her length past ten (at most 30 seconds); she swings round smoothly and everything aboard turns with her |
| **Sea chart** | Where she is, the ships her lookout sees and every harbour on this sea (see [Harbours and the sea chart](#harbours-and-the-sea-chart)) |
| **Make for a berth** | Slides her alongside a mooring post within 20 tiles |
| **Hail a ship** | Calls across to a ship her lookout sees (see [Hails and running dark](#hails-and-running-dark)) |
| **Run dark / Show her lights** | Hides her name from other ships, at slow ahead only |
| **Grapple a ship** | Throws lines at a ship within 3 tiles and holds both fast (see [Grappling and boarding](#grappling-and-boarding)) |
| **Ship's papers** | Register her so nobody can sail her away while you are gone (see [Ship's papers](Guide_to_Aircraft_Carriers.md#ships-papers)) |
| **Draw her lines** | Hold a blank sheet of paper: it becomes her **ship's draught** (see [Ship's draughts](#ships-draughts)) |
| **Rail her sides** | Shown while a side of her deck is open: fits gunwales or bulwarks from the wood or steel in your hand |
| **Refit the hull** | At anchor: surveys the hull again after you added or cut away floors. Her sides must be closed first |
| **Strike the hull** | She stops being a ship; the floors stay where they are |

- **Sails:** with nothing to drive her she is poled along at slow ahead. Craft a **ship mast** (15 planks, vehicle parts), stand it on one of her floors and rig a **small sail** on it (click the mast with the sail). With the wind across her, one rigged mast gives half ahead to a hull of up to **31 tiles**, two masts up to 62, and so on. Running straight before the wind a mast drives 23 tiles, and beating into it only 8 (see [Sailing and the wind](#sailing-and-the-wind)). The tiller tells you how many sails are drawing, how the wind stands to her and the best speed she makes. Sails are set while she is under way and furled at anchor, and need no fuel.
- **Oars:** craft **rowing benches** (6 planks each, Oared Ships) and set them on her floors. Someone sitting on a bench (drag yourself onto it) rows when she is under way. Each manned bench pushes **4 tiles** of hull, and oars count toward full ahead as well as half ahead, so seven benches row a 13-tile galley at full ahead. Rowing tires the rowers: slow ahead can be kept up for as long as you like, half ahead wears a crew out over a few minutes and full ahead in about two. A rower who is spent stops pulling and she loses way, so change rowers or ease off and let them rest. On a [voyage](#voyages-to-new-lands) oars are no faster than sails.
- **Engines:** stand any engine on one of her floors and start it as you would ashore: a steam engine with its fire lit beside it, or a fuel engine with a fuel tank connected. A running engine pushes as much as its power output. Half ahead needs **1 push for every tile of hull** from sails and engines together; full ahead needs **2 for every tile from engines and oars**. So a 66-power hot bulb engine drives 66 tiles at half ahead or 33 at full. The tiller shows what her drives push and what her hull needs.
- **Hull weight:** a plank floor weighs one tile of hull. A floor of steel, iron, stone or concrete weighs about two thirds more and an aluminium one a little less, so a heavy deck needs more push for the same speed. Every tile of an upper deck counts as well. The ship designer's estimate counts the same weights.
- **Fuel:** a fuel engine burns from its own tank every 30 seconds under way, three times as much at full ahead as at slow, on top of what it burns idling. When the tank cannot pay the engine stalls and she drops to the best speed her other drives make. Stop the engine at anchor to save fuel.
- The water she is about to cover is **marked in yellow** five seconds before she moves onto it and before a turn.
- She stops by herself, and tells everyone aboard why, at land, at somebody else's floor, at another ship, at anything solid in the water and at the edge of the world.
- **Canals:** she floats in a **flooded trench** as well as on open water, so you can dig a canal or a sheltered harbour for her. Every tile of her keeps the canal's levels: no part of her crosses a step where the canal goes up or down. A [pound lock](Guide_to_Trenches.md#pound-locks) lets a tile in only while its chamber stands at that tile's water level, and a lock is one tile long, so a hull longer than one tile cannot be lifted over a step. Keep her canals level.
- **Pushing her off:** a ship stuck against the shore with no room to turn can be pushed by hand. Stand beside her on land or in the shallows, not on her deck or in deep water, and click one of her floors with an empty hand. After a three-second heave she moves one tile away from you, keeping her heading. One person can push a hull of about **20 tiles**; a bigger ship needs more people heaving the same way within a few seconds of each other, and stronger people count for more. She can't be pushed while she is under way, and land or anything solid still stops her.
- **Gunwales** are part of her hull: they add no weight, sail and turn with her, cross the sea with her, and shots fly over them. Climb over one like a railing to go over the side. Climbing over into open water asks first. A boat or ship commissioned before gunwales were needed keeps sailing with open sides, and her tiller counts them; rail them before you refit her.
- **Going aloft:** grab a ship mast with a free hand (or right-click it and choose **Climb the Mast**) to climb to the masthead. From up there you see over walls and hills, **Look into Distance** reaches 8 tiles, and her lookout sees **4 tiles further** through haze, rain and dark. Use **8 wood** on a mast to build a **crow's nest** on it, or craft a **ship mast with a crow's nest** (23 wood, vehicle parts, Wagons & Ships): from the nest the lookout sees **8 tiles further** and you look 11 tiles out. Move, grab the mast again or use **Climb Down** to come down. Being stunned up there, or the mast breaking, is a fall.
- **Gangways:** craft a **wooden gangway** (4 wood, railings) or a **steel gangway** (4 steel, railings, with Steelmaking) and build it in her side instead of a gunwale. It closes her side and opens like a gate. With her stopped, **Alt-click** it (or right-click it and choose **Run out or take in the gangplank**) to run a gangplank across up to **3 tiles** of open water to a pier, the shore or another ship. A gangway built on her **upper deck** runs its plank across open air instead, to a raised quay or pier floor on that level, so she can be boarded from a level up. While the gangplank is out she is held: she will not get under way, turn, make for a berth or set out on a voyage until it is taken in the same way. A broken plank brings the rest in.
- **Gun ports:** craft a **wooden gun port** (4 wood, railings, with Cannon) or a **steel gun port** (4 steel, railings, with Steelmaking). Build it in her side instead of a gunwale, usually on a deck with another deck above. A gun port is a gate the full height of the deck:
    - Shut, it is a wall. Shots do not pass it and nobody climbs over it.
    - Open, her guns fire through it, and anyone can come through it: crew, or boarders from a boat or the water.
    - An open port on a deck that is **awash** lets in water, about 4 bucketfuls a minute each, until it is shut.
- **Barring:** from aboard her, right-click a shut gangway or gun port and choose **Bar or Unbar**. A barred gate will not open from outside. Anyone there can only hammer on it, and her crew hear it, or break it down. Unbar it from aboard to open it.
- **Cargo hatch:** craft a **cargo hatch** (6 wood, railings, with Cooperage & Containers) and build it on her main deck over her hold. Click it to open or batten it down. While it is open, drag a crate onto it to lower the crate into the hold, or stand on it and click it to climb down. Shut, it is deck.
- **Lifeboat davits:** craft **lifeboat davits** (6 steel, railings, with Steelmaking) and build them on her edge facing out over the water.
    - **Stowing a boat:** with her stopped, bring an empty raft, canoe or boat afloat right under the davits, then drag it onto them. It is hoisted aboard in eight seconds and rides there.
    - **Lowering it:** click the davits to lower the boat into the water beside her, then climb down into it.
    - Both need her stopped and an empty boat. If the davits are destroyed, the boat drops onto her deck.
- **Bowsprit and jib:** craft a **bowsprit** (10 wood, vehicle parts, Wagons & Ships) and stand it on her foremost floor, with no deck of hers ahead of it. Rig a **small sail** on it as on a mast to set a **jib**: it is set, reefed and furled like any sail and pushes **half as hard** as a sail on a mast. A jib only draws while the bowsprit stands at her bow, so one with her deck ahead of it along her heading does nothing. A bowsprit is not climbed and takes no crow's nest.
- **Figurehead:** craft a **figurehead** (6 wood, vehicle parts) and fix it on her bow. Click it with an empty hand to carve it as a bear, wolf, goose, monkey, serpent, ram or a carved face. It is only for looks, and the first carving stays.
- Build what you like aboard: walls, a cabin, crates, cannon. Anything standing on her floors rides along at once. New floors that widen the hull join her when you **refit**.
- **Upper deck:** where the world has a level above the sea, as on Gaia and the Americas, a roof you build over her deck gives a floor on the level above, the same as a roof over a house. That **upper deck** sails and turns with her, along with everyone and everything on it. Hold the roofs up with walls or pillars on her deck, and climb up with a **ladder** built on her deck. The tiller counts the tiles of both decks. She stops and tells you why when a bridge, another floor or anything solid stands in the air ahead of her upper deck. Her deck and upper deck together may be at most **300 tiles**. Her upper deck crosses the sea with her on a [voyage](#voyages-to-new-lands).
- **Second deck above:** where the world has a level above that one too, a roof built on her upper deck gives her a **second deck above**, one level higher again. Hold its roofs up with walls or pillars on the upper deck and climb to it by a ladder built there. It sails, turns and crosses the sea with her like the upper deck, and she stops when anything stands in the air ahead of it. All her decks together still count toward the 300 tiles.
- **Gun turrets:** after **Naval Mines** research, craft a **ship's gun turret** (60 steel, weapons) on her deck. It fires the same shells as the artillery cannon. Its shells come up the **barbette** from a **shell rack** standing on the tile right under it in her hold (or in the hold two decks down from a deck above). Stand beside the turret and **Alt-click** it to hoist a shell into the breech, which takes five seconds. Turret and rack sail and turn together, so keep the rack straight under the turret. Ashore, a rack in a cellar dug under the turret feeds it the same way.
- **Belt armour:** after **Naval Mines** research, craft **belt armour plates** (15 steel each, weapons) and set them on the tiles along her side. A blast on a plated tile, or on her hold right under it, hits her floor much weaker, so a direct hit does not hole her. The direct hit buckles the plate away, so carry spares. Each plate weighs as much as a tile of hull, so a heavily belted ship needs more push. The tiller tells you how many of her side tiles are belted. Use a wrench to unbolt a plate for 10 steel.
- **Hold breaches:** a blast that tears through the floor of her **hold** leaves a **breach** open to the sea. It lets water in like a hole in her deck until it is shut. Stand beside it in the hold with a **welding tool** in one hand and use **2 steel** on it to weld it shut, which takes five seconds.
- **Vehicles:** a car, truck or tank driven onto her floors rides along and turns with her, and drives off the way it then faces. Park it **wholly aboard**: while a vehicle stands half on her floors and half off, she will not move or come about, and everyone aboard is told which vehicle is in the way. The same holds for slipway ships and the aircraft carrier.

### Ship's draughts

A **ship's draught** is a ship drawn on paper: every tile of her hull, the floor it has, what stands on it and where she has deck above, drawn with her bow to the top of the sheet.

- **Designing one:** craft a blank **ship's draught** (1 wood, paper & printing, with Papermaking). Hold it with a pen, pencil or pigment in your other hand, use it and choose **Draw on the sheet**. A drawing window opens with the sheet as a parchment grid, bow to the top, with port and starboard marked on its edges:
    - pick what to draw from the pictures on the right. Under **Hull**: **plank floor**, **steel floor**, **deck above** and **erase**. Under the other headings, the fittings: tiller or steering oar, ship mast, mast with a crow's nest, rowing bench, steam engine, killick, iron anchor, hand bilge pump, steam bilge pump, sump pump, wood crate, bed, wooden cannon, cannon, torpedo tube, plank wall, door, window, steel window, steel door, gangway, wooden gun port, steel gun port, lifeboat davits, cargo hatch, bowsprit and figurehead. A fitting needs a floor under it. Picking anything, or pointing at it in the palette, shows under the sheet what it is, what it is for and how to use it;
    - click or drag on the sheet to draw. The tile under the pointer shows what a click will lay, and the line under the sheet names what is already there. **Right-click** takes a fitting off a tile, or the deck above when that tool is picked;
    - **gunwales** are drawn for you: a boat or ship of more than 12 tiles gets one along every open edge, shown as a gold rim inside her outline and counted under **Sides** in her figures. A wall, door or window on an edge tile closes it instead. A **gangway** or **gun port** goes on an edge tile facing out over the side, and closes that side. **Lifeboat davits** also stand on an edge tile facing out. A **cargo hatch** goes on her main deck over a tile of her hold;
    - benches, doors, guns, gangways, gun ports, davits and figureheads take a **facing**: use the arrows, or press **R** to turn the piece. A gold arrow on the sheet shows which way each one faces;
    - **Box** fills a dragged box at once instead of drawing tile by tile, and **Mirror** copies what you draw across the dashed middle line;
    - **Fit ship** shows her as large as the window allows and **Whole sheet** shows all of it. The mouse wheel or **+** and **-** zoom, and dragging with the middle button moves the sheet;
    - **Undo** (Ctrl+Z) and **Redo** (Ctrl+Y) step back and forward, and **Clear** wipes the sheet;
    - **Start from** loads a ready-made rowing galley, sloop or steel cutter to change as you like;
    - **Decks:** the **Deck** buttons above the sheet switch between her **hold**, her **main deck**, her **deck above** and her **second deck above**. A hold tile goes under a tile of her hull, a deck-above tile over one, and a second-deck tile over a tile of the deck above. The other decks show faintly so you can line them up. Walls, doors, windows, crates, beds, pumps, **deck pillars** and **ladders** can stand on any deck; masts, helms, benches, anchors, guns and engines stand only on her main deck. A **deck pillar** (2 wood) holds up the deck above it for 3 tiles around, and a plank wall holds up only the tiles beside it. A **ladder** climbs from the deck it stands on to a clear tile of the deck above;
    - **Checks** updates as you draw. Each line is marked **Fix**, **Warning** or **Note**. A **Fix** stops her being built: she needs exactly one tiller or steering oar, every tile must touch another, a gangway, gun port or pair of davits must face out over the side, a cargo hatch must stand over her hold, and she must stay within the most tiles a hull may have. A **Warning** still lets her be built but tells you what will not work well afloat: no masts, benches or engine, no bilge pump on a boat or ship, no anchor or anchors too small to hold every tile, too little ballast for her masts, a bowsprit with her deck ahead of it, a gun pointing across her own deck or with no open deck behind it, or a fitting walled in where the crew cannot reach it. On her other decks, a ladder that leads nowhere, a hold with no ladder up out of it and deck above with nothing holding it up are each a **Fix**. A **Note** is advice. Point at a line to light up the tiles it is about, or click it to keep them lit;
    - **Her figures** shows her size against the most tiles a hull may have, her decks, her sides, what she costs, and her speed under sail, at the oars and under steam;
    - **Save to the sheet** keeps the drawing, finished or not. **Unsaved changes** shows beside her name until you do.
- **Copying a ship afloat:** hold a blank sheet of paper and click her tiller, then choose **Draw her lines**. Only her crew can draw her, and only while she is commissioned.
- **Reading it:** examine the sheet, or use it in your hand. It tells you how many tiles she has and whether she is a small craft, a boat or a ship. It also lists the materials her floors and fittings cost to build by hand, and the speed she should make empty under sail on a reach and with every rowing bench manned. If her masts need more weight low in her, it tells you about how much ballast to carry.
- **Sharing it:** see [Sharing plans as design codes](#sharing-plans-as-design-codes) below.
- **Building her on a slipway:** build a [slipway](#slipway-ships) on the shore facing the water and use the drawn sheet on its post. She is marked out on the ground in front of it, stern at the post and bow toward the water, with a faint outline on every tile still to build. There must be open water ahead for her to launch into, and nothing solid or already floored where she will lie. Bring the materials to the post or to any marked tile: her floors go down first from stern to bow, then her fittings one kind at a time, each costing what it costs to build by hand (walls want planks as well as wood, anchors and pumps want iron). A boat or ship of more than 12 tiles then gets a gunwale on every open side of her deck, or a steel bulwark on steel floor, at 2 wood or 2 steel each; they are counted in what her draught says she costs, so she comes down the ways ready to commission. Each floor or fitting appears as soon as it is paid for, and you keep working while the stack in your hand still pays for the next piece. Several people can build her at once, and she goes up faster for every pair of hands. Her other decks come last: her **hold** is dug out and floored under the berth, her **deck above** is roofed over her and floored, then her **second deck above** over that, then what stands on those decks and her ladders. A hold needs rock or deep water under the berth, and each deck above needs open air over it, or the slipway tells you what is in the way.
- **Launching her:** when the last piece is fitted, use the slipway post and choose **Launch**. She slides down the ways until all of her floats, with every deck and everyone and everything aboard, and comes off commissioned under the name on her draught, ready to sail from her tiller. The ground she slides over must be clear, a dry dock must be flooded first, and her tiller or steering oar must still stand where the draught shows it. Giving her up before she is finished takes the draught off the post and leaves what was built where it stands.

- **Wiring:** cables, engines, generators and batteries aboard stay connected when she moves, so lamps and machines keep running under way. A cable that runs from her to the shore parts as soon as she moves. The same holds for slipway ships and the aircraft carrier.
- Once commissioned she can sail onto deep salt water. If you strike her out there, the floors are plain planks again and will not last on the deep sea.
- If the tiller is destroyed she is a raft of planks again. Set a new tiller and commission her.

#### Sharing plans as design codes

Every plan drawn on a ship's draught can be exported as a design code and imported again: ships (with their extra decks), submarines, vehicles, tanks, planes, airships, trains, railways, space stations and houses. Codes are plain text, so you can keep them outside the game or give them to other players.

- **Export.** Use the sheet and choose **Export its design code**, or press **Export code** beside **Save to the sheet** in the drawing window. Export from the window saves the drawing first. Copy the code from the window that opens.
- **Import.** Use any ship's draught and choose **Import a design code**, or press **Import code** in a drawing window, then paste the code. A blank sheet accepts any kind of plan. The imported plan replaces whatever was drawn, and the drawing window for that kind of plan opens.
- An imported plan follows the same rules as one you draw yourself. Codes that are too big or broken are refused, and space stations can only be imported once your age can draw them. After an import, the plan's checks are listed in chat, so you can see straight away whether it can be built as drawn. Building it still takes the usual materials, research and work.

### Bilge water

A hull you built takes in water through her seams once she is big enough, and somebody has to bail it out. How much work that is depends on her size and what she is made of.

| Hull | Tiles of floor | Water through the seams |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small craft | up to 12 | None |
| Boat | 13 to 40 | Seeps if she is built of wood |
| Ship | 41 and up | Seeps if she is built of wood |

- **Steel, iron and aluminium floors** let nothing through. Only tiles floored in wood or other materials seep.
- **When it comes in:** under way, and half as fast while she lies still with someone aboard. A boat at anchor or at a berth with nobody on her deck takes no water at all, so she is as you left her when you come back.
- **How fast:** left alone under way, a wooden boat is awash in about 45 minutes. Half an hour of sailing lets in about two minutes' worth of bailing for one person on a 30-tile boat; a bigger hull seeps more and wants more hands.
- **Reading it:** the **tiller** or **steering oar** tells you whether she is a small craft, a boat or a ship and how deep the bilge is: dry, damp, ankle deep, knee deep or awash. Everyone aboard is told when it reaches ankle deep, knee deep and awash.
- **Awash:** when she is awash or worse you can see it: water washes over every plank of her deck until she is bailed below awash again.
- **What it does:** once the bilge is more than damp the water is extra weight for her sails, oars and engines to push, as if her hull were bigger. A boat that made half ahead dry may only make slow ahead knee deep, and she is harder to push off a beach. Water from her seams stops rising when she is awash, half full: neglect makes her wallow but does not sink her. Holes do.
- **Bailing:** stand on her deck with an **empty bucket** in your hand and click one of her floors. Each bucketful takes three seconds and goes over the side. You keep bailing until the bilge is dry, you walk away or you put the bucket down, and you can bail while she sails. Several people can bail at once. A bucket with anything in it has to be emptied first.

### Bilge pumps

A bucket only goes so far. The pumps that keep a cellar dry also pump a hull's bilge when they are built on her deck. See [floods](Guide_to_Floods.md) for what each one costs and the research it needs.

| Pump | What it does aboard |
| --- | --- |
| **Lever pump** | Click it to work the handle. Each three-second stroke lifts two bucketfuls, twice what a bucket does, and you keep pumping until the bilge is dry or you stop. One person at the pump keeps ahead of one hole. |
| **Steam mine pump** | Feed it coal, charcoal or wood and start it. It lifts 80 bucketfuls a minute while the fire burns, enough to stay ahead of three holes. |
| **Sump pump** | Lifts 40 bucketfuls a minute on cable power or its battery. It starts by itself when there is water in the bilge and stops when she is dry. |

- You can pump while sailing. On a ship divided into watertight compartments, a pump or bucket in a dry bay will not drain a different, flooded bay. See [Ship Damage Control](Guide_to_Ship_Damage_Control.md#the-pump-is-running-but-water-isnt-going-down).
- The steam and sump pumps only run, and only burn fuel or battery, while someone is aboard or she is under way. A boat left at anchor is as you left her.
- The tiller counts the pumps on her deck and how many are working.

### Load and ballast

Everything on the deck of a boat or ship weighs on her: crew, crates and what is in them, guns, engines, masts, cabins built aboard and loose goods. Small craft of 12 tiles or fewer are not weighed.

- **Burden:** she carries half a tile's worth of weight for every tile of hull without noticing. A 30-tile boat has a burden of 15. As a guide, a person is a fifth, a crate a half or more, a gun one and a half, and a full stack of fifty of anything is one.
- **Deep laden:** past her burden every extra tile of weight is one more tile of hull for her sails, oars and engines to push, so she may drop from half ahead to slow ahead.
- **Overladen:** past twice her burden she rides so low that the sea slops over her side while she sails. The bilge fills until she is awash, though this alone will not sink her. Lighten her or keep bailing.
- **Ballast:** a ship of 41 tiles or more needs weight low in her to stand up to her sails: a twentieth of her tiles for every rigged mast. A 60-tile ship with two masts wants 6. Without it she is **crank**: under sail in a strong wind she heels and ships water over the rail. Any cargo counts, and a few stacks of stone are the cheap way.
- The **tiller** shows her weight against her burden and tells you if she is deep laden, overladen or crank. Everyone aboard is told when she goes a mark deeper.

### Anchors and mooring

A boat or ship of 13 tiles or more needs something to hold her when you choose **Drop anchor** at the tiller. Small craft need nothing.

| What holds her | How |
| --- | --- |
| **Killick** | 8 planks, vehicle parts, **Oared Ships**. A wooden anchor weighted with a stone. Build it on her deck. Holds 40 tiles of hull. |
| **Anchor** | 8 iron, vehicle parts, **Wagons & Ships**. Build it on her deck. Holds 100 tiles of hull. |
| **Mooring post or bollard** | Lie with any part of her within two tiles of one and drop anchor: her lines go to the post. |

- Anchors add up, and together they have to cover every tile of her. A 30-tile boat lies to one killick; a 150-tile ship wants two iron anchors.
- **Drifting:** with her anchor up and no way on, or with her anchor down and nothing to hold her, she drifts while anyone is aboard: a tile every half minute with the current, or before the wind when it blows at all hard. The shore and other hulls stop her. Everyone aboard is told when she starts to drift.
- A boat with **nobody aboard stays where she was left**, held or not.
- The tiller and the anchor itself tell you whether she is held. An anchor weighs on her like any other load.
- A hull that was already afloat before anchors had to be built keeps the anchor she always had.

### Handling sail

Every mast on a hull you built carries its sail one of three ways. A new sail is **set full**, and a hull you leave alone sails as she always did. Click a rigged mast to change it; it takes four seconds and you can do it under way.

| Sail | Push | In a strong wind | In a gale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Set full** | All of it | May tear | May tear |
| **Reefed** | Six tenths | Safe | May tear, though rarely |
| **Furled** | None | Safe | Safe |

- **Tearing:** a sail only tears while she is under way. In a strong wind a full sail has about one chance in seven of tearing every half minute, so reef when the weather turns and furl in a gale. Everyone aboard is warned when the wind is strong enough to tear what she is carrying, and again when a sail goes.
- **A torn sail** pushes less than a third of what it did. Use **two pieces of cloth** on its mast to mend it, which takes six seconds.
- A furled sail does not count as a rigged mast for [ballast](#load-and-ballast), so furling also stops a crank ship heeling.
- The tiller lists how her sails are carried and how many are torn. Sails on small craft of 12 tiles or fewer never tear.

### The watch

A hull is worked from places on her deck, and the helm can ask who is where. Choose **Muster the watch** at the tiller to list everyone aboard and their station: at the helm, at an oar, at a mast, at a pump, at the anchor, in the bow or simply on deck. Anyone can ask; it does not need the ship's papers.

- **Lookout:** on a hull of 13 tiles or more, someone standing in her bow, her two foremost rows of deck, keeps the lookout while she is under way. They see what lies on her course up to twelve tiles ahead and call it to everyone aboard when it is first sighted and again when it is four tiles off or closer.
- Whoever is at the helm or sat at an oar cannot keep the lookout, and neither can anyone lying down.
- Without a lookout the crew only learns of what is ahead as she comes up on it, so post one before sailing at speed or near a coast. The muster says when nobody is keeping it.

### A foul bottom

Weed and shell grow on the bottom of a boat or ship of 13 tiles or more while she is in use, at anchor or under way. A boat with nobody aboard does not foul, and small craft never do.

| Bottom | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Clean** | Nothing |
| **Slimed** | Starts to drag |
| **Weedy** | Drags like a fifth of her hull more to push |
| **Foul** | Drags like four tenths of her hull more to push |

- A bare bottom goes from clean to foul over about a day of use. The tiller says how her bottom is, and everyone aboard is told when she turns weedy and when she turns foul.
- **Scraping:** lay her over shallow water, bring her to a stop, stand on her deck and use a **shovel or a knife** on one of her planks. Each go takes four seconds and cleans three tiles of hull, and you keep going until she is clean or you step away. Her bottom is out of reach on deep water, unless she lies in a [boathouse slip](#working-on-a-boat-in-the-boathouse-slip).
- **Copper sheathing:** on a wooden hull with a clean bottom, lying still in the shallows or in a boathouse slip, use **copper** on a plank with a **hammer** in your other hand. Each sheet covers two tiles and takes three seconds. Fully sheathed, she fouls a tenth as fast. A plated hull cannot be coppered.

### Caulking

A wooden boat or ship keeps the sea out with **tarred oakum** driven into the seams between her planks. Sailing slowly works it loose, and the looser her seams, the faster the bilge fills.

- **Wear:** only time under way wears her seams. A new hull has sound seams. After about an hour of sailing they start working loose, and after a little over two hours they are open and let in twice the water. Everyone aboard is told both times, and the tiller says how her seams are. A hull at anchor does not wear.
- **Tarred oakum:** use a coil of **rope** on a bucket, pot or other open container holding at least 10 units of **pitch** from a tar kiln. Each coil makes three twists.
- **Caulking:** bring her to a stop and bail her if she is awash. Stand on her deck with the oakum in your active hand and a **chisel or a hammer** in the other, and click one of her floors. Each twist takes four seconds and tightens three tiles of hull, and you keep going until the job is done or the oakum runs out. A 30-tile boat with open seams wants 7 twists to make her sound again, or 10 in the shallows to make her tight.
- **On deep water** you can only reach part of her seams, so caulking brings her back to sound and no better.
- **In the shallows** or in a [boathouse slip](#working-on-a-boat-in-the-boathouse-slip) she can be caulked right down to tight, where she seeps half as fast as a new hull. Lay her over shallow water before a long voyage.
- Small craft and hulls floored in steel, iron or aluminium have no seams to caulk.

### Holes and sinking

If water is already coming in, follow [Ship Damage Control](Guide_to_Ship_Damage_Control.md) for the order to close doors, patch and pump.

Any hull you built can be holed, whatever her size and whether she is wood or steel. A tile of her hull whose floor is gone is a hole: shot away, blown out by a mine or a blast, cut out by her own crew, or broken on a reef or in a storm once that plank was already battered. You can see the sea through it, and stepping on it puts you in the water. Walls and cabins knocked down aboard are not holes.

- **The sea comes in fast.** While anyone is aboard or she is under way, each open hole lets in more than one person with a bucket can bail. Everyone aboard is told when she is holed and where, and the tiller counts her holes.
- **Listing:** three quarters full she lists and makes slow ahead at best.
- **Patching a main-deck hole:** stand on her deck next to the hole with **wood** in your active hand and a **hammer** in the other, and click the open water of the hole. Six seconds and two pieces of wood nail a **hull patch** over it. A patch is a floor again and closes the hole, but it weeps a little, so a patched boat needs somewhat more bailing. If a patch is destroyed the hole is open again.
- **Welding a hold breach:** stand next to the breach below deck, hold a welding tool in your other hand, and use **2 steel** on the breach. It takes about five seconds. Pump out the trapped water afterward.
- **Refitting a holed hull:** a hole inside her outline must be patched before you refit the hull. A floor taken up from her **edge** is different: drop anchor and choose **Refit the hull**, and that tile is cut away from her shape. Until then it is still part of her, so it can stop her against a bank, a trench or a pier, and she tells you so. This is how to make her a tile smaller to slip past an obstacle.
- **In the shallows** a hull that fills settles on the bottom. She will not sail or turn, but nothing is lost. Patch every hole and bail her to below awash, and she floats again.
- **On deep water** a hull that fills founders. Her crew is warned every half minute, and after two minutes she founders unless they have got ahead of the water by then. A ship can also founder if nearly full compartments cover more than half the hull, even if the overall bilge is not full. Patch the leaks and keep pumping the flooded bays until the warning stops; taking out a little water may not be enough.
- **When she founders** she is not destroyed. She stops, cannot make way or turn, and keeps her floors, tiller, masts, engines, guns, crew and cargo. Where the sea below is deep and clear enough she settles beneath the surface as a whole; otherwise she stays waterlogged where she is. Patch every hole and pump her below 40% of her bilge capacity, and she rises and can sail again.
- **Waterlogged engines:** an engine that floods stops and will not start again until it is dry and you service it with a **wrench**. A powered crane on the surface can lift an unfastened waterlogged engine up through open sea within its reach.
- **Getting out below:** from inside a sunken hull, use an open cargo hatch from directly below if the deck above it is clear.
- **Trapped air:** a sealed steel compartment, with intact watertight walls, closed watertight doors and a solid deck overhead, keeps a limited supply of air shared by everyone inside. It does not refill. Opening it to the sea or letting it flood loses the air. Ordinary roofs, hatches and ladder openings do not seal.
- **Heavy seas:** a storm can roll an unstable ship. Anyone standing loose on her open main deck can slide across it and be thrown over the lee rail into the water. Something solid in the way stops the slide, and people below deck, in a closed cabin or buckled into a seat stay aboard.

### Ship's boats

A ship with a deck, whether a [hull you built yourself](#building-your-own-hull), a [slipway ship](#slipway-ships) or an aircraft carrier, can carry a [modular boat](#modular-ships) on her deck and put it back in the water wherever she stops. Craft a **boat davit** (10 wood, vehicle parts, the same research as boats) and build it on her deck. The davit's **cradle** is the patch of deck ahead of it, 3 by 3 tiles to begin with. Click the davit to:

| Choice | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Show the cradle** | Marks the cradle's tiles for a few seconds |
| **Turn the cradle** | Swings the cradle round to another side of the davit |
| **Set the cradle's size** | From 2 to 5 tiles each way. The whole cradle must lie on her deck |
| **Hoist a boat aboard** | Lifts a boat lying within 8 tiles into the cradle |
| **Lower it to the north / east / south / west** | Sets the boat down in the first clear water over that side |

A lift takes about ten seconds at the capstan, and the ship must be stopped. The boat comes aboard whole, with everyone and everything in it, and lies the way it lay in the water, so bring it alongside pointing the way the cradle is long. The cradle must be clear of anything solid, and it holds one boat at a time.

While the boat is in the cradle it rides with the ship through every move and turn, its anchor is down and it will not sail. The ship's [sea chart](#harbours-and-the-sea-chart) lists it under **In her davits** instead of among the boats in sight. If the ship is registered, only her crew can work the davit.

## Voyages to new lands

Past the edge of the map lies open sea, and somewhere out there are lands nobody has seen yet. A ship [you built yourself](#building-your-own-hull) can sail off the edge of the mainland to find them.

### Setting out

1. Bring her within 4 tiles of the edge of the map, ring **All stop**, and make sure she can make way: a rigged mast or a running engine.
2. Have at least one person aboard and awake; a lone sailor can make the voyage. Stay aboard through landfall if you intend to cross. If you log out while still aboard, your character can still cross with the ship.
3. Click the tiller and choose **Set out on a voyage**. You see your chart: your faction's chart if you belong to one, otherwise your own.

From there you can:

- **Sail for a land** your chart shows. Each land lists its size (tiny or small), its climate, its kind of country (coastal plain, river delta, highlands, fjord coast...) and how long the voyage takes: about **a minute for each square of the chart** under sail. A ship driven at full ahead by her engines takes about 40 seconds a square.
- **Scout unknown water** to the north, east, south or west. She sails 4 squares out and back (8 minutes); what her crew saw goes on your chart.

Any crew may open a new land; nobody has to approve it. A few rules keep the sea from filling up:

- Your crew opens **one new land at a time**. While a land you opened is still undecided, [settle, claim or abandon it](#settle-claim-or-abandon) before you open another.
- After opening a land, your crew can open the next one **3 hours** later. Lands that are already open can be sailed for at any time.
- After she makes landfall, a ship stays at least **15 minutes** before she can put to sea again.
- Only a few lands are awake at once. A land that stands empty for an hour may be **laid up** when its harbour is needed; nothing on it is lost. The chart marks it as laid up, and it is made ready again while a ship sails for it. If your character lives on a laid-up land, press Join and it is made ready within a few minutes.
- If every harbour is busy, the tiller tells you so and she stays under your hand. Try again a little later, or sail for a land that is already open.

### Rations and the sea

Every crew member eats **one ration for each square** of the voyage, and any food aboard counts: in the hold, in crates or in your pack. If she carries too little, the tiller warns you before she sails. The food is eaten when she arrives; if she was short, everyone lands hungry. Nobody starves at sea.

On the way the sea may take a hand, at most once of each kind a voyage:

- **A storm** costs her a minute or two, or washes something loose on deck overboard. Stow what you want to keep.
- **A sighting**: the lookout spots water off her course, and it goes on your chart.
- **Short rations**: past halfway on too little food, the crew are warned, so you can still turn back.

While she is at sea she lies at anchor off the coast and nobody steers. The tiller tells you how long is left. **Turn back** cancels the voyage when the option is available. If the ship is already committed to landfall, wait aboard: "not yet in sight" means the destination is still getting ready, not that you need to choose again. If the coast never becomes ready, the ship turns back after up to 30 minutes of waiting. When she arrives, she and her crew appear anchored off the new land's coast.

Every land has its own riches. Smaller lands are richer in ore and more often hide something rare, such as gems, a rich vein of gold or silver, or diamonds; larger lands grow food better. Each land is known for a few kinds of ore, which turn up as rich lodes when you mine there, and for a few kinds of game, which are plentiful there.

### Sailing between the continents

The continents lie across the sea from one another, and a ship can carry you between them. Every land has a coast: Gaia, North and South America, Europe, Africa, Asia, Oceania, Greenland and Antarctica (see [Regions of the World](../regions/Regions.md)). Bring a ship you [built yourself](#building-your-own-hull) within 4 tiles of the map edge, click the tiller and choose **Set out on a voyage**. The eligible coasts the tiller can reach are at the top of the list, with their direction and estimated travel time. Gaia is about 6 minutes from North America under sail, Europe 5 from Gaia and Asia 7 from Europe; engines are faster.

The usual voyage rules apply: she needs a rigged mast or a running engine, and food for everyone aboard. One sailor is enough. Everything aboard sails with her: what the crew wear and carry, loose gear on her deck and whatever is in her chests. Animals, the dead and things standing on her deck (barrels with what is in them, tables, troughs, doors, machines) sail too, and come off as they went aboard. So do carts, rafts, dugouts, outriggers, motorboats and bicycles, once they are empty; anyone riding one gets off as she sails. A canoe or motorboat takes her outboard and its fuel tank with her, and it arrives stopped. A **car, truck or light plane** parked wholly on her main deck crosses too, with its plate, key, fuel and what is in its boot, and is parked where it stood when she arrives. Stop it, switch its engine off, get out and clear anything loose off it first. Her **engines** sail too, so a motor freighter can make the voyage: each arrives stopped and still coupled to the fuel barrel beside it, with the fuel that was in it. Start it again from the engine. A ship with a **cargo hold** takes it with her, along with everything in it and the ladder down to it. On the far side the hold is hollowed out again under her deck where the sea floor allows; where it doesn't, its cargo is put on the deck above it and the hold itself is lost. Her **upper deck** goes too, with its floors, the ladder up and whatever stands on it, and is built again on her roofs where there is open air above her. Where something is in the way up there, the roof under it stays a plain roof and what stood on it is put on her deck. A floor built on a roof of the upper deck, a storey higher still, has to come down first. A warship goes whole: her **gun turrets** keep the shells in their breeches, her **shell racks** the shells in them, and her **belt armour** stays bolted along her sides, so a light cruiser arrives ready to fight. Only an animal someone is playing or riding, a loaded cart, anything strapped into a seat that is not crew, a tank or other vehicle with a turret or heavy axis, and a body with a creature inside it have to be put ashore first; the tiller names them and points them out on deck. If nobody can take her across, everything stays aboard where it was. She arrives anchored off the coast that faces where you came from. Your character lives in the land you sail to from then on; sail back to return. She also arrives in the state she left in: the water in her bilge, her seams, the growth on her bottom and how each sail is set all cross with her.

### Settle, claim or abandon

A new land is undecided until its crew choose:

| Choice | How | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Settle** | Click the tiller and choose **Settle this land**. It needs a mooring post on the coast and beds slept in by **3 different people** within 40 tiles of it. | The land stays for good and appears on everyone's chart, so any ship can reach it. Beds there keep their sleepers' homes. |
| **Claim** | A faction leader who sailed here plants their **colony charter**. | The land becomes your faction's colony. Each faction can claim **one** land, and each land has one claimant. |
| **Claim it for yourself** | Click the tiller and choose **Claim this land**. It needs a mooring post on the coast and **your own bed** within 40 tiles of it. | The land is yours and stays for good, on everyone's chart. Each person can claim **one** land, besides the one their faction claims. **Give up your claim** at a tiller there frees you to claim another; the land stays, settled. |
| **Give up the claim** | The colony's leader clicks its charter and gives the claim up. | The charter comes down and the land stays, settled and open to any faction's claim. Your faction may then claim another land. |
| **Abandon** | When you sail away from an undecided land, the captain is asked whether to abandon it. | The land is gone for good: nobody can sail there again, and anything left there is lost. |

A land that nobody settles or claims, and that nobody visits for **7 days**, is abandoned.

**A claimed land stays your faction's** while your faction lives there. If raiders destroy the charter, the colony still stands: members who arrive still join it, nobody else can claim the land, and the colony's leader can plant the charter again. The claim lapses only if fewer than **3** of the colony's members have their own fastened bed there for **14 days** in a row. Members are warned when that starts, and examining the charter shows how long is left. When a claim lapses the charter comes down and the land stays settled, open to any faction's claim.

### Your faction's island

Every faction can claim **one island of its own** across the sea, as a safe haven that only the people it chooses can reach. To claim one, your leader sails there (see above) and plants the colony charter. **Faction > Faction Island** shows whether your faction has an island and who may land there; without one, it tells you how to claim it.

Your leader, and officers who issue papers, choose the island's border from any land with **Faction > Faction Island**:

| Border | Who may land |
| --- | --- |
| **Open to visitors** | Anyone. |
| **Members and guests** (the start) | Members, the guests your faction names, and anyone holding one of its visitor passes. A guest is named while standing beside the officer. |
| **Members only** | Only members. The border is closed, whatever passes say. |

**Visitor passes:** your leader or an officer who issues papers right-clicks one of your faction's recruitment stations and chooses **Grant Island Visitor Pass**. It goes to someone standing beside the station, for 1 hour to 7 days, and can be taken back the same way. A pass only works while the border admits guests.

Border and pass changes reach every land within a minute or two. Each officer's change is applied on its own, so two officers working at once do not undo each other. The station tells you once the pass is recorded, or if it was refused.

Nobody else can sail for the island. Your chart only lists it for people it admits, and a ship will not set out, or make landfall, with anyone aboard who may not land. Every other way there refuses them too. Anyone who is on the island without leave, because the border closed, a pass ran out or they left the faction, is warned and has **10 minutes** to go. After that they are put ashore on the mainland. Captives held in restraints stay where they are.

Members who properly go to **Sleep** in their own bed on the island, and then log off, cannot be harmed while they are away, just as in a province their faction holds. If your faction gives the claim up, or it lapses, the border goes with it and any ship can reach the land again.

Until a land is settled or claimed, **dying there sends you back to the mainland**, even if you have a bed there. Your body and gear stay where you fell, and your crew and ship carry on without you.

### Sailing on

From any land you can sail home with **Leave these waters**, or set out on another voyage from the land's own coast to find the next one.

## Boathouse

A **boathouse** keeps your boats out of the water until you need them, the way a garage keeps cars. Craft it from 20 planks (vehicle parts, Wagons & Ships) and build it on the shore.

Its **slip** is the 7 by 7 square of water diagonally next to it, to the north-east when it is built. Examining the boathouse tells you which way its slip lies. A **wrench** turns the slip to the next corner, but only while nothing is stored.

To put a boat away, sail it so that **all of it** lies inside the slip, stop it and get everyone off. Then click the boathouse and choose **Put away a boat from the slip**. It takes a few seconds. The boat leaves the water with everything lying on its deck. A boathouse holds **three boats**. It stores [modular boats](#modular-ships), [submarines](#submarines), rafts, outriggers and rigid hull inflatable boats. [Slipway ships](#slipway-ships) and [hulls you build yourself](#building-your-own-hull) are too big for it.

| A boat is refused while | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| It is moving | Stop it |
| Anyone is aboard | Get everyone off |
| Its engine runs | Stop the engine |
| It is on a tow line | Cast off the line |
| It is a submarine that is submerged or has its floodlights on | Surface and switch the floodlights off |
| It is locked with a key you do not carry | Bring its key |

**Take a boat out** puts it back in the slip on the same tiles where it was put away, with its cargo, fuel, name and paint. Those tiles must be clear of people, other boats and anything solid. A keyed boat needs its key to come out.

A raft or dugout kept in the boathouse dries out three times as fast as one left on the shore.

### Working on a boat in the boathouse slip

A [hull you built yourself](#building-your-own-hull) that fits wholly inside the slip, up to 7 by 7 tiles, can't be stored, but she can be worked on there. Bring her to a stop in the slip and she counts as lying in the shallows, however deep the water is:

- Her seams can be [caulked](#caulking) right down to tight.
- Her bottom can be [scraped and coppered](#a-foul-bottom).
- Her **patches can be replaced for good**. Stand on her deck next to a patch with one piece of **wood** (or one **steel** sheet) in your active hand and a **hammer** in the other, and click the patch. After four seconds it becomes sound planking, or steel plating, and stops weeping. This only works at a berth like the slip, a [slipway](#hauling-a-ship-out-for-repairs) or a [drydock](#drydock).

Click the boathouse and choose **Survey her condition** to see each boat in the slip and each stored raft or canoe. It lists what each one needs and what the work costs.

## Harbours and the sea chart

Slipway ships, the aircraft carrier and hulls you built yourself share one sea chart and can lie moored at a harbour.

**The sea chart.** Choose **Chart** at a ship's wheel or **Sea chart** at a tiller. The sea is divided into squares of 10 tiles, lettered across and numbered up, so "E3" is the fifth square from the west and the third from the south. The chart shows:

- the square and position your ship lies in, and the harbour she is moored at;
- every other ship within **40 tiles** of her: distance, bearing, square, where her bow points and whether she is under way, stopped or moored;
- **boats and aircraft in sight**: modular boats on the water and aircraft flying over within the same lookout range, with distance, bearing and heading. One registered in its [vehicle papers](Guide_to_Tanks.md#vehicle-papers) goes by its name, marked "(ours)" when it is your faction's;
- every **harbour** on that sea, however far, and whether its berth would take your ship.

**Weather and night.** The lookout sees 40 tiles in clear weather. **Rain or snow** cuts that to 30, **smog** to 20 and a **storm** to 15, and the chart tells you so. **Mist** takes a further 15 tiles off and **fog** 25, on top of any rain, so a ship in fog sees 15 tiles at best; the lookout never drops below 5. What counts is the worse weather of the two ships, so a ship lying in a storm is as hard to see as it is for her to see out. You can only hail a ship your lookout sees. By **night** a ship [running dark](#hails-and-running-dark) is made out only within **10 tiles**, while she still sees every ship that shows lights. The carrier's radar sees through weather and darkness.

**Building a harbour.** Craft a **mooring post** (6 wood, vehicle parts, the same research as boats) and set it at the water's edge, on the shore or at the end of a pier, with open water on one side. Click the post to:

| Choice | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Claim the berth** | The post belongs to your faction. An unclaimed post takes any ship |
| **Name the harbour** | The name ships read on their charts |
| **Welcome only our ships / our guests / every ship** | Who may berth. A ship counts as yours when she is registered to your faction in her [ship's papers](Guide_to_Aircraft_Carriers.md#ships-papers) |
| **Add a guest / Strike a guest** | A guest faction is picked from the registered ships lying within 20 tiles of the post |
| **Give up the berth** | Anyone may claim it again |
| **Freight** | The harbour's freight board (see [Freight contracts](#freight-contracts)) |

One post takes one ship, up to 40 tiles long and 12 across. Set several posts well apart for a bigger harbour.

**Berthing.** Bring the ship within **20 tiles** of the post and choose **Make for a berth** at the wheel or tiller. If the post will have her, the water she is about to lie in is **marked in yellow for ten seconds**, then she slides in along the shore with her middle abreast of the post, stops and drops anchor. If not, you are told why: too far, not welcome, promised to another ship, taken, or something in the way.

A moored ship holds the berth until she next moves: weigh anchor and sail and she has cast off. After a server restart she lies at anchor beside the post; make for the berth again to moor her.

### Freight contracts

Every mooring post is also a **freight board**: one faction pays another to carry a crate to a different harbour. Click the post and choose **Freight**.

On land, a [freight depot](Guide_to_Tanks.md#freight-depots) is the same kind of board for registered vehicles, and a crate can be bound from a harbour to a depot or back.

**Offering a contract.**

1. Fill a **crate**, close it and stand it within **2 tiles** of the post.
2. Hold the **reward** in your active hand: coins, or any one item or stack. An empty hand means no reward.
3. Choose **Offer a contract** and pick the harbour it is bound for.

The post takes in the crate and the reward. At a claimed post only those it welcomes may offer contracts: its owners, their guests, or everyone, the same rule as for berthing. A board holds **6** contracts.

**Carrying one.**

1. Bring a ship that is registered in her [ship's papers](Guide_to_Aircraft_Carriers.md#ships-papers), with you among her owners or crew, within **20 tiles** of the post.
2. Choose **Take a contract**. The sealed crate is set down beside you, named for its harbour.
3. Carry it to that harbour, stand it within 2 tiles of the mooring post there, and choose **Hand in freight**. The harbour pays you the reward on the spot.

- **Do not open the crate.** Opening it breaks the seal (examine the crate to check), and a crate handed in with a broken seal earns nothing.
- Whoever hands in the sealed crate is paid, so guard your freight.
- The post and every ship's **sea chart** show how many contracts a harbour has on offer.

**If it goes wrong.** Whoever offered the contract can **Withdraw a contract** nobody has taken, and gets the crate and reward back. If the crate arrives with a broken seal, or it was destroyed and they **Write off lost freight**, the reward goes back to the board where it was offered, and they take it out with **Collect a returned reward**.

### Hails and running dark

**Hail a ship.** At a wheel or tiller choose **Hail a ship**, pick one of the ships your lookout sees and type what you call across. Everyone aboard her reads it with the distance and bearing it came from, and your own crew reads what was said. Anyone at the helm may hail, and hails must be five seconds apart.

**Run dark.** Choose **Run dark** to keep your ship's name to herself:

- other ships' sea charts and the carrier's radar still show where she is, but list her as an **unknown contact** with no name, heading or speed;
- her hails come from "an unknown ship";
- ships registered to your own faction still know her;
- she makes **slow ahead at most**, and a slipway ship or carrier puts out her deck lights.

Lamps you set on a hull you built are yours to put out. Choose **Show her lights** to be known and fast again. Running dark is an order like setting a speed, so on a registered ship it answers only to her crew. It ends at a server restart.

### Grappling and boarding

Bring your ship alongside another so that **3 tiles of open water or less** lie in a straight line between the two hulls, then choose **Grapple a ship** at the wheel or tiller and pick her.

- **Both ships are held fast.** Neither moves, comes about or makes for a berth while the lines hold, whatever her telegraph says. When they are let go, a ship with her anchor up and a speed rung sails on.
- **Boarding lines** lie across the gap. Walk over them to board her, or to be boarded. Whoever stands on a line when it parts goes into the water.
- The ship that threw the lines chooses **Cast off the grapple** at her helm and is free at once.
- The ship that was caught chooses **Cut the grapple** at her helm, or anyone clicks one of the boarding lines with an empty hand. Cutting takes five seconds, and **3 seconds more for every grapnel** holding the lines.
- A line that is destroyed parts the ships, and so does striking or sinking either of them.
- After her lines are let go a ship needs **one minute** to coil them before she can grapple again.
- **Grapnels:** craft a **grapnel** (3 iron, vehicle parts, Iron Smithing). Standing on your ship's deck, click another ship within **5 tiles** with it in hand to throw it. It catches about three times in five; a miss is hauled back to try again. A grapnel that catches throws your lines on her as the helm does, even while the helm's lines are still being coiled, or adds to the lines already holding the two ships. Either crew can throw more grapnels onto the lines to make them slower to cut. The hooks come back to the deck of the ship that threw the lines when they part.

A ship takes one set of lines at a time. Grappling is an order like setting a speed, so on a registered ship it answers only to her crew, but anyone can cut a line they can reach. The lines do not last through a server restart.

## Mines, torpedoes and degaussing

Naval mines and torpedoes sink ships from below. Steel hulls are easiest to hit, because they carry a **magnetic signature** that magnetic weapons can feel.

### Magnetic signature

The wheel, the tiller and a submarine's systems report show her **magnetic signature**: none, faint, strong or very strong. Wooden hulls and rafts show none. Steel plating, steel frames and engines add to it, and a big steel freighter or carrier is very strong.

### Laying and clearing mines

- Hold a mine and click a tile of **open water** next to you, from a deck or the shore. It takes five seconds, and the mine settles just under the surface as a dark shape.
- A **contact mine** goes off when any hull, wooden or steel, runs onto its tile.
- A **magnetic mine** goes off when a steel hull comes close enough for her signature. A small steel boat sets one off only by passing over it or right beside it. A large steel ship sets one off from up to 4 tiles away. A wooden hull sails over it safely.
- The blast goes off under the nearest part of the hull. It breaks frames, and on a submarine it springs leaks.
- Mines stay where they were laid. Shooting a mine, hitting it or catching it in another blast sets it off.
- To **disarm** one, use a **wrench** on it and wait eight seconds. A clumsy hand can set it off instead.

### Degaussing

- A **degaussing coil** cuts her signature by 80% while it runs. Bolt it to her deck with a wrench, then click it to switch it on. On a submarine it uses one unit of battery for every tile she moves. On a slipway ship it burns fuel oil, so a ship under sail has no coil. Any other ship needs an engine running aboard. When the power fails the coil switches itself off.
- A **wiping station** stands on the shore or a quay, wired to a cable with 30 power to spare. Feed it 20 lengths of cable coil for each wiping. Stop the ship within 2 tiles of it, then click the station and wait two minutes. The wiping takes 70% off her signature and slowly wears off as she sails, until it is gone after about 700 tiles.
- A coil and a wiping together cut her signature by about 94%.

### Minesweeping

Both sweeps are bolted to a deck with a wrench, then clicked to stream them over the side or to wind them back in.

- A **magnetic sweep reel** sets off every magnetic mine within 6 tiles of the reel as the ship moves. It needs the same power as a degaussing coil and is wound in when the power fails. The mines still go off close by, so sweep in a wooden or wiped ship, well ahead of steel ships.
- An **Oropesa sweep** cuts the moorings of contact mines 1 to 3 tiles off her sides while she is under way. A cut mine bobs up to the surface where everyone can see it. Shoot it from a safe distance, or disarm it with a wrench.

### Torpedoes

- Unbolt a **torpedo tube** and click it to turn it a quarter turn. When it points where you want, bolt it to a deck or a quay with a wrench. Load a torpedo into it (four seconds), then click it to fire.
- The torpedo runs straight along the tube's line for up to 40 tiles, just under the surface. It sinks when it reaches the shallows or the end of its run.
- A **torpedo** goes off when it strikes a hull, so its path has to cross her.
- A **magnetic torpedo** also goes off under a steel hull it passes close to, by the same rule as a magnetic mine. A wooden, wiped or degaussed hull lets it run past unless it strikes her.
- A torpedo never goes off under the ship that fired it. A ship that runs onto a torpedo sets it off.

## Sailing and the wind

Sails depend on the wind. The **Status** tab shows the prevailing wind, for example "A moderate Northern breeze", and the game announces in chat when it changes. It can blow from any of eight directions, north, north-east, east and so on round the compass, and turns an eighth of the way at a time. The wind is named after the direction it **blows from**, so a northern wind pushes ships south. The ship's wheel and the tiller tell you how the wind stands to her where she is now.

The wind where you are is not always the prevailing wind:

- **Round storms and low pressure** the wind picks up and swings round them. North of the equator it circles them anticlockwise, south of it clockwise.
- **Along a coast** a sea breeze blows in off the water on sunny afternoons, and a land breeze blows back out to sea on clear nights. Cloud weakens both.
- **In hilly country** hilltops and ridges are windier and valleys are sheltered. A valley also turns the wind along its floor, and on clear nights cold air drifts down the slopes into it.

A **weather vane** reads the wind on its own spot. Build one by your harbour or mill to see what the wind is doing there. An **anemometer** counts it in m/s and on the Beaufort scale, and a **barometer** warns of a low before the wind gets up: a falling needle means reef early or make for harbour. See [Weather and Climate](Guide_to_Weather.md#wind).

| Course | Speed |
| --- | --- |
| Across the wind (northern wind, heading east or west): a **beam reach** | Full speed |
| With the wind behind you (northern wind, heading south): **running** | About 1.3 times slower |
| Into the wind (northern wind, heading north): **beating** | About 3.5 times slower, as she tacks back and forth across the wind |

A stronger wind is faster. A gale is the quickest, and with no wind at all sails barely move. A storm overhead blows a gale. These rules apply to outrigger canoes under sail, sailing ships and the sails on a hull you build yourself. If you need to go upwind, oars or an engine are the reliable choice.
