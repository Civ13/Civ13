# Guide to Character

***"Know thyself."***<br>
*The Delphic maxim*

~~What is a 2D spessman civilizer, if not a bunch of pixels with loads of code behind it dictating the way it runs around, builds and crafts stuff, and whacks other civilizers?~~

What is a human being, if not a sum of their physical abilities and mental faculties? The knowledge of one's strengths and weaknesses, the awareness of the workings of the body and the psyche pave the road to the ultimate ideal - steamrolling whatever the world throws at you.

## Character tab

Go to the Character tab on the right panel. There you will see various info about your character, including: 

- stamina level;
- civilization and religion to which you belong, if any; 
- current epoch;
- skill levels (stats).

## Skills

The "standard" maximum level of a skill is 2.5, but it may be higher with the excellent **[mood](../Starter_Guide.md#mood)** bonus and additional **[trait](#traits)** bonuses (for instance, excellent mood and the Gigantism trait provide strength of 4.3125). On the contrary, **bad mood decreases skill levels (for instance, with the horrible mood 2.5 becomes 2.0).**

Skills are trained by various activities and by reading **scientific literature** on respective subjects (skill books - slates, scrolls and books, depending on the epoch). Strength, dexterity and stamina can also be trained deliberately with [exercise equipment](Guide_to_Exercise.md). The literature written by a character with a higher skill level grants a larger skill boost to the reader. **You can only read literature written by others, not by yourself!**

**In game, open the IC tab and click Skills Guide.** It lists every skill with your current level, what the skill does, what raises it and the next recipes it will let you make. Where knowledge comes from books it also lists each discipline and the next mastery milestone. The Status tab shows the same levels at a glance.

Farming and hunting raise the yield of renewable food. Every 0.01 above 1.0 adds 1% more, so a skill of 1.5 gives half again as much and 2.0 or higher gives double. A skill at or below 1.0 never lowers the yield, and a bad mood lowers the bonus along with the skill.

On persistent worlds, variety and company teach more than repetition:

- **New things teach most.** The first time you craft each different recipe, complete each kind of surgery step or harvest each kind of crop, the skill gain is five times the usual. The next four times are normal, the five after that give half, and from then on a quarter. Making one of everything teaches far more than a hundred of the same knife.
- **Crafting grows step by step.** However big the thing you make, one craft raises crafting by at most about 2%, so becoming a master crafter takes many crafts.
- **Work beside a master.** While someone within 2 tiles is awake and at least a quarter better than you at a skill, everything you do with that skill teaches you double. It works for crafting, medical, farming, hunting, throwing, philosophy and the weapon skills, so spar with a better fighter and craft next to a better smith. The master loses nothing.
- **Mastery teaches scholarship.** Reaching 150, 200 and 250 in a skill gives a large boost to the matching discipline. See [Scholarship](Guide_to_Books_and_Scholars.md#scholarship).

P.S. 20 is a lie, it is 2**<u>.</u>**0

## Traits

You choose traits in **Character Setup** before you join: press **Setup Character & Preferences** in the lobby, or **Character Preferences Setup** in the OOC tab. The window has four tabs.

- **Character**: your name, gender, age, blood type, skin tone, eyes, hair and facial hair, with a preview you can turn around. **Randomise appearance** rolls a new look.
- **Traits**: every trait as a card with what it does and what it costs.
- **Birth chart**: the sign your character is born under. See [Birth charts](#birth-charts).
- **Settings**: HUD style, mouse cursor, frame rate, the tutorial and your sound and chat choices. These can be changed at any time.

![The Character tab of Character Setup](../images/Character_setup_character.png)

Perks cost points and drawbacks give points back. The top of the Traits tab shows your perks, your drawbacks and the difference. You can take a perk first and pay for it afterwards, but you can't join while you are over. Free traits cost nothing.

![The Traits tab of Character Setup](../images/Character_setup_traits.png)

- **Take** adds a trait and **Remove** drops it. The names under **Your traits** can also be removed with their x.
- Some traits can't be held together. Their button reads **Swap in** and the card says which trait it replaces. In a **Pick one** group, such as Playstyle, **Choose instead** replaces your current pick.
- The list on the left jumps to one group. **Find a trait** searches names and descriptions, and **Perks**, **Drawbacks** and **Free** narrow the list.
- **Random set** rolls a balanced set and **Clear all** drops everything.

Your body and traits are set when you join. To change them, wait until that character has died.

Some traits come as opposite pairs, and you can't take both halves of a pair:

| Perk (costs) | Drawback (gives back) |
| ------------ | --------------------- |
| Quick Learner (3): all stat skills train 15% faster | Slow Learner (3): all stat skills train 15% slower |
| Light Eater (2): you use 15% less food | Glutton (2): you use 20% more food |
| Deep Lungs (2): stamina recovers 25% faster | Asthmatic (2): stamina recovers 25% slower |
| Thick Skinned (2): wounds hurt 15% less | Low Pain Tolerance (2): wounds hurt 20% more |
| Bookworm (2): studying a research chapter takes 15% less time | Dyslexic (2): studying a research chapter takes 25% longer |
| Steady Hands (2): medical skill x1.15 | Butterfingers (2): crafting and medical skill x0.9 |
| Strong Back (1): pulling carts, crates and bodies slows you 20% less | Bad Back (1): pulling slows you 25% more |
| Sting Resistant (1): bee stings hurt a third as much and never knock you down | Bee Sting Allergy (2): stings hurt twice as much, and from the second sting you collapse, choking, for a while (it never kills) |

**Squeamish** (gives back 1) makes seeing someone maimed hurt your mood twice as much. Thick Skinned and Squeamish can't be taken with Hardened Fighter, and Strong Back can't be taken with Quartermaster.

Two more allergies each give back 1 point. **Hay Fever** sets you sneezing now and then outdoors in spring or the wet season, and blurs your eyes for a moment. **Shellfish Allergy** brings out an itchy rash and makes you throw up when you eat crab or lobster.

### Eyesight traits

- **Nearsighted** (gives back 2): everything past a few tiles fades into grey fog that grows darker toward the edges of your screen. You can still make out shapes in the fog. If you stand still for a moment you squint and the fog thins by about half.
- **Farsighted** (gives back 1): you squint at a paper or book for a moment before you can read it. Studying, writing and copying take 50% longer.

Corrective glasses or a monocle fix both traits while you wear them. Both can be crafted once your people know glassworking. Neither trait can be taken with Blind, but you can take both together.

### Playstyle traits

Playstyle traits suit a way of playing. Each one costs nothing because it comes with its own drawback, and you can take only one of them. Most drawbacks only slow how fast a skill trains, so you can still learn everything. Mood effects, food use and the Wanderer's stamina bonus apply on maps where you get hungry.

| Trait | Benefit | Drawback |
| ----- | ------- | -------- |
| Recluse Scholar | Studying and research are 25% faster with nobody else within 5 tiles | 25% slower with people around; your mood settles lower in crowds of 3 or more. Can't be taken with Extroverted |
| Menacing | **Intimidate** (IC tab) sours the mood of someone within 5 tiles who can see you, and wild grazing animals bolt from you | Everyone who examines you sees that you look dangerous; you are a slower tutor |
| Hardened Fighter | Sword and melee skill trains 50% faster; wounds hurt 15% less | Crafting and scholarship train 25% slower |
| Quartermaster | Pulling carts, crates and bodies slows you 40% less; examining a closed crate tells you how many items it holds | Combat skills train 25% slower |
| Green Thumb | Farming trains 50% faster and harvests yield about 10% more | Hunting and combat skills train 25% slower |
| Tinkerer | Crafting x1.1; Engineering scholarship trains 50% faster | Strength x0.9 |
| Wanderer | Stamina recovers 50% faster while you are on the move; you use 10% less food | Your mood settles lower once you have stayed within 20 tiles of one spot for 45 minutes |
| Rallying Voice | When you shout (end a line with !), friendly people who hear you get a small mood lift, at most once every 2 minutes each | Your mood settles lower when nobody is within 3 tiles. Can't be taken with Introverted |


### Fields of study

On [persistent worlds](Guide_to_Books_and_Scholars.md), a scholar can focus on one research discipline. A field of study is entirely optional and costs nothing. You can take one in character setup, or later with **Choose field of study** (IC tab). You can have only one, and Basic Mathematics is never affected.

| Field | Research speed in its discipline |
| ----- | -------------------------------- |
| Engineer | Engineering x1.1 |
| Strategist | Military Science x1.2 |
| Naturalist | Natural Science x1.3 |
| Agronomist | Agriculture x1.4 |
| Physician | Medicine x1.4 |

- Scholarship in your field grows 25% faster.
- In the other four disciplines, research runs at x0.9, scholarship grows 25% slower, and their chapters take 15% longer to study.
- Smaller disciplines get bigger boosts. An all-rounder who researches everything is a little slower with any field than with none; a field pays off for a scholar who mostly works in one discipline.
- You can drop your field at any time. After any change, you must wait 7 real days before picking a new one.
- Engineer can't be taken with Tinkerer.

### Study traits

Two more free traits change how a character studies. Each has its own strengths and costs, and they can be combined with any field of study or playstyle trait.

| Trait | Strength | Cost |
| ----- | -------- | ---- |
| Autistic | Chapters in your special interest take 20% less time to study. Your special interest is your field of study, or a discipline you choose with **Choose field of study** | The first chapter after you switch to a different discipline takes 35% longer. Going back to a chapter you started, or to your special interest, never costs |
| ADHD | The first minute of a reading sitting goes 30% faster, and the first five minutes at a research bench 20% faster | Reading slows by 20% after three minutes in one sitting, and bench work by 10% after twenty minutes. A minute away from reading, or two from the bench, starts a fresh sitting |

### Trades

A trade is an optional free trait for hands-on work. You can take one in character setup, or later with **Choose trade** (IC tab). You can have only one trade, and it can be combined with a field of study. A trade only changes how long the work takes; it never changes your crafting skill or which recipes you can make.

| Trade | Its own work takes | Covers |
| ----- | ------------------ | ------ |
| Mechanic | 40% less time | Building and repairing vehicles, aircraft and engines, submarine hull repairs, vehicle keys |
| Gunsmith | 30% less time | Gunsmith bench work, black powder gun assembly, fletching |
| Smith | 25% less time | Anvil work and the metal workbench |
| Tailor | 25% less time | Dressmaking, and crafting from cloth, wool, hides and leather |
| Carpenter | 8% less time | Crafting from wood and stone, and woodworking |
| Navvy | 20% less time | Digging, trenches, channels, wells, tunnels, and raising or lowering ground |
| Miner | 25% less time | Mining rock, ore seams and cave roofs, and breaking up stone roads |
| Woodcutter | 20% less time | Felling trees |

- The work of the other trades takes 10% longer. Work that belongs to no trade is unchanged.
- Trades for rarer work get bigger boosts. An all-rounder comes out about even or a little slower with any trade; a trade pays off for someone who mostly does one kind of work.
- You can drop your trade at any time. After any change, you must wait 7 real days before picking a new one.

## Birth charts

Every new character is born under one of twelve signs, one for each season of the year. Your sign leans your starting skills a little: one skill +10 and two +5, one skill -10 and two -5, from the usual starting level of 100. Every sign adds and takes away the same amount, so none is better than another, only different. Skills still grow with use, so a sign is a head start and a character, not a limit.

Your sign's +10 skill also trains 5% faster and its -10 skill 5% slower. This does not apply to a skill whose training speed one of your traits already changes, such as farming with Green Thumb, or any skill with Quick Learner or Slow Learner. The trait's effect is used instead.

By default your sign is drawn at random when you are born. Players who have verified their Discord account (**Verify Discord** in the OOC tab) may choose their sign in the **Birth chart** tab of Character Setup. Your sign is written in your Notes and stays with your character for life. A new character can be born under a different sign.

## Languages

Your character belongs to a people, and your people's language is your mother tongue. You know every language to a score from 0 to 100: you can speak one from 25 (Broken), read and write paper in it from 60 (Conversational), and you are fluent from 90.

**Learning a language on your own takes days; a fluent teacher gets you there in about fifteen minutes.** You pick it up by hearing and speaking with people who know it better, by taking lessons from a fluent speaker (**Teach Language**), from a language book, and by studying writing. Related languages, such as German and Dutch, give you a head start in each other.

The IC tab has the following commands regarding languages: **Check Default Language**, **Check Known Languages**, **Set Default Language** and **Teach Language**.

See [Peoples and Languages](Guide_to_Peoples_and_Languages.md) for the peoples of each land, the levels and every way to learn.

## Combat

You can alter your combat behaviour with the following options:<br>
<img src="../images/hud_combat.png"><br>
from left to right, **tactics**, **mode** and **secondary attack**.

**<u>Tactics</u>**

**Aim** - increases accuracy by 10%

**Charge** - increases melee damage by 10%

**Defend** - increases the chance to parry or dodge by 20%

**Rush** - reduces the melee weapon cooldown by 15%

Click the icon to switch or use the N hotkey in the hotkeys mode.

**<u>Mode</u>**

**Dodge** - focuses on dodging melee attacks rather than parrying. Good when you have a small weapon or fight without a shield. Influenced by the dexterity skill.

**Parry** - focuses on parrying melee attacks rather than dodging. Works better when you use a shield or a larger weapon.

**<u>Secondary attack</u>**

**Bite** - when there are no other options. You bite an arm or hand: aim at one to pick it, otherwise you bite whichever arm or hand is nearest your aim.

**Kick** - when the enemy thinks handcuffing you is enough.

**Jump** - when the enemy thinks you are cornered (maximum jumping distance is 2 tiles).

These are tied to the middle mouse button. Could be switched with the U hotkey in the hotkeys mode.

### Pulling punches

Allows sparring without beating each other to a bloody pulp. On the IC tab, click "Pulling punches" - now you will not harm an opponent when attacking them **unarmed**. Click again to stop pulling punches.
