# Resource Extraction

Use underground mining for larger batches from finite deposits, or gather smaller trace batches without consuming terrain. Fossils come from finite deposits only. Check the map's crafting catalog, your skills and your faction's research before planning a machine chain.

## Manual trace gathering

On uncovered, non-mineable dirt or sand, choose **Crafting > Sift soil for resources**. Stay still for three minutes to obtain one random unit. **Basic Toolmaking** unlocks a wooden resource sieve costing **4 wood**; activate it in your active hand for a two-minute, one-unit batch.

Copper, tin, raw sand and stone are available first. Iron and calamine (zinc ore) join the soil pool at age 1; coal, saltpeter, sulphur and lead join at age 2. Trace gathering does not supply fossils.

For **Pan river sediment**, hold a wooden bowl in your active hand on eligible soil beside shallow freshwater. A two-minute action yields one random copper, tin, silver or gold ore. Bottled water, salt water and deep water do not qualify. Movement, incapacitation, changing the active hand or replacing the soil interrupts gathering; panning also needs the original water tile to remain valid.

## Powered gathering

**Electricity** unlocks the industrial drill, powered soil sifter and powered river concentrator. Each costs **20 electronics and 20 steel**, reserves **150 cable power**, and produces **2 random units every 2 minutes** while running.

| Machine | Placement | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Industrial drill | Anchored on uncovered dirt, on the surface or underground | Mineral pool |
| Powered soil sifter | Anchored on uncovered, non-mineable dirt or sand | Soil trace pool |
| Powered river concentrator | The same soil, beside shallow freshwater | Copper, tin, silver or gold ore |

Connect cable power and activate the machine. Every restart waits a full interval. Loss of power, invalid terrain or anchoring, or blocked output stops extraction. The concentrator also stops if its water tile is replaced or no longer qualifies.

Output fills matching stacks up to their limits. A tile holds at most **12 output stacks**; room in existing matching stacks remains usable at that limit. Clear output when the machine cannot place another batch. Saving restores these machines stopped: reconnect and activate them to resume.

[Power & Batteries](Guide_to_Power.md) explains cable supplies. **Paved Infrastructure** provides plain white cable for the Electricity prototype; colored cable and electronics require Electricity. Electronics use copper and iron. Use [Metallurgy](Guide_to_Metallurgy.md) to establish molds, furnaces and metal production.

## Mining several ores

Each ore in a rock wall rolls on its own, so one mined wall can drop several kinds of ore at once, for example coal together with iron. Coal is twice as common as it used to be. Some walls now give no ore at all, because the drops cluster. A wall that gives ore also gives 8-12 stone, and a wall with no ore gives 16-24.

## Each continent's ores

On the continents, each one is rich in its own resources, which turn up **twice as often** there:

| Continent | Rich in |
| --- | --- |
| [Gaia](../maps/Gaia.md) | copper |
| [North America](../maps/The_Americas.md) | iron and lead |
| [South America](../maps/The_Americas.md) | gold, silver and bauxite |
| [Europe](../regions/Europe.md) | coal and calamine (zinc) |
| [Africa](../regions/Africa.md) | sulphur and tin |
| [Asia](../regions/Asia.md) | saltpeter, silver and gemstones |
| [Oceania](../regions/Oceania.md) | bauxite, uranium and gemstones |
| [Greenland](../regions/Greenland.md) | calamine (zinc), lead and gold |
| [Antarctica](../regions/Antarctica.md) | coal |

Everywhere else these are scarcer: copper, tin, coal, calamine, silver, gold and gems turn up at half the usual rate, and saltpeter, sulphur, lead and bauxite at a third (these lower rates phase in over a world's first two weeks). **Iron** is found at the usual rate on every continent. Gaia also keeps tin at the usual rate, so you can reach bronze and iron there on your own, but it has **no bauxite or uranium**: later industry needs trade or a foothold on another continent. This applies to rock walls, gold and silver lodes, gem pockets and mountain seams. Lands across the sea keep their own ores. **Province Holdings** on the Faction tab shows what your continent is rich in.

### Rubber trees

Rubber comes only from the jungle. In the jungles of **Africa** and **Asia**, every living jungle tree is a rubber tree. Other continents have none; lands across the sea keep theirs.

1. Hold a **knife** in one hand and use an open container (a bucket, bowl or jar) on the tree. It catches **10 units of latex**. A tree can be tapped again after about 20 minutes.
2. Use a container holding at least 10 latex on a **sulphur rock** to cure one **sheet of rubber**.

Rubber does what plastic sheeting does in these places: a sheet and a copper ingot make **insulated cable**, five sheets rubberise an [airship gas cell](Guide_to_Aircraft.md), and three make **rubber boots**.

### Rich provinces

Two or three [provinces](../gamemodes/Civilizations_and_Nomads.md#provinces) of each continent are rich in one of its own resources. There that resource turns up **three times as often** instead of twice. Each rich province also has a **rich seam**, a tile near its middle marked with the ore. Work it with a pickaxe for a load of that ore at a time. It never runs out, but it gives only so many loads a day:

| Holder's settlement | Loads a day |
| --- | --- |
| Nobody holds it, or no rank yet | 4 |
| Camp | 6 |
| Village | 10 |
| Town | 16 |
| City | 24 |

Anyone can work a rich seam, so the side that holds the province will want to guard it. A side's third and later rich provinces on a land give half as many loads. Each ore can be worked from the same age as in a rock wall. **Province Holdings** and the seat's Province ledger name the rich provinces and give each seam's position.

## Opening a mined mountain to the sky

Mining into a mountain leaves the rock above you in place, so a dug-out hall stays dark and roofed and needs mine supports. To open it up, use a pickaxe on a mined tile that sits next to open ground. After about 12 seconds (faster with strength and a better pickaxe) the rock overhead is broken away and that spot is outdoors: it gets sun and rain, outdoor crops grow there, and it no longer needs supports. Work inward from the open edge one tile at a time. This gives no stone or ore, and the rock does not grow back, but you can still build a roof over the spot later. Clear away furniture and other structures first; mine supports can stay.

## Old mountain floor and ore seams

On the North and South America and Asia lands, the ground left behind once a mountain has been mined away is still worth working. Use a pickaxe on that bare floor. Each job takes twice as long as breaking a rock wall.

- Most of the old floor is bedrock and gives **6-10 stone** every time. It never runs out.
- About one tile in twelve is an **ore seam**. Mining the rock above a seam reveals it ("A vein of copper runs on down into the floor here."), and a faint ore mark stays on the tile. Each job on a seam gives **4-8 ore** and some stone. Coal gives 8-12, saltpeter, sulphur and lead 3-4, and silver and gold 1-2.
- A seam holds **six loads**. When it is worked out it gives stone only, and one load comes back every **20 minutes**. Examine a seam to see whether it is rich, thinning or worked out.
- Seams of the same ore cluster together, so one hillside may be a copper district and the next an iron district. Iron and calamine seams can be worked from age 1, and coal, saltpeter, sulphur and lead seams from age 2. Until then such a seam gives only stone.
- Gems, flint, obsidian, diamonds and other rare finds still come only from fresh rock, and fresh rock is about twice as productive, so unmined mountains remain the better dig.

## Digging down

Every land with ground under it has four underground levels of solid rock beneath the surface. To get to the first, stand on diggable ground with a shovel in hand, use the shovel (click it in your hand) and pick **Tunnel**. After about 20 seconds (faster with strength and crafting skill) you break through into the rock below and a tunnel entrance leads down.

Underground, pick **Tunnel** again and choose **Down** to dig a shaft into the next level, or **Up** to dig back toward the level above. Each level down takes about 10 seconds longer to break into than the one above it, and the fourth level is the deepest. Every level has its own rock, veins and natural caves, so a find on one level says nothing about the level below.

The deeper you go, the richer the ores, though each one still needs the age it normally needs:

| Level | Richer in |
| --- | --- |
| First | The ordinary mix of ores, flint and bauxite |
| Second | Iron, copper, tin, calamine and lead, and twice as much coal |
| Third | Silver, gold, gemstones, diamonds and uranium, and sulphur and saltpetre |
| Fourth | The richest silver and gold, the most diamonds, gemstones and uranium, and more nickel, cobalt, chromite, cinnabar and obsidian |

Flint and bauxite lie near the top: there is less bauxite on every level below the first, and no flint below the second.

Down there the rock opens up only where you dig: each tile you mine or dig out shows the rock around it, and now and then you break into a natural cave. What lies under a tile depends on what is on the surface above it:

| Above | Below |
| --- | --- |
| Dry ground | Ordinary cave rock to mine or dig |
| A deep lake or river | Wet rock. Breaking it lets the water pour into your tunnel. |
| Shallows, swamp or coast water | Wet earth that slowly seeps water into the tunnel |
| The sea | Solid seabed rock |
| Lava | Searing hot rock that cannot be dug and burns anyone who touches it |

Only the first level feels the water above it; the deeper levels lie under its rock, so lakes, rivers and shallows do not reach them. The sea floor and lava go all the way down. A tunnel down stops when it hits solid stone or hot rock, and you cannot dig up into water or lava; the game tells you why, so try another spot. Pack a flooding tunnel with dirt to stop the water (see [Filling tunnels back in](#filling-tunnels-back-in)).

## Blasting rock

Once your faction knows **Gunpowder**, find the in-game blasting charge entry in the
**Crafting Atlas**. A detonated blasting charge near ordinary mineable rock leaves
finite **blasted rock clusters** where the rock opened. It does not refill
exposed floors, spent clusters, or loose debris. Stay clear of the game's
ordinary blast damage. The wall rolls its source geology once. Part of a
multi-unit ore find comes loose, and the rest stays in the cluster as
**ore-bearing rubble**. Deeper rock gives more building stone, not more ore
rolls.

Use a **pickaxe** on a cluster to break off up to **five units** at a time.
Inspect the cluster to see what's left. Clear the output from the floor if
it backs up. The cluster shrinks as you work; it disappears when exhausted
and does not refill on restart. Use the stone for **stone bricks** or crush it
for sand. When you harvest named rubble, such as **iron-bearing rubble**, use a
stone crusher or stamp mill to recover one unit of that ore per chunk. The
rubble keeps the same ore when you carry it to another region. Sandstone and
marble use their own ordinary crafting routes.

Some dry underground pockets have **hard quarry rock**. Pickaxes and shovels
cannot open these walls. Ordinary blasting charges open shallow hard bands
on the first two cave levels. On the third and fourth cave levels, **deep
quarry rock** needs a **reinforced quarry charge**, unlocked by **Deep
Quarrying** after Ore Dressing. Both charges still need gunpowder to use.
The stronger charge reaches
harder rock, not farther. Examine the wall before using a charge.

For the same stone roll, shallow hard bands leave one-third more stone
than blasting ordinary rock; deep bands leave about two-thirds more.
The bands are pockets: use ordinary rock or an open pocket to dig around
one if you are only trying to reach the next cave level.

In the dedicated **Tutorial** region, read **Quarry stone and process it** on
the camp lesson board. A sample cluster, pickaxe, and crusher are in the yard
past the camp's north door. It teaches collection and processing without
needing a charge.

## Digging for dirt underground

Dirt piles make dirt walls and barricades, turn into clay with water, and fill graded ground back up. On the surface you scoop them from dirt ground with a shovel or cut them out of a hill. Underground there is more:

- **Dig out cave rock:** use a shovel on a soft cave rock wall. After about 30 seconds (faster with strength and a better shovel) the wall is dug out and leaves **2 dirt piles**; sandy rock also leaves a sand pile. You get no stone or ore this way, so use a pickaxe when you want those. The new floor can still be scooped for more dirt.
- **Clear rubble:** use a shovel on cave-in rubble to clear it in about 12 seconds for **1 dirt pile**.
- **Dig a tunnel:** when you finish digging a tunnel down or up with a shovel, the earth from the shaft is left as **2 dirt piles** at your feet.

Frozen rock is too hard for a shovel; break it with a pickaxe. A dug-out tunnel can cave in like a mined one, so put up mine supports.

### Filling tunnels back in

- **Close a hole:** use a dirt pile on a tunnel entrance or dug hole. Each pile takes a couple of seconds and four piles close it for good. Examine the hole to see how many piles it still needs; the count is kept across saves. If a wooden ladder is fitted, you get it back when the hole closes.
- **Pack a tunnel:** below ground, hold a shovel on help or grab intent with a dirt pile in your other hand (or lying on the tile) and use the shovel on an open tunnel floor next to you. After about 6 seconds the tile is packed solid with earth. The tile must be empty of people and objects, and a hole right above it must be closed first. Packed earth can be dug out again for its one dirt pile.

Groundwater cannot seep into packed earth, so packing a tunnel that runs under a river stops it flooding there. See [Floods](Guide_to_Floods.md).

## Stone crushing

Use a **stone crusher** (Resource Processing) or **powered stamp mill** (Electricity) for named ore-bearing rubble or excess stone. Plain stone from a mined deposit can yield sand but no more ore. Older or otherwise unmarked stone blocks still work and have a small chance of trace ore from the Gaia table, regardless of where you crush them. See [Factory Automation](Guide_to_Factory_Automation.md#stone-crushing).

## Petroleum wells

Wooden oil wells remain renewable and must sit on an oil spring. Use a barrel on the well to load it; click a loaded well to remove the barrel, or an empty well to connect a nearby barrel. Extraction runs when the spring and contained barrel are valid. The well does not fill unrelated barrels on the ground. Unloading or destroying the well returns its barrel. Saving retains the barrel and petroleum; check the barrel connection after loading before collecting output.

On the continents, oil springs on land flow only on the **desert ground** (desert sand or dust) of **Africa**. Elsewhere on the continents they run dry for good, after the same two-week phase-in as the scarcer ores, and a well over a dry spring stops. Examine a spring to see whether it still flows. Lands across the sea keep their springs. Offshore rigs work on every continent.

## Offshore drilling rigs

**Combustion** also unlocks the **offshore drilling rig**, which costs **45 steel**. Lay a steel bridge deck out over deep saltwater, stand on it and build the rig there. Not every stretch of sea holds oil. If the seabed under the deck is dry, the build is refused and nothing is spent, so try another spot further out. Seabed oil fields vary in size: a thin field pumps **10 petroleum**, a good one **20** and a rich one **30** every two minutes. Examine the rig to see which kind it is drilling.

Rigs need no oil spring and never run dry. Load, remove and connect barrels the same way as on a wooden well, or connect factory pipes. Saving keeps the barrel and its petroleum. A hammer takes the rig apart and returns some of its steel.

## Hydraulic fracturing

At **Modern**, research **Hydraulic Fracturing**, which requires **Electricity** and **Modern Construction**. Craft the rig from **20 electronics and 20 steel**.

On the continents, fracking works only in **North and South America**, and only in a [province](../gamemodes/Civilizations_and_Nomads.md#provinces) your side holds. It costs the land around it: each batch [pollutes](Guide_to_Pollution.md) the ground within 3 tiles of the rig, so nearby crops slow and then stop growing, and it fouls the groundwater, so wells and puddles within 6 tiles give dirty water for a day. Lands across the sea frack as before.

1. Anchor it beside an undepleted, mineable underground rock tile on the same level.
2. Connect a cable network with **300 available power**.
3. Load a barrel containing only freshwater, an empty or petroleum-only barrel with at least **50 units free**, and at least **5 raw sand**.
4. Activate the rig and allow the two-minute batch to complete.

Each successful batch consumes **20 water**, **5 sand** and **one rock tile**, producing **50 petroleum**. The rig stops after each batch; check supplies and the next target before restarting. Missing or contaminated inputs, removed inputs, replaced terrain, insufficient output capacity or lost power stop the batch without paying its inputs. **Unload Fracking Rig** stops it and returns contained inputs.

Depleted rock stays depleted through saves. Moving or rebuilding a rig does not renew that deposit. Saved rigs retain their contained inputs and restore stopped. **Combustion** unlocks the petroleum refinery; refine and use fuel accepted by the destination engine rather than assuming every petroleum mixture works in every engine.
