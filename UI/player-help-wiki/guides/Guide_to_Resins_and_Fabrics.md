# Guide to Resins, Silk and Fabrics

Tree sap and treated cloth run through every era. In the Stone Age you tap pine trees for resin and cook it into lumps. Later you raise silkworms, coat cloth against the rain and brush lacquer onto your best wooden things. With industry, the bakelizer turns petroleum into phenolic resin, nylon, epoxy and printing resin, which make laminate armour, fiberglass and precise printed parts.

```admonish info
Tapping a tree and cutting bark both need a **knife in your other hand**. A tapped tree needs about twenty minutes before it bleeds again, so tap several trees.
```

## Pine resin

Any living pine, on any land, bleeds resin.

1. **Tap the tree.** Hold a knife in one hand and an open container, such as a bowl or bucket, in the other. Click the pine with the container. You score the bark and catch 10 units of liquid resin.
2. **Cook it down.** In help intent, click a lit brazier, hearth or stove with a container holding 10 resin. You get a **lump of resin**, and 2 units of **turpentine** stay behind in the container.
3. **Melt it again.** Heat an open container over a fire in grab intent, then click a resin lump with it to melt the lump back into 10 units of liquid resin.

Liquid resin and turpentine also refuel torches and lamps. Resin lumps go into the resin body shield at the armor workbench (8 cloth and 4 resin), into spirit varnish and into the hot press.

## Silk

On the Asian lands of Earth, and on lands that are not one of Earth's continents, living broadleaf trees are **mulberries**.

1. **Pick leaves.** Click a mulberry in grab intent with an empty hand for **mulberry leaves**. Now and then the leaves carry a **clutch of silkworm eggs**. Eggs and leaves can be carried and traded, so silk can be raised anywhere that gets leaves.
2. **Rear the worms.** Build a **silkworm tray** (4 wood, economy & production category, needs Weaving). Put in one clutch of eggs and 6 mulberry leaves. After about ten minutes the tray gives 6 **cocoons** and keeps the clutch for the next batch.
3. **Reel the thread.** Heat an open container of water, then click the cocoons with it. Up to 5 cocoons unwind into **silk thread**, one thread each.
4. **Weave.** Use the thread on a loom to weave **silk cloth**.

Silk cloth makes kimonos, hanfu, sashes and scarves, and it is the best cloth for resin laminate.

## Coated cloth

Plain cloth soaked in a coating keeps out rain and spray. Click a stack of cloth with an open container of the coating: each sheet takes 5 units, up to 5 sheets at a time.

| Coating | Makes | Rain kept out by clothes made from it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pitch from the tar kiln | Tarred canvas | 75% |
| Linseed, olive, sunflower or corn oil | Oilcloth | 75% |
| A cake of beeswax, rubbed in | Waxed cloth (up to 3 sheets per cake) | 70% |
| Latex, with sulphur held in your other hand | Rubberised cloth | 90% |

For waxed cloth, click the cloth with a beeswax cake (or the cake with the cloth); the cake is used up. For rubberised cloth, one lump of sulphur cures a whole batch.

Clothes sewn from coated cloth keep out as much rain as the cloth does. The oilskin coat and sou'wester are made from oilcloth. Oilcloth and rubberised cloth both make ponchos, and rubberised cloth also makes gas masks and rubberised gas cells for airships. Fifteen sheets of tarred canvas make a small tarred sail.

## Lacquer and varnish

**Lacquer** comes from the same broadleaf trees as silk. Hold a knife in one hand and click the tree with an open container: it catches 5 units of raw lacquer. Keep it off bare skin, because it raises a rash.

**Spirit varnish** can be made anywhere. Mix 2 units of liquid resin with 1 unit of distilled ethanol in a container for 3 units of varnish.

To use either, make a **finishing pot** (2 wood, tools category, needs Fine Furniture) and pour in at least 5 units. In help intent, click an item of wood, bamboo or leather, a wooden shield, or leather or lamellar armour. Each coat uses 5 units. A finished item wears out 25% more slowly until the coat is worn through, and lacquer lasts about twice as long as varnish. A finish never changes armour or weapon stats. You can brush on a fresh coat once the old one is half worn.

## The bakelizer and its resins

The bakelizer cooks petroleum into plastic sheets. Use its **Change Setting** verb to make something else instead. More settings open as your era advances:

| Setting | From | Every 5 petroleum makes | Take it out |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Plastic | Always | Plastic sheets | Comes out as sheets |
| Phenolic resin | Always | 10 units of liquid resin | Click with an empty container |
| Thermoplastic pellets | World War II, needs Industrial Plastics | 1 measure of pellets | Remove verb |
| Nylon thread | World War II | 2 skeins of nylon thread | Remove verb |
| Epoxy | Cold War | 10 units of epoxy | Click with an empty container |
| Photopolymer | Digital Age | 2 bottles of photopolymer resin | Remove verb |

Turn the bakelizer off before changing its setting. Each batch takes a minute and needs cable power. Thermoplastic pellets feed the plastics molding machine; see the [Guide to Factory Automation](Guide_to_Factory_Automation.md#plastics-molding-machine).

## Resin laminate

The **hot press** (12 steel, production category, needs Combustion Engine) bonds cloth and resin into hard **resin laminate**.

1. Pour at least 10 units of phenolic resin, or plain pine resin, into the press from a container.
2. Click the press with 4 sheets of plain cloth or silk.

Phenolic resin gives 2 sheets of laminate from cloth and 3 from silk. Plain pine resin gives half as much. At the armor workbench, Dayfield body armor takes 4 laminate and 6 steel, and heavy Dayfield body armor takes 6 laminate and 14 steel.

## Particle board

Large and powered saw mills leave a pile of **sawdust** beside the planks for every two logs they cut. The primitive saw mill leaves none. The hot press bonds sawdust and resin into **particle board**:

1. Pour at least 10 units of phenolic resin, or plain pine resin, into the press.
2. Click the press with 4 piles of sawdust.

Phenolic resin gives 3 sheets and pine resin gives 2. Particle board is cheaper and quicker to build with than planks, but weaker. Use the sheets in hand to build these:

| Build | Sheets |
| --- | --- |
| Shelf | 4 |
| Table | 4 |
| Wardrobe | 4 |
| Wall cabinet | 3 |
| Bookcase | 4 |
| Partition wall | 3 |
| Floor | 1 |

Particle board is for indoor work only, and it cannot be built aboard a ship. Keep it dry. Sheets or builds that sit in floodwater, puddles or open water, or out in the rain, swell after about two minutes. Swollen sheets can no longer be built with, and a swollen partition wall is half as strong. After about five wet minutes it falls apart and drops whatever it held.

## Nylon

Weave nylon thread at a loom for **nylon cloth**, a light, strong cloth that does not rot. It makes foldable canopies, ponchos, folding umbrellas and rope.

## Fiberglass and aramid laminate

Weave glass sheets at a loom for **glass cloth**. Then build a **layup table** (10 steel, production category, needs Modern Construction) and pour in at least 10 units of epoxy.

- 3 glass cloth lay up into 2 sheets of **fiberglass**, which makes hardhats.
- 3 Kevlar sheets bond into 2 sheets of **aramid laminate**, which makes civilian kevlar armor and the 6B3 body armor.

## Resin 3D printing

The **resin 3D printer** (12 electronics, needs 3D Printing) stands beside the plastic printer and needs cable power in the same way. Load bottles of photopolymer resin by hand or with arms and belts. It prints small, precise parts: lathe-grade gear sets, bearings and springs, chess sets and dice. The plastic printer keeps all its own recipes. See the [Guide to Factory Automation](Guide_to_Factory_Automation.md) for powering and feeding printers.
