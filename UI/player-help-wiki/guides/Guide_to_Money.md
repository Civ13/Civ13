# Guide to Money

Money starts as metal and ends as coin. Every coin is struck by players from their own metal, and the faction whose mark it carries vouches for it.

## Weighing metal

A **trading balance** (2 wood) weighs metal sheets and coins in **shekels** and tells you what they are worth in silver. One coin weighs one shekel, and a sheet weighs 10. See [Tribal Life](Guide_to_Tribal_Life.md#weighing-metal) for the full details.

## Striking coins

Coins aren't crafted from a sheet. They are struck at an anvil with a **coin die**.

1. Craft a coin die from 1 bronze (economy & production). It needs Trade & Coinage.
2. Your faction's leader, or an officer trusted with the mint, uses the uncut die in hand and cuts it with the faction's mark. From then on it is the *coin die of your faction*.
3. Stand beside an anvil, hold the die, and use it on copper, silver or gold sheets.
4. Choose how many sheets to strike. For silver or gold, also choose how fine the coins should be.

A full-weight sheet strikes **10 coins**. The coins are named after your faction, for example "Eagles silver coins".

Anyone holding the die can strike coins with your faction's mark, so guard it like a seal.

### Debased coins

Silver and gold coins can be struck at 90%, 75% or 50% fine. Each sheet of precious metal then makes more coins, and copper makes up the rest. The copper sheets must be in your hands or at your feet. For example, one sheet of silver at 50% fine needs one copper sheet and strikes 20 coins.

A debased coin looks the same as a full one, but it's only worth the metal in it. The resource forge and the coin smelter both count its real metal. A trading balance shows it plainly, as "debased silver (50% fine)", so traders who weigh their coins will catch it.

### Keeping coins apart

Coins only stack with coins of the same metal, the same faction and the same fineness. Unmarked coins stay separate from marked ones. Splitting a pile keeps its mark.

## Melting coins

A coin smelter melts coins back by their metal: 10 full coins make one sheet. A debased coin gives back its share of the precious metal, and its copper.

## Faction treasury

A faction keeps its money as coin in a **strongbox** (10 wood, economy & production). It needs Trade & Coinage.

1. Build a strongbox where it can be guarded.
2. Your faction's leader uses it with an empty hand and chooses **Make it the treasury**. It is then bolted down and locked. A faction has one treasury box. Making a new one turns the old one back into a plain chest.
3. The leader can open it, and so can the faction's treasurers. That means anyone appointed to an office with the treasury power, such as the Treasurer, or anyone carrying a [faction seal](Guide_to_Paperwork.md) of such an office. Revoked seals don't open it. See [offices and elections](../gamemodes/Civilizations_and_Nomads.md#offices-and-elections).

Keys don't fit a strongbox and it can't be unbolted. Someone outside the faction can still pry it open with a crowbar or hammer. That takes a minute, and every member online is warned.

### What the box does

- **Taxes.** Sales and business taxes build up as players trade. A keeper chooses **Collect taxes into the box** to turn them into coin inside it. The leader sets both rates (0-30%) at the box.
- **Wages.** The leader gives a member a wage in silver a day. It builds up for at most 7 days unpaid. Members collect wages from the coin that is actually in the box, so an empty treasury pays nothing until it is refilled. Whatever the box can't pay stays owed.
- **Dues.** The leader can set weekly dues. Members pay by holding coins and choosing **Pay dues from my hand**. The box records who paid, but nobody is forced to pay. That's up to the faction.
- **Ledger.** Any member can read who collected, paid and changed what, along with the coin in the box and the taxes waiting.

### Bills and notes

A treasury can pay in paper, so merchants don't have to carry sacks of coin. At the strongbox, a treasurer chooses **Draw bills or notes**:

- **Write a bill of exchange** for 1 to 500 silver. It uses one blank sheet of paper in your hands or at your feet.
- **Print treasury notes**: up to 20 notes of one value at a time. You must stand beside a printing press, and each note uses a blank sheet.

Anyone can bring a bill or note to that faction's strongbox and use it on the box to be paid in coin, whether or not they belong to the faction. The box pays only with the coin inside it. If it can't cover the whole bill, it pays what it can, and the rest stays owed on the bill until the box is refilled. A treasury that promises more than it holds will see the first people to redeem get paid while everyone else waits. The ledger shows the coin in the box next to what is promised on open bills.

Every bill is numbered in the faction's register. Copies and forgeries are worthless, and examining a bill tells you if the register doesn't know it.

## Money changers

Every faction strikes its own coin, so traders often carry coin that a market won't take. A **money changer's table** (6 wood, economy & production) swaps one faction's coin for another's. It needs Trade & Coinage.

1. Build the table and click it with an empty hand to keep it as yours. It locks, and only you can open it.
2. Put struck coin in it by using coins on it.
3. Choose **Set a rate** for each coin you will buy. The rate is a share of the coin's weighed worth, from 1% to 200%. Pay under 100% and you keep the difference.

Anyone can examine the table to see its rates and which coin is in its till. To change money, use your coins on the table and pick which coin you want back. You are paid in whole coins from the till, never more than the rate. The coins you hand over stay in the table, and a table can only pay with coin someone put there. **Give up the table** leaves its coin inside for the next person to claim it. Like a strongbox, an open table can be emptied by anyone.

## Land and deeds

A faction can register land and hand it out by deed.

1. Set a **boundary stone** (2 stone, economy & production) at each of two opposite corners of the plot. It needs Trade & Coinage.
2. The faction's registrar uses one stone and picks the other. A plot is 2 to 32 tiles on a side and can't overlap another registered plot. The registrar is the leader, someone appointed to an office with the land register power (such as the Registrar), or anyone carrying a [faction seal](Guide_to_Paperwork.md) of such an office.
3. The registrar names the plot and gets its **deed**.

Whoever holds the deed holds the land. To sell land, hand over its deed. Standing beside a keyed door or a chest on the plot, the holder can use the deed to lock or unlock it. Deeds don't work on jail doors or the faction strongbox. Examine a deed to check it against the register.

At a plot's boundary stone, the registrar can:

- read the register
- set a land tax of 0 to 100 silver a week
- rename the plot
- strike the plot from the register
- reissue the deed, which makes the old deed void wherever it is. Use this when a deed is lost, or when land is taken back for unpaid tax.

Anyone can pay land tax at the faction's strongbox with **Pay land tax from my hand**, including paying ahead. The game never throws anyone off their land; the faction enforces its own register.
