# Guide to Tanks
Tank crews need coordination as well as firepower.

There are 5 roles for the crew of the tank. In order to be effective,
all roles must communicate with each other, organize and work
together.

## The Roles

  - **The Commander**: The commander of the tank is the role requiring
    the most responsibility. You're the one calling the shots (quite
    literally). Your job is to calculate the distance between you and
    enemy and inform your gunner about the meter and the left right
    sway. You also have to organize the tank crew, tell driver where to
    drive, yell at loader what ammo type to load, and order
    machine-gunner to fire. To calculate the distance of the enemy, you
    have to buckle yourself down to the commander's seat. Once you sit
    down, you will now calculate the distance of anything between you,
    by clicking on it. After sitting, you will also be available to use
    the telescope of the tank. This way, you can see afar, and thus be
    able to calculate the longer distances. (That's the whole purpose)

  - **The Loader**: Your job is the easiest one. You will wait for the
    commander to tell you what shell type to load, and you will simply
    take the said shell from the rack or floor (depending on where your crew stored it) and will insert it into the turret. To achieve that, get
    behind the turret of the tank. Click on the rack behind you, then
    click on the shell. After taking the shell into your hand, click on
    the turret of the tank. Check that you loaded the requested shell type.

  - **The Gunner**: As a gunner, your job is to enter the parameters
    commander told you. Depending on the type of tank you might have to load the gun yourself using the autoloader (In your firing panel there is a button to load a shell). After that wait for his order to fire. To accomplish
    that, you must get to the side of the turret (preferably, you can
    buckle yourself down to the Gunners Seat) and left click on the
    turret. You will now access a menu, telling you what shell is
    loaded, what is the firing distance in meters, what is the
    left/right sway in numbers and a big fire button. To enter a
    parameter, you must click on the meter or left right sway button,
    then type down the numbers your commander gave you. After doing
    that, you must left click on the fire button and wait a couple of
    seconds to fire. Congratulations, you fired the turret. (Probably
    missed though)

  - **The Machine-Gunner**; You are expected to operate the machine gun
    in front of the tank. To do that, approach to the machine gun in
    front, inside the tank. Take an ammo belt from the floor into your
    hands, then left click on the gun to load the ammo. Once the ammo is
    loaded, left click on the gun with empty hands. Congratulations, you
    managed to man the gun. You are also now aiming forward to enhance
    your view. Now, to fire the gun, simply press on the direction you
    want to fire. (Have you actually hit anything with that machine
    gun?!)
    
  - **The Driver**: You are expected to drive/rotate the tank. Your job
    is more complex compared to the other roles, but do not let that
    scare you off. In order to start the tank, you must first sit on the
    driver's seat. After sitting (Buckle yourself by dragging your sprite into the Driver's seat or use the hotkey B) and having the Driver Wheel in your
    hand, you can start the engine by pressing Z, you must wait for the engine to fully start (Once the engine visibly starts to vibrate) and you can accelerate
    it by pressing Z again. To decelerate or to kill the engine, you
    must press the "C" key. To turn the tank left, you must press "A".
    To turn the tank to right, you must press "D". In order to shift to
    reverse you must press the button "S", but to do so, you must be halted. To be able to move
    forward again, you must press the key "W" to shift into forward. Keep practicing to master your
    blitzkrieg/encirclement skills.(You will probably run over your own
    soldiers)


**Tips:**
* The tank can only go forward or backwards and rotate to left and
right. This is different from WASD(W to go forward, S to go back, you
know the drill) control, so it may take some time to master. It is
advised to drive jeeps first to get used to the driving system.
* Cars, trucks, buses and tanks roll smoothly instead of jumping from tile to tile. Take one out of gear and it coasts on a little, slowing, and stops square on the next tile. A turn squares it on its tiles first.
* You can run over trees with your tank and it does not damage your tank.
* Any vehicle can ram a wall, pillar, door or barricade. The heavier it is and the faster it goes, the harder it hits: a wall that gives way lets it through a gear slower, one that holds stops it. The vehicle's front takes damage and the crew are thrown about. A tank pushes through wood and clay at a crawl; a light car mostly wrecks its own front on stone. Bollards stop vehicles while people walk between them; see [Fences, barricades and barriers](Guide_to_Construction.md#fences-barricades-and-barriers).
* You can insta gib/kill any infantry if you run over them.
* It is advised to face directly towards the enemy. It will make
shooting it a lot easier. Most tanks have their heaviest armour on the front.
* Your loader can not load the turret if you are moving. Let him load the gun
by halting the tank.
* If you run over non-deployed anti-tank mines/anti-tank grenades, they
will still explode and damage your tank.
* You can not rotate your tank on its own axis if it is standing still.
The tank must be moving in order rotate it.
* Keep reading the chatlog for your commander's instructions.
* Rotating the tank at same time when right next to another tank causes
a rare tank meeting to happen.(It is a bug and it completely ruins both
of the collided tanks.)
* Faster you go, easier it is to rotate the tank.
* Do not rotate your tank near deep water tiles. If a single tile of
your tank is on the deep water tile, you will be stuck there for an
eternity.

### Driving
So you want to finally be useful and not just dead weight to a crew eh? Few can drive, or well enough to do anything worthy
of mentioning. If you want to know how to drive you've got to listen
carefully to the steps given, and learn the mechanics of how our
machines really work. Also a tip, **the tank is safest place you can
be**, if you leave it because the tracks were broken you should shoot
yourself. You're not a true tanker. A real tanker stays with his tank
regardless of the damage until there is no tank to cling to. That
armored coffin becomes a moving pillbox if you use it well enough.

  - All modular vehicles are built with a matrix of a 5x5 area. This is
    how you'll judge where you'll be turning. Learn the position of the
    vehicle in all 4 directions and know where it is in that 5x5 area. A
    good idea is often to use a place, like the driver's seat for
    reference. Most vehicles have the front at the edge of the 5x5 area.
    Tanks are one of them.
  - Tanks should be well equipped for the enemy you're facing. If it's
    enemy armor, take more AP and APCR shells. If it's more infantry and
    buildings, take more HE. Overstock and you'll be too heavy to move.
    A good idea is to empty a crate of belts onto the floor for your MG
    in case you become stranded. The MG's will be your main tool of
    survival.
  - A tank crew can operate smoothly and effectively with just 1 crewman
    if they're a true veteran at it. However most can't run a tank
    effectively by themselves. The main things that you should have the
    experienced crew members in the tank be are the commander, to give
    fire orders and call hits, and driver. Your success relies almost
    entirely on these two positions. However you can get by alright
    without a commander if you're not against enemy armor. A good driver
    is the key to success.

#### Example: turning in tight spaces

A vehicle turns inside a square as wide as its longest side, which is 5x5 for most tanks. Its front always sits against the edge of that square. That means the tank's position inside the area shifts each time it turns: facing north, the tank sits against the north edge; facing south, against the south edge, and so on. Use the driver's seat as your reference point.

Use this to get into spaces you cannot turn into directly. Say you want to enter a narrow alley to your left, but a wall to the north stops you from turning left:

1. Drive up alongside the wall so the alley entrance is next to you.
2. Turn right instead, before the tank reaches the wall. Turning moves the tank to the other side of its square, clear of the wall.
3. Shift into reverse and back into the alley.

This lets you flank the enemy through routes they may not expect a tank to fit through.

### Doors

Tanks, trucks and cars have doors you can work by hand. Examine a vehicle to see whether its door is open, shut or locked, and who is holding it.

- **From outside**, use an empty hand on the door. This works on any intent except grab, which climbs onto the roof. An open door swings shut. A shut door that isn't locked pulls open. A locked door won't move.
- **Locks**: a vehicle with a key or a door code keeps its shut doors locked. The key unlocks the door and swings it open. Used on an open door, or on a shut one that isn't locked, the key locks it. A door shut by hand is not locked. A door with no lock never locks.
- **Holding the door shut**: from inside, use the door on **grab intent**, or the **Hold Car Door** verb (IC tab). The door goes into your hand. Use it in hand, or **Tighten or Loosen Grip**, to brace it. Drop it, or use the verb again, to let go.
- **Yanking it open**: anyone outside who pulls on a held door starts a tug of war. Each pull takes a moment. The stronger side is more likely to win, and pulling with both hands free helps. A tight grip holds much better than a loose one. A grip taken or tightened a second ago isn't braced yet, so a quick yank can catch it. Every pull you hold off tires your arm, and the arm recovers if the pulling stops.
- You let go of the door if you leave your seat's tile for outside the vehicle, pass out, or are tied up. A fall can shake a loose hold free. A blow to your hand can break even a tight grip.

### Towing

A vehicle that will not move by itself, with no fuel, a dead engine or a broken track, can be towed by another.

1. Park the towing vehicle in front of the other one, **in line, facing the same way**, with no more than **2 tiles** between them. Both must stand still.
2. Use a **rope** on the vehicle to be towed. After five seconds the line is fast and one rope is used.
3. Drive off. The towed vehicle rolls when you roll and **turns where you turned**, the same distance behind you all the way.

- The towing vehicle pulls the **weight of both**. An engine too small for the pair stalls, so tow with something at least as strong as what it pulls.
- If the towed vehicle is blocked, or cannot make a turn, the line pays out. More than **3 tiles** behind and it **snaps**; the rope is lost.
- You cannot **reverse** with a tow line out. Cast it off first.
- To let go, stop, right-click either vehicle and choose **Cast Off Tow Line**. The rope drops beside the towed vehicle.
- A vehicle takes one tow line and pulls one, so vehicles can be chained in a column. The line is gone after a server restart.

Examining either vehicle tells you what it tows or what tows it. Boats tow boats the same way; aircraft cannot be towed.

### Vehicle papers

A vehicle nobody has registered drives for anyone who sits in it. To make a tank, truck or car yours, **right-click the driver's seat** and choose **Vehicle Papers**.

| Entry | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Register it to your faction** | The vehicle is yours and you are its **commander**. Without a faction it is registered to you alone |
| **Sign on crew** | Add someone standing near the seat as **commander**, **driver**, **mechanic**, **gunner** or **crewman** |
| **Change a role or strike off** | Change a crew member's role or remove them |
| **Bar someone** / **Lift a bar** | A barred person leaves the crew and cannot ask to join |
| **Who may join** | Anyone may sign on, new crew must ask, or no new crew |
| **Write the memo** | A note every member of the crew can read in the papers |
| **Name the vehicle** | Give it a name of its own |
| **Our vehicles** | Lists every registered vehicle of your faction: how far, which way, whether it is rolling and who is driving |
| **Give up the vehicle** | Strike it from the register. Anyone may then drive or register it |

Who may drive a registered vehicle:

- **Members of the owning faction**, and listed crew who are **commander** or **driver**, drive as usual.
- **Anyone else** is asked whether to **force the controls**. It takes about 15 seconds and everyone aboard is warned. This only works while one of the owners is in the world.
- With **no owner about**, the controls are locked.

The **turret guns** follow a simpler rule: anyone on the crew list, whatever their role, can always fire them, and a stranger can only fire them while one of the owners is in the world. A tank parked while your faction is away cannot be turned on your base.

Slowing down and stopping are never locked. A vehicle that needs a **key** still needs it; the papers are a second lock, not a replacement. Examine the driver's seat to see who a vehicle belongs to. Modular boats and aircraft have the same papers at their driver's seat.

### Freight depots

A **freight depot** is a notice board where one faction pays another to haul a crate with a registered vehicle. Craft one from **8 wood** under vehicle parts (it needs the **Wagons & Ships** research) and set it up anywhere on land. Click it for its menu. A faction can **Claim the depot**, name it, and decide whether it takes freight from every party or only its own.

To take freight, bring a vehicle that is registered in its [vehicle papers](#vehicle-papers), with you among its owners or crew, within **8 tiles** of the depot.

**Freight** works exactly like the [freight contracts](Guide_to_Boats.md#freight-contracts) of a harbour: one faction leaves a sealed crate and a reward, another carries the crate to the depot or harbour it is bound for and is paid there. Freight can go from depot to depot, or between a depot and a harbour's mooring post.
