# Quicksilver Crafts

Beyond working gold and silver ore, quicksilver goes into gilding, mirrors, weather instruments and hats. Most of these crafts give off a little mercury vapour, so work in the open air or with a mask on. To learn how to get quicksilver and what it does to you, see [Mercury](Guide_to_Mercury.md). Gold amalgam is covered in [Amalgamation](Guide_to_Amalgamation.md).

You can pour quicksilver from a mercury flask or from any other open vessel.

## Fire gilding

Fire gilding needs **Bronze Working**.

1. Hold a copper, bronze, brass or silver piece in one hand.
2. Use a lump of **gold amalgam** on a lit fire, brazier or furnace.

The amalgam is rubbed on, and the quicksilver smokes off over the fire. That leaves a thin coat of gold, and the piece becomes **gilded** and worth more. Iron and steel won't take it. A piece can only be gilded once. It also can't be both gilded and electroplated.

Gilding gives off a lot of vapour. Everyone within two tiles breathes it, but much less of it reaches them outdoors.

## Wall mirrors

<img src="../images/Unsilvered_mirror.png"> The glass mirror recipe makes an **unsilvered mirror**. It is plain glass, and you can't see yourself in it yet. To turn it into a mirror:

1. Fasten it to a wall.
2. Use a sheet of **tin** on it.
3. Pour **5 units** of quicksilver over the tin.

## Barometer and thermometer

<img src="../images/Thermometer.png"> A newly made **barometer** (8 iron) is empty. Pour **5 units** of quicksilver into it, then use it in your hand to read the air pressure in hPa, whether it is rising or falling, and the weather coming.

A **thermometer** (1 glass, Optics and Instruments) is also empty when you make it. It takes **2 units** of quicksilver, and when you use it in your hand it tells you how warm the air is, and outdoors what it feels like.

## Felt hats

Carroting needs **Haberdashery**.

1. <img src="../images/Carroted_felt.png"> Pour quicksilver from an open vessel onto a **pelt**. One pelt and **2 units** of quicksilver make **2 sheets of carroted felt**. Each pelt you carrot gives off a little vapour.
2. <img src="../images/Felt_top_hat.png"> Use the felt in your hand and choose a **felt tricorne**, **felt bicorne** or **felt top hat**. Each hat takes 2 sheets.

Felt hats keep off far more rain than cloth hats.
