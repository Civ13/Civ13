# Mercury

Mercury, also called quicksilver, is a liquid metal that you get by roasting cinnabar. It is also a poison. Its fumes build up in your body slowly and take hours to clear, so work it carefully.

## Cinnabar

Cinnabar is a brick-red ore. From the **Classical age** on, mining rock now and then turns up four lumps of it, on top of whatever ore the rock gives. It is about as rare as silver ore. On **Europe, Asia and both Americas** it turns up about twice as often. On the Americas and Asia, the regrowing seams in old mountain floor can also be cinnabar.

Ground up in a mortar, cinnabar is still the red vermilion paint for paintings.

## Roasting cinnabar over a fire

You need **Bronze Working** for this.

1. Hold an open vessel, such as a bowl, a beaker or a mercury flask, in one hand.
2. Hold cinnabar in the other hand.
3. Use the cinnabar on a lit fire, furnace or brazier.

Each lump takes about six seconds. It gives **2 units** of mercury and a cloud of poisonous fumes. The fumes reach everyone within two tiles of the fire, including you. They are only half as bad under the open sky, so roast outdoors.

## The mercury retort

<img src="../images/Mercury_retort.png"> A **mercury retort** (10 iron) needs **Amalgamation**. It is an iron pot over a firebox, with a pipe that runs the fumes into a cooled receiver.

1. Fasten it down with a wrench.
2. Charge it with up to **10 cinnabar**.
3. Use one piece of charcoal, coal or wood on it to fire it.
4. Draw the mercury off the receiver with a flask or any open vessel.

Each lump gives **6 units** of mercury, three times what open roasting gives, and no fumes. The receiver holds 60 units, so draw it off before you load more.

Every firing wears the retort's clay seal. A badly worn seal cracks: the retort then leaks fumes and loses about a third of the mercury. Examine the retort to check the seal, and use one **clay** on it to pack a fresh seal.

<img src="../images/Mercury_flask.png"> A **mercury flask** (2 iron, **Ore Dressing**) holds 60 units of mercury.

## Mercury poisoning

Breathing mercury fumes, drinking from or eating fish from a river fouled with it, and to a lesser degree swallowing liquid mercury, builds up a **mercury burden** in your body. It drains on its own, but slowly. About a tenth of it goes each hour.

| Stage | Signs |
| --- | --- |
| Mild | A metallic taste and headaches. The first time, you are told what is poisoning you. |
| Moderate | A tremor in your hands. Crafting, fine work, medicine and aiming all get slower or worse. |
| Heavy | Bleeding gums, loose teeth, a slipping memory that slows study, and slow kidney damage. |
| Deadly | Brain damage and collapsing. It kills if the exposure keeps going. |

How to stay safe:

- Roast outdoors, or better, use a retort and keep its seal patched.
- Stand back. The fumes reach only two tiles.
- Wear a mask. A cloth mask stops a quarter of the fumes, and a gas mask stops almost all of them.
- If you start to shake, stop working with mercury and let your body clear it.

A doctor's handbook used on a patient shows mercury poisoning and its stage.

**Dimercaprol** draws mercury out of the body much faster. A 5 unit dose clears a large part of the burden. It can only be made from the Modern Medicine age, by mixing glycerol, bromine and sodium sulfide in equal parts.
