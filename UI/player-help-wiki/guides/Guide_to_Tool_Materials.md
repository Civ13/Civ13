# Tool Materials

Most tools can be made from more than one material. A softer material is easier to come by and works more slowly; a better one works faster. You are never locked out of a job for lacking iron, you are only slower at it.

## Reading a material

Examine any tool, weapon or part made of a material to see a **Material** line. It describes the material in plain words, such as how firm, tough and hard-wearing it is and whether it burns, rusts or throws sparks.

## Picks, shovels and hatchets

The head sets how fast the tool works. Heads you cast in moulds or assemble from a head and a handle follow the same order as the ready-made tools:

| Head | Pickaxe | Shovel | Hatchet |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bone | Slowest | Slowest | Slowest |
| Flint or stone | Slow | Slow | Slow |
| Copper | Between stone and iron | Between bone and iron | Between stone and bronze |
| Bronze | A little slower than iron | A little slower than iron | A little slower than iron |
| Iron | The usual speed | The usual speed | The usual speed |
| Steel | Fastest | Fastest | Fastest |

A light handle speeds a pick or shovel up a little and a heavy one slows it down. A steel head on a tool you assemble yourself works at the full steel speed.

The **navvy shovel** is the fastest hand shovel, a broad steel blade on a long handle. It takes 3 steel from the **Tools** menu once your people know Machined Tools, and digs about two thirds faster than an iron shovel.

The **mattock** is a pick-mattock, a broad adze blade and a pick on a long haft. Make it from 3 copper, bronze, iron or steel under **Tools** once your people know **Bronze Working**. It does every job a shovel does and breaks ground faster than a shovel of the same metal, with an iron mattock about a third faster than an iron shovel. It is clumsy with loose earth, though: shovelling up piles, filling tunnels back in and filling roads go at half speed. Carry a shovel as well for that work.

The **wheelbarrow** (8 wood, **Iron Tools**) carries six loads of dirt or sand. Dirt and sand you dig with a shovel go straight into a wheelbarrow you are pulling or standing beside, instead of piling up on the ground. You can also put a dirt pile or some sand in it by hand. Click it with an empty hand to tip one load out onto its tile.

## Who holds the tool

The worker matters as much as the tool. Digging, mining, chopping and tilling all get faster the stronger you are:

| Work | Draws on |
| --- | --- |
| Digging, trenching and moving earth | Mostly strength, some stamina |
| Mining | Mostly strength, a little stamina |
| Felling trees | Mostly strength, some dexterity |
| Tilling and ploughing | Mostly farming, some strength |

An average worker with an iron tool works at the usual speed. Someone twice as strong works about twice as fast. A very weak worker is never slower than half speed. The Navvy, Miner and Woodcutter trades speed up their own work further (see [Character](Guide_to_Character.md#trades)). However good the tool and the worker, a job never takes less than a quarter of the usual time.

## Hand tools in other metals

Hold a stack of the metal, open its crafting menu and look in **Tools**. The tool comes out named for its metal and tinted to match.

| Tool | Usual material | Also made from | Research |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hammer | Wood (5) | Copper, bronze or steel (3, plus a wood handle) | Basic Toolmaking |
| Chisel | Stone, sandstone or iron | Bone (4), copper, bronze or steel (8) | Basic Toolmaking |
| Wrench | Iron (5) | Bronze or steel | Basic Toolmaking |
| Shears | Bronze or iron (2) | Copper or steel | Basic Agriculture |
| Screwdriver | Iron (2) | Bronze or steel | Iron Tools |
| Saw | Iron (3) | Copper, bronze or steel | Iron Tools |
| Crowbar | Iron (4) | Bronze or steel | Iron Tools |
| Wirecutters | Steel (4) | Iron or bronze | Iron Tools |

Compared with the usual tool, copper works at about two thirds of the speed, bronze a little under full speed and steel a little over. You feel it when you saw logs into planks and when you fasten or unfasten something with a wrench.

## Sickle and hoe

Both come with the **Basic Agriculture** research and are made from the stack you hold, under **Tools**.

| Tool | Made from | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sickle | 2 copper, bronze, iron or steel, with 1 wood (a log or a sawn plank) and a hammer to hand | Harvests like a knife and also reaps ripe plants of the same crop beside the one you cut: 2 with copper, 3 with bronze or iron, 4 with steel. |
| Hoe | 2 wood, or 2 copper, bronze, iron or steel with a wood handle | Breaks ground for planting the way a plough does. A wooden hoe works at two thirds of a plough's speed and a metal one about matches it. It is small enough to hang from a belt. |

Examine a sickle to see how many neighbours it reaps.

## Soft tools wear out

A tool softer than what it cuts still works, but badly. You wait about four seconds before it bites, and the tool takes heavy wear each time.

| Work | Tool | Too soft | Cuts cleanly |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Carving rock walls, cave floors and stone walls | Chisel | Bone, copper | Stone, bronze, iron, steel |
| Carving marble or sandstone walls | Chisel | Bone, copper | Stone, bronze, iron, steel |
| Cutting copper, aluminium, silver or gold cable | Wirecutters | - | Bronze, iron, steel |
| Cutting iron cable, barbed wire or chain-link fence | Wirecutters | Bronze | Iron, steel |

- A tool in a metal that lasts less well than its usual one also wears a little on every such job, even when it is hard enough.
- Examine a tool to see whether it shows some wear, is worn or is nearly worn out. A worn-out tool breaks.
- Nothing here applies when you strike on harm intent.

## Hatchet heads and handles

A work hatchet wears its head and its handle separately. Examine it to see the condition of each.

- When the **handle** gives out, the head drops off with the wear it had left. Fit it to a new handle as you made the hatchet, or bind a flint head to a new branch.
- When a **metal head** is worn out, it drops as a hatchet head that needs sharpening on a grindstone before you can fit a new handle. A flint or other brittle head shatters instead.
- Sharpening a head restores it, but each sharpening wears a little off what it can hold.
- Battle axes, hand axes and adzes still wear as one piece.

## Recipes that need a second material

Some builds need a second material as well as the one you are holding. A raft needs 2 rope and a carriage needs 20. The second material can be in your other hand, worn on you or lying at your feet. It is only taken when the build finishes, so a cancelled build costs you nothing. The Crafting window lists what a recipe still needs.

## Machine parts

Gear sets, shafts, bearings, springs, drive belts and wheels are items you make from a material of your choice. Examine a part to see how much work it does and how long it lasts compared with the standard one; steel is the standard for gears, shafts, bearings and springs, and leather for belts.

| Part | By hand | At an anvil, under **Parts** | Cast in a parts mold |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gear set | Wood or bone (4), with a chisel | Bronze, iron or steel (4) | Copper, bronze, brass, iron or steel |
| Shaft | Wood (2), with a hatchet | Bronze, iron or steel (2) | Copper, bronze, brass, iron or steel |
| Bearing | Wood (2) and 1 animal fat, with a chisel | Bronze, iron or steel (2), as a bushing | Copper, bronze, brass, iron or steel |
| Spring | - | Bronze, iron or steel (2) | - |
| Drive belt | Rope (3), leather (2) or cloth (3), with a knife | Bronze, iron or steel (3), as a drive chain | - |
| Wheel | Wood (3), with a hatchet and a chisel | Bronze, iron or steel (4) | Copper, bronze, brass, iron or steel |

- **By hand:** hold the stack, open its crafting menu and look in **Machine parts**. The tool can be held, worn or at your feet.
- **At an anvil:** use bronze, iron or steel on the anvil and pick **Parts**.
- **Cast:** shape an **unfired parts mold** from 3 clay (in **Molds**, with Bronze Working), fire it like any other mold, fill it from a blacksmith pot and use it on an anvil. You pick the shape there. Brass now pours into every mold at the same cost as bronze.

Wooden parts are the cheapest and the weakest: a wooden gear set does about two fifths of the work of a steel one and wears out five times as fast. Bronze and brass come close to iron. Metals that are too soft, such as lead, tin and gold, cannot be made into gears, shafts or bearings at all. Carving parts by hand needs Basic Toolmaking; springs need Bronze Working.

An **iron-shod wheel** is a wooden wheel with an iron tyre: hold 2 iron with a wheel and a hammer to hand, and pick it in **Machine parts**. It lasts twice as long as the bare wooden wheel.

Wheels go into bicycles. A **pedal bicycle** needs 8 steel or aluminium, two wheels and a drive belt or chain, fitted with a wrench; an aluminium one is about 15% quicker. A **running bike** needs 6 wood and two wheels and comes with the Carts research: it has no pedals or chain and is slower than a bicycle. See [Pedal bicycles](Guide_to_Crafting.md#pedal-bicycles).

## Lathes

A part you carve, forge or cast is **rough**. A lathe makes **precise** parts; examine a part to see which it is.

| Lathe | Research | Built from | Cuts | You also need |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pole lathe | Workshops | 8 wood and 2 rope | Wood, bone and brass | A chisel held, worn or at your feet |
| Engine lathe | Machined Tools | 12 iron, plus a gear set, a shaft and 2 bearings of any material, with a wrench | Copper, bronze, brass, iron and steel | Nothing; it works twice as fast |

Both are in the **Economy & production** crafting menu of their main material and are fastened down with a wrench like other workshop machines. The parts that go into an engine lathe can be rough, so you never need a lathe to build your first one.

- **Turn a new part:** use a stack of the material on the lathe and pick a gear set (4), shaft (2) or bearing (2).
- **True a rough part:** use a rough gear set, shaft or bearing on the lathe. It keeps its material and becomes precise.

Springs, drive belts and chains are never turned.

## Parts inside machines

The hand mill, the large mill, the large and powered saw mills, the loom, the printing press and the wind and water generators have working parts that wear out. Examine one to see its parts, their condition and how fast the machine is running.

| Machine | Parts | Built with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mill | Shaft, bearing | Wood |
| Large mill | Gear set, shaft, bearing | Wood |
| Large saw mill | Gear set, shaft, bearing | Wood |
| Powered saw mill | Gear set, shaft, bearing | Iron |
| Loom | Shaft, bearing | Wood |
| Printing press | Shaft (the press screw) | Wood |
| Waterwheel generator | Shaft, bearing | Wood |
| Wind charger | Gear set, shaft, bearing | Iron |
| Wind turbine | Gear set, shaft, bearing | Steel |

- A new machine runs at its usual speed. Each finished job wears its parts a little; the wooden parts a machine is built with last about 400 jobs.
- A **worn** part makes the machine a quarter slower. A **failing** part sometimes jams a job before it starts. A **worn out** part stops the machine.
- To replace a part, use a gear set, shaft or bearing on the machine. A part you fitted earlier comes back out; the original part is scrap.
- The machine works as fast as its weakest part. Bronze, iron or steel parts in a wooden machine make it faster, up to a quarter faster than usual, and last several times longer.
- The powered saw mill, the wind charger and the wind turbine need accurate parts. A rough part fitted to one runs at 60% at best, so true your parts on a lathe first.
- A generator's power follows its parts in the same way: better parts give more power and a worn out part gives none. Generators never jam, and a standard set lasts about 110 hours of running.
- Taking a machine apart with a hammer gives back the parts you fitted, with their wear. The original parts and worn out parts are lost.

## Pipes

Pipe can be made of bamboo, logs, lead, copper, iron or steel. Tougher material carries more of a pump's flow, a network runs at its weakest pipe, and lead pipe slowly poisons the water. See [Factory Automation](Guide_to_Factory_Automation.md#pipes-of-other-materials-and-plumbing-before-electricity) for the table and the hand pump, wooden intake, cistern and water trough.

## Cannon barrels

With the **Cannon** research you can make the cannon from more than steel. Hold the stack, open its crafting menu and look in **Siege Weapons**.

| Cannon | Made from | Barrel lasts | How it ends |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cannon (steel) | 35 steel | No limit | It does not wear. |
| Iron cannon | 35 iron | About 204 shots | No warning. Once it has fired about half of that, any shot has a small chance to burst it. |
| Bronze cannon | 35 bronze | About 101 shots | The barrel starts to bulge at three quarters, splits when spent, and bursts on the shot after. |
| Iron-hooped wooden cannon | 12 wood, 4 iron and a hammer | About 5 shots | The same warnings as bronze. |

- Examine a cannon to read its barrel, how many shots it is good for and its condition.
- A bursting barrel explodes where the cannon stands and destroys it. Stop firing a bulged barrel and stand clear of an old iron one.
- Artillery and tank guns always have steel barrels.

## Sparks

Iron, steel and flint throw sparks. If you strike a barrel that holds gunpowder or fuel with a tool made of one of them while on **Harm intent**, there is a small chance each blow sets it off. Tools of bronze, copper, wood and bone never spark, so keep a bronze or copper set for work around powder and fuel. Examine a barrel to see whether its contents are dangerous. Filling, pouring and carrying a barrel are always safe.
