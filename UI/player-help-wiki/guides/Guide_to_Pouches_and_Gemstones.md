# Pouches, Belts & Gemstones

Small pouches keep herbs, stones, ore and coins close at hand, and belts carry them. Lanyards and wrist loops keep small tools from getting lost, and fire kits, sheaths, needle cases and hones you wear keep tools ready to use. Gemstones turn up while mining. Once cut, they are valuable to trade and can be set into clothing, jewellery and necklaces.

## Pouches

| Pouch | How to make it | Needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leather belt pouch | 1 leather, **Storage** tab | Basic Toolmaking |
| Cloth drawstring pouch | 1 cloth | Weaving |

Each pouch has six slots for small things, such as herbs, seeds, ore, gems and coins. It can't hold bags or other pouches.

You can carry a pouch in four ways:

- **In the belt slot or a pocket.** Click it to open it.
- **In the hip slot.** This is the slot beside your clothing that also takes a lantern, canteen or keys. You need to be wearing clothes. Your belt slot stays free.
- **On your clothes.** Use the pouch on a worn garment to hang it there, and click the garment to open it. One garment takes two pouches at most.
- **On a leather belt.** See the next section.

When two pouches hang from the same belt or garment, the second one sits on your other hip.

## Belts

| Belt | How to make it | Needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leather belt | 1 leather, **Storage** tab | Basic Toolmaking |
| Shoulder belt | 1 leather, **Storage** tab | Basic Toolmaking |
| Tool loop | 1 leather, **Storage** tab | Basic Toolmaking |

- **Leather belt.** You wear it in the belt slot, and it stays on even if you take your clothes off. Use a pouch on it to hang the pouch there. Other people can see the pouches on you. To take something off the belt, drag its tile from the row described below onto a hand or the floor, or use **Remove Accessory** on the belt.
- **Shoulder belt.** This strap goes across your body from shoulder to hip. Use it on your clothes to put it on. It doesn't use the belt slot, so you can wear it together with a leather belt. It holds two pouches, or one sword and one pouch.
- **Tool loop.** A leather loop that hangs from a leather belt and holds one thing made to be worn on a belt, such as a hammer. It can't hold bags, clothing or guns.

### What a leather belt carries

A leather belt has four places:

- two pouches;
- one sword sheath, knife sheath or holster;
- one tool loop.

A sheath or holster on your belt leaves your clothes free for a harness or chest webbing, which only go on clothing.

### Opening one attachment

When a worn belt or garment carries one attachment, clicking it opens that attachment. When it carries several, clicking it shows a row of tiles above your bag window, one tile for each attachment. Point at a tile to see the attachment's name. Click a tile with an empty hand to open or use that attachment (a pouch, sheath or holster) or to take anything else, such as a necklace, off into your hand. Click a tile with an item in your hand to put the item in it. Click the belt or garment again to put the tiles away.

To look at an attachment, **shift-click** its tile. To take one off, drag its tile onto a hand or onto the floor next to you, or right-click the tile and choose **Take Off**. Right-click also offers **Examine Attachment**.

If you click the belt or garment with an item in your hand instead, the item goes into the first attachment that can take it.

## Lanyards and wrist loops

A lanyard or wrist loop keeps one small item from being lost. Each kind only takes the things it was made for.

| Item | How to make it | Needs | Takes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wrist thong | 1 leather | Basic Toolmaking | hatchet, machete, knife, club, mace or hammer |
| Sword knot | 1 leather | Steel Blades | a one-handed sword |
| Neck cord | 1 rope | Weaving | knife, whistle or key |
| Fob chain | 1 brass | Optics & Instruments | compass or key |
| Pistol lanyard | 1 rope | Cartridge Firearms | revolver, pistol, whistle, compass or knife |
| Retention lanyard | 1 plastic | Synthetic Fabrics | pistol, knife, compass, key, whistle or small tool |

Long guns, bows, spears, flintlock pistols and two-handed weapons can't be tied to any of these.

### Wrist loops

1. Hold the tool or sword in one hand and the wrist thong or sword knot in the other.
2. Use the thong or knot. It is bound to the tool for good.
3. With the tool in your hand, use **Wrist Loop** (IC tab) to slip your hand through the loop. Use it again to slip your hand out.

While your hand is through the loop, a tool that slips from your grip or is knocked away swings from your wrist and you grip it again after a moment. To put the tool down, slip your hand out of the loop first.

### Lanyards

1. Put the lanyard on a worn garment, the same way as a pouch. The fob chain and retention lanyard also hang from a leather belt.
2. Put the item on the lanyard. It is now tied to it.
3. Click the lanyard to take the item out. It stays tied.

If you drop a tied item, or it is knocked out of your hand, it falls back onto the lanyard instead of the ground. To set it down for good, use **Untie Lanyard** (IC tab) with the item in your hand. Putting it in a bag or handing it to someone also unties it. Each garment takes one lanyard.

You can't throw a tied item. Untie it, or slip your hand out of the loop, first.

## Tools you wear

People have always carried their everyday tools on their bodies: a fire kit at the belt, a knife in the boot, a needle case on a cord, a whetstone around the neck. A tool you wear counts as **at hand**. The tool doesn't need to be in your other hand when an action calls for it, as long as it is:

- in a pouch or loop on your leather belt, or in your belt slot;
- in a pouch in your hip slot;
- hanging from your clothes, coat or shoes, or carried on a chatelaine.

Tools in a backpack or a pocket don't count, because you have to unpack them first.

### Fire kits

| Kit | How to make it | Needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fire pouch | 1 leather, **Storage** tab | Basic Toolmaking |
| Fire drill | 1 wood, **Tools** tab | Basic Toolmaking |
| Strike-a-light pouch | 1 iron | Iron Tools |

- **Fire pouch.** A small pouch packed with tinder fungus and pyrite, like the one the Iceman carried 5,000 years ago. It holds flint, a fire drill, a fire striker and small flint tools. Keep a flint inside, then hold a candle, pipe or cigarette against the pouch you are wearing to light it. Each strike takes a second and usually catches. With a fire drill inside instead of a flint, it takes about four seconds and always works.
- **Fire drill.** A wooden spindle and hearth board. Use it in your hand and spin it for about four seconds to raise an ember. The ember lasts five seconds and lights a candle or pipe.
- **Strike-a-light pouch.** A fire pouch with a steel striker riveted along its base. It catches more often than the plain fire pouch.

Both pouches count toward the two pouches a belt or garment can hold. Campfires, ovens and torches still light by hand.

### Knife sheaths

| Sheath | How to make it | Worn | Needs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Knife sheath | 1 leather, **Weapon Accessories** tab | on a belt or clothes | Basic Toolmaking |
| Neck sheath | 1 leather, **Storage** tab | on clothes, at the chest | Basic Toolmaking |
| Boot sheath | 1 leather, **Weapon Accessories** tab | sewn into shoes or boots | Iron Tools |
| Sleeve sheath | 1 leather, **Weapon Accessories** tab | sewn into a coat sleeve | Iron Tools |

The neck, boot and sleeve sheaths hold one **small** knife, such as a flint, obsidian, bone, copper or iron knife. Sickles and butcher, meat, bread, bowie and hook knives are too big. A sleeve sheath can hold the bone awl instead.

Use a boot sheath on your shoes, or a sleeve sheath on your coat, to sew it in. Each shoe or coat takes one. To draw the knife, click the worn shoes or coat with an empty hand. The neck sheath works the same way when it is the only thing on your clothes; otherwise use its tile. Boot and sleeve sheaths are hidden from view, but anyone who examines your shoes or coat can see the sheath and what is in it.

### Sewing and sharpening

| Item | How to make it | Needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Needle case | 1 bone | Basic Toolmaking |
| Pendant hone | 1 stone | Bronze Working |
| Chatelaine | 2 iron | Iron Tools |

- **Needle case.** A bone tube you wear on your clothes. It holds the bone needle and some sinew. When you mend clothing with the bone needle, it uses sinew from the case when your other hand holds no sinew or leather. It never takes leather from your other pouches.
- **Pendant hone.** A small whetstone on a cord. Use a chipped blade on the hone, or on the clothes it hangs from, to sharpen it just as you would on a flint.
- **Chatelaine.** A clasp with three short chains, worn on your clothes. It holds a small knife, keys, a needle case, a pendant hone or shears. Anything hanging from it counts as at hand.

Using the bone needle on a damaged garment mends it, and using a chipped blade on your worn clothes hones it if you wear a hone. In both cases the tool stays in your hand instead of going into a pouch.

## Gemstones

### Finding them

Every underground rock wall you mine has a small chance to give a **rough gemstone**, on top of any ore. Special caves give two. You might find:

- garnets and amethysts, which are the most common;
- topazes;
- emeralds and sapphires;
- rubies, which are the rarest.

Rough diamonds still come from the ordinary ore finds.

### Cutting

To cut a gemstone you need **Regalia & Heraldry**.

1. Hold a **chisel** in your active hand and a **hammer** in your other hand.
2. Use the chisel on a rough stone that is next to you or in your bag.

Each cut takes a few seconds and makes one cut stone, which drops on that tile. Sometimes a stone has a hidden flaw and shatters. Skilled crafters lose fewer stones this way.

Cut stones are worth much more than rough ones. Diamonds and rubies are worth the most, then emeralds and sapphires, then topazes, garnets and amethysts.

### Setting a stone

1. Hold a cut stone in your active hand and a chisel in your other hand.
2. Use the stone on a piece of clothing, such as a ring, crown, belt or garment.

The stone is set into the item, which raises its value and shows when the item is examined. Each item takes one stone.

A gold or silver crown works a little differently: use a cut diamond on it, with or without a chisel, and it becomes an **inlaid crown** that protects a little better. A rough diamond has to be cut first.

### Taking a stone out

Use a chisel on an item with a set stone. After a few seconds the stone comes out whole and goes into your hand, and the item loses the value it added. You can then set the stone into something else.

## Practical uses for stones

Some stones are useful as well as pretty. Most of these jobs use the same tools as gem cutting: a **chisel** in your active hand and a **hammer** in the other.

### Jewel bearings

*Needs Optics & Instruments.*

- **Make them:** use the chisel and hammer on a cut ruby or sapphire for 4 jewel bearings, or on a cut garnet for 2 cheaper ones.
- **Fit them:** use 2 bearings on a pocket watch, wrist watch or compass.

A plain watch runs unevenly and only tells the time to about the quarter hour. A jewelled watch reads to the minute. A jewelled compass gives a closer position and reads faster.

### Diamond grit and the lapping wheel

- **Diamond grit:** use a hammer on a rough diamond to crush it into 3 pinches of grit (needs Regalia & Heraldry). A diamond that splits while being cut leaves a pinch of grit.
- **Lapping wheel:** made from 4 iron (needs Workshops). Rub diamond grit into it to charge it. When you cut a stone while standing next to a charged wheel, the cut takes half the time and the stone never splits.
- **Emery:** a ruby or sapphire that splits leaves emery. Emery also charges the wheel, but it only works for stones softer than ruby: garnet, amethyst, topaz, emerald and quartz.
- **Diamond drill bit:** use a steel sheet on a wheel with plenty of grit (needs Machined Tools). Fitted to an industrial drill, it brings up an extra ore each cycle.

### Quartz and radio crystals

You can find rough quartz while mining. Cut it like any other stone.

- **Reading stone** (needs Glassworking): use the chisel and hammer on cut quartz. Hold the stone in either hand and you can read and write in dimmer light.
- **Crystal radio set** (needs Electricity): use the chisel and hammer on lead ore to split out a galena crystal. Then use the crystal while holding 10 cable in your other hand. The set listens to the radio without any power, but it can't transmit.
- **Quartz crystal unit** (needs Assembly Line): use cut quartz while holding a tin sheet in your other hand.
  - Fit it to a portable radio to give the radio a second frequency that it always listens to.
  - Fit it to a wrist watch (needs Digital Computing) to make a quartz watch that keeps exact time.

### Garnet paper

*Needs Factories.*

- **Make it:** use a rough garnet while holding paper in your other hand. This makes 4 sheets.
- **Use it:** use a sheet while holding a worn wooden tool or weapon in your other hand, and it is sanded smooth again.

### Synthetic ruby

1. **Grow a boule** (needs Electricity): use 4 pinches of emery while standing next to a lit furnace.
2. **Make jewel bearings:** use the chisel and hammer on a boule for 10 cheap bearings.
3. **Make a ruby rod** (needs Consumer Electronics): use a boule on a lapping wheel charged with diamond.
4. **Make a laser rangefinder:** use the ruby rod on field binoculars.

A synthetic boule is not a gemstone and can't be traded or set as one.
