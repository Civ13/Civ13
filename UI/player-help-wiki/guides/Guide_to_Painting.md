# Painting

You can paint your own pictures: small miniatures to wear in a locket, pictures for a table or shelf, and paintings to hang on a wall. You paint them pixel by pixel, using colours ground from earth, charcoal and bone.

## Pigments

Grind one of these in a **mortar and pestle**. Hold the item and click the mortar with it.

| Pigment | Ground from | Colours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Charcoal pigment | charcoal | black, dark grey |
| Red ochre | iron ore | deep red, bright red |
| Yellow ochre | clay | ochre, pale yellow |
| Bone white | bone | white, warm grey |

Later ages add brighter colours, also ground in the mortar:

| Pigment | Ground from | Colours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Malachite green | copper ore | deep green, light green |
| Vermilion | mercury ore | scarlet, orange-red |
| Lead white | lead ore | bright white, pale grey |
| Ultramarine | rough sapphire | deep blue, light blue |

With **Fine Arts** you can make a **box of oil paints** from tin. It gives eight colours (black, white, red, yellow, blue, green, brown and orange) and has 40 uses.

You can only paint with the pigments you are carrying, in your hands, pockets or bag. Any two of your colours can also be mixed half and half. When you finish a painting, each pigment you used gets a little smaller: one use for every 256 pixels painted with it. A fresh pigment has 10 uses.

## Canvases and the easel

All of these are made from wood on the **Paintings** tab. The panels, wall canvas and easel need Basic Toolmaking, and the gallery canvases need Fine Arts.

| Item | Painted at | How you paint it | What it becomes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Miniature panel | 16x16 | in your hands | a locket for a necklace |
| Table picture panel | 32x32 | in your hands | a small standing picture |
| Wall canvas | 64x64 | on an easel | a painting for a wall |
| Gallery canvas | 128x128 | on an easel | a large framed painting, 2x2 tiles |
| Wide gallery canvas | 128x64 | on an easel | a wide framed painting, 2 tiles across |
| Easel | - | - | holds a wall or gallery canvas while you paint |

To paint a wall canvas, put it on the easel by clicking the easel with it. Then click the easel with an empty hand and choose **Paint**. For a miniature or a table picture, use it in your hand.

## The painting window

- **Brush** paints one pixel, or a 2x2 or 3x3 block.
- **Fill** colours every connected pixel that has the same colour.
- **Pick** takes a colour from the painting.
- **Undo** steps back one stroke, and **Grid** shows or hides the grid lines.
- The top row shows your colours. The smaller swatches below it are the mixes.
- One painting can use at most 16 different colours, counting the canvas colour.

**Save** keeps your work so you can come back to it later. **Finish** asks for a title and locks the painting for good, so it can't be changed afterwards.

## Rock art

You can paint straight onto natural rock, such as a cave wall or a cliff, with the same pigments and no canvas or easel. Stand right in front of the rock, facing it squarely, and use a pigment on it. The painting window opens. Anything left in the base colour stays bare stone, so only your paint shows.

Only someone standing on that spot can paint there. Use a pigment on the rock again to carry on with an unfinished painting. A finished rock painting can be scraped off with a chisel or hammer. Finishing one is a deed you can carve into your tribe's totem pole (see [Tribal Life](Guide_to_Tribal_Life.md)).

## Showing your work

- **Locket.** Click a necklace with a finished miniature to set it in as a locket. A necklace can hold either a gemstone or a locket, not both. When you wear it, the picture shows on your chest. Use **Take Out Miniature** to remove it.
- **Table picture.** A finished one stands in its own frame. Put it on a table or shelf like any other item.
- **Wall painting.** Stand in front of a wall and click the wall with a finished wall painting to hang it. Use a hammer on it to take it down again.
- **Gallery painting.** These hang on a wall to your north, in a gilded frame two tiles wide. Stand on the left tile and click the wall. The wall must carry on to your right. Pigment goes further on these big canvases, so even a gallery painting covered in one colour uses only 8 uses of it.

The picture you see in the world is a smaller copy. Examine a painting, or click a hanging one, to see it at full size with its title and painter.

## Drawings on paper and in books

You can also draw a picture on a sheet of paper or in a book.

1. Hold the paper or book, and hold a pen, pencil, crayon or pigment in your active hand.
2. Use **Draw Picture** (Object tab).
3. Choose a **Small sketch** (32x32) or a **Large drawing** (64x64). The same painting window opens.

You can draw with your pen's ink, any pigments you carry, and their mixes. A drawing uses no pigment. **Finish** asks for a title and puts the drawing on the page.

- A sheet of paper holds 2 drawings and a book holds 12.
- Drawings show under the writing when you read a page. A book opens its pictures in a window beside it.
- The printing press and the mail copy a page together with its drawings. Monks copy them along with the book.
- When you bind a page into a book, its drawings go into the book too. If the book has no room left for them, the page can't be bound.

## Design codes

A design code is a picture written out as text, starting with `C13ART/`. You can keep it anywhere outside the game and paste it back in later, on any land.

- **Export** in the painting window puts the current picture's code in the box below it. Press Ctrl+C to copy it.
- **Import** paints a pasted code onto the canvas in front of you. A code of a different size is fitted to the canvas. Each colour is changed to the nearest colour you can paint with right now, so carry the right pigments first. **Undo** puts the canvas back.
- The painter of a finished painting or drawing can open its code with **Design code** under the full-size picture. Nobody else sees it.

Importing doesn't skip anything: you still need the canvas, the pigments are still used when you finish, and finished paintings still count toward the land's limit.

## Good to know

- Each land has room for about 300 finished wall and gallery paintings. Small paintings count as a quarter of one.
- Every finished painting and drawing records who made it. Offensive pictures will be removed by staff.
