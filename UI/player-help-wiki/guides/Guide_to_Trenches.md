# Trenches & Fortifications

A trench is a knee-deep ditch that protects the people inside it from distant gunfire. This page covers digging, using and removing trenches, and how they fit with the barricades described in [Construction](Guide_to_Construction.md#fences-barricades-and-barriers).

## Digging a trench

You need a **shovel**: a bone shovel, a shovel with a cast head, or a **trench shovel** made from 2 iron in the Tools section. In research modes your people must have researched **Field Defenses** first; the game tells you if they have not.

1. Stand next to a **dirt** or **sand** tile. On grass or snow, first click the tile with the shovel to remove the grass or snow layer.
2. Switch to **Harm intent** and click the tile with the shovel.
3. Click it again. The first dig leaves a hollow, and the second turns the tile into a trench.

Dirt is quicker to dig than sand. Line up several trench tiles to make a continuous position you can move along.

## Using a trench

- **Getting in** takes half a second. **Climbing out** takes two seconds, so plan your exits before the shooting starts.
- A teammate can **pull you out** of a trench, or help you in, by pulling you across the edge.
- Small animals move in and out freely.
- Lay **wooden planks** across a trench to make a bridge that people can walk over.

### Protection

Trenches change how bullets reach you:

| Your position | Shooter more than three tiles away | Shooter within three tiles |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standing in a trench | Can only hit your **head**, and often misses. | Normal chance to hit. |
| Prone in a trench | Cannot hit you. | Normal chance to hit. |

The catch: if you fire from a trench while **prone**, your bullet hits the trench wall. Stand up to shoot over the edge, then go prone again to take cover. Grenades and close-range attackers are still dangerous, so cover the ends of your trench.

## Water in trenches

On Nomads, Civilizations and colony maps, a trench dug next to water, or next to another flooded trench, floods, as long as that water stands at least as high as the trench's own ground. Water doesn't run uphill, so a canal can't climb a hill by itself. A flooded trench:

- can be drunk from, or used to fill containers, though saltwater is not safe to drink;
- lets boats travel through it, so you can dig a canal from the sea.

A dry trench beside an [aqueduct](Guide_to_Floods.md#aqueducts) channel also floods, if the channel's water is at least as high, so you can feed a canal from higher ground.

### Pound locks

Where the ground steps up or down, a canal's water sits at two levels and no boat can cross the step. Build a **pound lock** (12 stone, Construction, needs **Wagons & Ships**) on the flooded canal tile at the step. The chamber stands at the lower or the upper level, and boats move between it and the side it matches. Click the lock and wait ten seconds to fill or empty it to the other level. Examine it to see which side it is open to. Several locks in a row climb several steps.

For crops, use **irrigation channels** instead. They carry water without blocking movement. See [Farming](Guide_to_Farming.md#watering).

## Filling in a trench

Use a shovel on **dirt** with any intent other than Harm to shovel a **dirt pile**. Use a dirt pile on a trench and confirm to start filling it. Two piles fill a trench tile back to level ground. Sandbags cannot be used for this.

## Pits and climbing

A tunnel shaft you dig with a shovel is an open **pit** with no ladder down. Click it to climb down carefully. Anyone who walks onto it falls one floor, takes some damage and is stunned for a moment.

- **Hide a pit:** use **five leaves** on it and wait three seconds. A covered pit looks like ordinary ground until someone falls in, which tears the cover away.
- **Pulling:** a pull does not carry anyone across a pit. Whoever steps on it falls and the pull is let go.
- **Climbing up:** to get out of a pit or onto a higher floor, face a wall or the rim of a hole with a free hand and use **IC > Climb Up**, or simply click that wall or rim with **grab intent** and an empty hand. After three seconds you climb onto the ledge one floor up, if there is solid, clear ground there to stand on.
- **Ladders:** craft a **wooden ladder** from **4 wood logs** (Tools, needs **Basic Toolmaking**) and use it on a pit to fit it, if there is clear floor at the bottom. Nobody falls into a pit with a ladder. Click the pit to climb down, and click the ladder at the bottom to climb back up. Use grab intent on the pit to pull the ladder out again. A pit with a ladder can't be hidden with leaves.

## Building a defensive line

- Dig the trench first, then build **sandbag** or **dirt barricades** along the side that faces the enemy. Barricades reduce your chance of being hit but can be dismantled. On the [tech tree](Guide_to_Research.md), empty sandbags unlock with **Fortifications**.
- String **barbed wire** in front of the line to slow attackers. Wire cutters remove it.
- Leave a covered route back to your own lines so the wounded can be moved out.

See [Construction](Guide_to_Construction.md#fences-barricades-and-barriers) for barricade recipes, and [Combat](Guide_to_Combat.md#cover) for using cover in a fight.

## Sieges and castles

For how to lay out and hold a castle with these pieces, see [Defending a Castle](Guide_to_Castles.md).

Once your people know **Siege Engines**, you can build a battering ram, a ballista and a siege tower. **Fortifications** adds the trebuchet and the castle gate pieces. Everything here is built from wood except the murder hole.

- **Battering ram** (30 wood). Push it up against a gate, door, barricade or built wall, face it there and swing it. Each swing takes three seconds and hits harder for every person standing beside the ram, up to four. A full crew breaks a gate in three swings.
- **Ballista** (25 wood) and **ballista bolts** (2 wood). Fasten it with a hammer, load a bolt and loose it. The bolt flies straight ahead for up to 24 tiles and can pass through two people.
- **Siege tower** (60 wood). Push it against a wall and face it there, then lower its drop bridge. Anyone standing beside the tower can climb it and land on the first clear tile beyond the wall. The tower blocks sight, so the defenders can't see who is gathering behind it. Moving or turning it raises the bridge again.
- **Trebuchet** (80 wood). A bigger catapult: it throws from 30 to 140 tiles and hits harder, but takes longer to wind.
- **Portcullis** (12 wood) and **drawbridge** (15 wood). Heavy castle gates that can't be opened by hand. Build a **gate winch** (8 wood) beside them; working it raises or drops every portcullis and drawbridge within two tiles. Portcullis sections built side by side (or in a column) join into one wide gate, and the winch works the whole run if it reaches any section of it. A wider gate takes longer to wind, and a second person with a free hand next to the winch halves the time. A shut portcullis stops people but not arrows, and a raised drawbridge also blocks sight. For a moat, dig a ditch in front of the gate, let the river flood it and span it with the drawbridge.
- **Murder hole** (3 stone). An opening in a floor: you can see the floor below through it. Pour a container through it, or drop anything else you hold, onto whoever stands underneath (on a single floor, onto the tile in front of it).
- **Arched iron gates.** A gate winch within two tiles of any section of a continuous straight run works the whole run. A winch operating only arched iron gates opens a mixed open/closed run first; its next use closes it. The gates’ ordinary controls still work, and they do not crush like a dropping portcullis.

- **Machicolation** (8 stone) and **hoarding** (12 wood, from Siege Engines). Build one on the walkway on top of your wall, facing out. Whatever you pour or drop through it lands at the foot of the wall below. A hoarding is timber and can be set on fire.

**Arrow slits and gun loops.** Once you know **Fortifications**, stand right against the inside of a built stone, clay or timber wall, square on, and use a chisel on it with help intent (a hatchet also works on timber). After 15 seconds the wall has an arrow slit, opening away from you; with **Gunpowder** you can cut a gun loop instead. The wall stays solid and you can see through it, but it loses a fifth of its strength. Stand on the tile right behind the slit to shoot out of it, straight ahead or at an angle. Arrows and bullets from outside nearly always hit the stone, though a lucky shot can still reach whoever stands behind it. A piece of the wall's own material fills the slit in again. Straw walls, paper screens and archways can't take a slit. To close a slit, use two planks on it from the tile behind it to fit a wooden shutter. After that, click the wall from that tile with an empty hand to shut or open it. While the shutter is shut, nobody can see through the slit and no shot passes either way.

**Battlements.** A crenelated wall you build protects the side you were facing while you built it, so face outward. Defenders behind it shoot out over it freely, most shots from outside are stopped, and someone lying down behind it can't be hit at all.

**Parapets.** A **parapet** (4 stone, or 4 sandstone for a sandstone parapet, from Fortifications) is the battlement for the walkway on top of your wall. Stand on the walkway, face out and build it: it runs along the outer edge of the tile you stand on, so a walkway one tile wide keeps its room to walk. It covers whoever stands on that tile the same way a battlement does, and nobody can walk or climb over it, so nobody steps off the wall by accident.

**Pouring from above.** Heat a container first: hold it over a lit brazier, hearth, campfire or stove on grab intent for a few seconds. It stays hot for about two minutes. Boiling water scalds, hot oil burns badly and leaves a slick, and hot pitch sticks and sets people alight. Hot sand gets under armour, and molten lead is the worst of all. Quicklime needs no heating; it burns eyes and blinds. Sand, quicklime and lead sheets go into a container a piece at a time; melt lead before you pour it. A big pot (60 units or more) splashes the tiles around the target too. Oil and pitch slicks burst into flame when a torch or a fire arrow reaches them. Pitch comes from a **tar kiln** (6 stone): load it with ten pieces of wood, light it with a torch, and two minutes later draw off the pitch.

**Dropping things.** Anything dropped through a murder hole or off a ledge falls on whoever is below: stones and logs hurt badly, and a helmet helps.

**Gateway traps.**

- **Drop trapdoor** (6 wood) and **trap lever** (3 wood), from Fortifications. Fit the trapdoor over an open pit (dig one with a pit cover and a shovel, and stake it for worse), or over a hole in an upper floor. While it is shut, people walk over it safely. Pulling a lever within three tiles drops it, and the lever also works from the floor above. Everyone standing on it falls into the pit or down to the level below, and anyone who walks onto it while it is open falls too. Push the lever back to shut it.
- **Portcullis.** A portcullis dropped on someone hurts them badly and knocks them down.
- **Caltrops** (1 iron, from Bronze Working). Use them in your hand to scatter them where you stand, or throw them. Stepping on them hurts your foot and slows you, and boots halve the wound. A horse that treads on them goes lame for half a minute and may throw its rider. Gathering them up again takes a moment.
- **Ladder fork** (3 wood, from Siege Engines). From the top of the wall, use it on an enemy siege ladder to push the ladder off in three seconds, and whoever is climbing it falls. A spear, pike or halberd works too.
- **Pit traps.** Anyone can dig one, not only tribes. Your own people, or your own side on a team map, never fall into your pits.

**Undermining.** Dig a tunnel under an enemy wall, gate or barricade on the floor above, and it collapses into your tunnel about 30 seconds later. A **mine support** beside the tunnel holds it up, and pulling out or burning that prop brings it down at once. Defenders can dig their own tunnels to find and stop the miners.
