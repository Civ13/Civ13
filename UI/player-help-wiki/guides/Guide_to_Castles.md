# Defending a Castle

This page walks through laying out and holding a castle. The tools themselves are described in [Trenches & Fortifications](Guide_to_Trenches.md#sieges-and-castles), which lists their costs and exact behaviour.

## What your people need to know

- **Stone Masonry**: the tar kiln, which makes pitch.
- **Field Defenses**: pit covers and punji sticks.
- **Bronze Working**: caltrops.
- **Siege Engines**: hoardings, siege ladders and the ladder fork.
- **Fortifications**: battlements, arrow slits, murder holes, machicolations, the portcullis, drawbridge and gate winch, and the drop trapdoor and its lever.
- **Gunpowder**: gun loops.

## The walls

1. **Build the curtain wall of stone.** Timber burns and straw does nothing. A stone, clay or timber wall can take an arrow slit; stand inside it, square on, and cut the slit with a chisel. Cut slits where the approach is open, a few tiles apart, and keep one archer per slit: only the person right behind it shoots out.
2. **Crown the wall with parapets.** Put a floor over the wall so you can walk on top of it. Then, standing on that walkway and facing out, build parapets along its outer edge. Defenders behind them shoot over freely, and lying down makes them safe from arrows. On a wider wall top, full crenelated walls (battlements) do the same job.
3. **Hang the wall with machicolations** (or cheaper hoardings early on) facing out over the foot of the wall. That is where attackers stand to set ladders and rams.

## The gateway

The gate is where the attack comes, so make it a killing ground:

1. **Moat and drawbridge.** Dig a ditch in front of the gate, flood it, and span it with a drawbridge on a winch.
2. **Caltrops** scattered on the approach lame horses and slow people on foot.
3. **Outer portcullis.** Drop it on whoever is under it: it crushes and pins them.
4. **The passage.** Fit a drop trapdoor over a staked pit, with its lever on the floor above or behind the inner gate. Put a murder hole in the floor above the passage.
5. **Inner portcullis.** Let the attackers break the first gate, then drop the outer portcullis behind them, pull the trapdoor and pour from above.

## During the siege

- **Keep a fire going on the wall walk.** A brazier works well. Pots of water, oil or pitch heated over it stay hot for about two minutes, so heat them just before you need them. Stock stones beside each murder hole and machicolation to drop.
- **Fire.** Oil and pitch slicks below the wall burn when a torch or fire arrow reaches them. Remember that the enemy's fire arrows can do the same to your hoardings, so keep water by them.
- **Ladders.** Keep a ladder fork, spear or pike on every stretch of wall, and push ladders off as soon as they go up.
- **Rams and towers** have to come right up to the wall or gate: that is where the machicolations, murder holes and the archers at the slits do their work.
- **Mines.** Dig your own counter-mines along the wall to find enemy miners before their tunnel brings it down.

## Attacking a castle

Siege towers, rams, trebuchets and mines are covered in [Trenches & Fortifications](Guide_to_Trenches.md#sieges-and-castles). Armour halves most hot liquids (not hot sand), and a helmet takes much of a dropped stone. Ride around caltrops rather than through them, and spread out at the gate.
