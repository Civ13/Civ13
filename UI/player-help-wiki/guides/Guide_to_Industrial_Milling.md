# Industrial Milling

In the Industrial age, iron machines take over the work of washing and amalgamating ore. They run on a mill wheel, treadwheel, horse gin or windmill turning beside them. You need **Industrial Milling**, which comes after Amalgamation and Steam Power.

For crushed ore, see [Ore Dressing](Guide_to_Ore_Dressing.md). For pay dirt, see [Placer Mining](Guide_to_Placer_Mining.md). For amalgam and the dangers of quicksilver, see [Amalgamation](Guide_to_Amalgamation.md) and [Mercury](Guide_to_Mercury.md).

## Working the machines

Every machine on this page is set up the same way:

1. Build it next to a turning mill wheel, treadwheel, horse gin or windmill.
2. Fasten it with a wrench.
3. Pour in water with a bucket.
4. Load up to fifty loads of feed.
5. Start it with an empty hand.

The machine works one load per cycle until it runs out of feed, water or drive. If its output tile fills up, it also stops and tells you why. Factory ports can feed it, the same way they feed a stamp mill.

## The machines

| Machine | Cost | Feed | Every | What it gives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <img src="../images/Trommel.png"> **Trommel** | 12 iron | pay dirt | 4 s | sends each load into a sluice box beside it |
| <img src="../images/Amalgamator_drum.png"> **Amalgamator drum** | 15 iron | crushed ore and quicksilver | 6 s | amalgam from most of the metal, plus amalgam tailings |
| <img src="../images/Shaking_table.png"> **Shaking table** | 10 iron | crushed ore | 4 s | clean gold or silver ore from about two thirds of the metal, plus tailings |

- **Trommel:** it needs a free sluice box right beside it. The sluice washes the gravel at its usual rate and uses its own quicksilver, if it has any.
- **Amalgamator drum:** it recovers more than any other method. Pour quicksilver into it with a flask; each lump of amalgam takes 2 units. Every load also loses some quicksilver into the river or the ground.
- <img src="../images/Mercury_separator.png"> **Mercury separator** (8 iron): set it beside an amalgamator drum. Most of the quicksilver the drum would lose settles in it instead.
- **Shaking table:** it uses no quicksilver at all. It recovers less than the drum, but it leaves nothing poisonous behind.

## Amalgamation plates

<img src="../images/Amalgamation_plates.png"> **Amalgamation plates** (4 copper) fit under the stamps of a water stamp mill or an electric stamp mill.

1. Use the plates on the mill.
2. Pour quicksilver onto the plates.

From then on, stamped gold-bearing quartz and silver-bearing ore come out as amalgam and amalgam tailings, with no washing step. About half of the metal is caught, and a little quicksilver is lost with each load. When the quicksilver runs out, the mill crushes ore as usual.

## The hydraulic monitor

<img src="../images/Hydraulic_monitor.png"> A **hydraulic monitor** (15 iron) blasts a whole river bank down at once.

1. Build it on a tile that is right beside fresh river water and next to a mill wheel, treadwheel, horse gin or windmill.
2. Bolt it down with a wrench.
3. Use **Turn Nozzle** to aim it at an uncovered dirt or sand tile.
4. Work it with an empty hand.

The jet tears that tile down into **6 loads of pay dirt** in one go. The land pays a heavy price for it.

- <img src="../images/Stripped_bank.png"> The tile becomes a **stripped bank** of bare gravel. Nothing grows on it, and it never gives pay dirt or anything else again.
- The silt fouls the river downstream.

## The cyanide vat

<img src="../images/Cyanide_vat.png"> A **cyanide vat** (8 wood) gets the last gold out of old tailings. It only works from the Early Modern age.

1. Pour water and cyanide into the vat.
2. Put a sheet of zinc in its zinc box. One sheet lasts for 10 loads.
3. Use **tailings** or **amalgam tailings** on the vat.

Each load uses 1 unit of cyanide and some water. About one load in seven gives a lump of gold ore, and the tailings are used up. Cyanide kills, so keep it out of reach. Draining the vat or breaking it spills its cyanide into the river.

Press and retort the amalgam as described in [Amalgamation](Guide_to_Amalgamation.md#getting-the-metal-out). Keep the drum, the plates and their tailings away from water that people drink or fish.
