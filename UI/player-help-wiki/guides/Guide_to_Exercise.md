# Exercise & Training

Your **strength**, **dexterity** and **stamina** skills normally grow slowly as you work, fight and travel. Training equipment lets you train them on purpose. Start once and you keep training, set after set, until you stop or run out of stamina. See [Character & Skills](Guide_to_Character.md#skills) for what each skill does.

## Lifting weights

Weights are items you carry, made from the **sports** section of the crafting menu.

| Weight | Material | Hands | Suits a strength of | Training limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small lifting stone | 2 stone | One | Up to 0.8 | 1.0 |
| Heavy lifting stone | 5 stone | Both | 0.7 to 1.05 | 1.15 |
| Great lifting stone | 10 stone | Both | 0.95 and up | 1.25 |
| Bronze hand weights | 3 bronze | One | 0.4 and up | 1.25 |
| Iron barbell | 12 iron | Both | 0.9 and up | 1.5 |

Limits are in the same units as your Stats panel, before mood bonuses. The stones need Basic Toolmaking, the hand weights need Bronze Working, and the barbell needs Factories and the Modern era. All but the small stone need some crafting skill to make.

To train:

1. Hold the weight. The two-handed ones need your other hand empty.
2. Use it in your hand once to start lifting. You keep lifting by yourself, about two seconds a lift, while you stand still.
3. Every **six lifts** make a set. Strength is paid when each set is done.

To stop, use the weight in your hand again, move, switch hands, put it down or use **Stop Exercise**. A set you stop part way through earns nothing.

**Pick the right weight.** Examine a weight to see how it suits you. One that is too heavy slips from your hands. One that has become too light teaches only a quarter as much, so move up through the sizes as you grow. Once your strength reaches a weight's limit it can teach you nothing more.

**Hauling.** Walking about with a heavy stone, a great stone or a barbell in your hands is a workout of its own. Every forty tiles pays a little strength and stamina and costs one fatigue. They are heavy, so you walk more slowly while carrying one.

## Skipping rope

A **skipping rope** is made from 2 rope in the **sports** section of the crafting menu. It needs Basic Toolmaking and no crafting skill.

1. Hold the rope with your other hand empty.
2. Use it in your hand once to start skipping. You keep skipping by yourself, about two seconds a skip, while you stand still.
3. Every **six skips** make a set. Each set pays dexterity and a little stamina, up to **1.25**.

You stop the same way as with weights.

## Tread wheels and treadmills

A **tread wheel** is a big wooden wheel you walk inside. It trains you while you stay in one place, like a treadmill.

| Equipment | Material | Needs | Training limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tread wheel | 8 wood | Primitive Construction, some crafting skill | 1.25 |
| Treadmill | 6 steel | Factories, the Modern era, high crafting skill | 1.5 |

Both are built from the **sports** section, one to a tile. Right-click one and choose **Turn Around** to face it another way. A hammer takes it apart.

To train:

1. Click the wheel to step in. You face the way the wheel faces.
2. Hold the movement key for that direction. You stay where you are, the wheel turns, and every stride counts.
3. **Forty strides** make a set.

**Run or walk.** Strides at a run pay dexterity and stamina. Strides at a walk pay only a little stamina, so switch to running for the full workout.

If you stop for more than five seconds, the set starts over. To get off, press any other direction, or click the wheel again to step off the back. Getting off ends the set. Only one person can use a wheel at a time.

## Floor exercises

No equipment? Use **Floor Exercise** in the IC tab and choose **Push-ups** (strength) or **Sit-ups** (stamina). You keep going on dry ground, **three repetitions of 8 seconds** to a set, until you move, run out of stamina, use **Floor Exercise** again or use **Stop Exercise**.

Floor exercises train more slowly than equipment and stop at **1.0**. Past that you need weights, a skipping rope or a tread wheel. They use the same fatigue as equipment, and you cannot do them while you are training with a piece of equipment.

## Hardening your hands

Striking hard things toughens your knuckles, so later punches are less likely to hurt your own hand. The harder the target, the more it teaches you and the more likely it is to hurt you.

| Target | How to get one | Each set adds | Hardens up to | Chance of injury |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Makiwara post | 4 wood, **sports** section | 2 | 30 | Low: a bruise |
| Gravel pail | 3 wood, **sports** section | 3 | 60 | Medium: split knuckles, rarely a break |
| Bare rock wall | Any natural rock face | 4 | 100 | High: about one injury in four breaks the hand |

The post and the pail need the Martial Disciplines research and no crafting skill. Examine one to see how far it can take you.

To train, empty your active hand, switch to **harm** intent and click the target. A set takes about six seconds; stay still until it ends. Each set also trains your **unarmed** skill, which makes your punches hit harder. Landing real punches trains it too.

- **Wraps and gloves** halve both the chance of injury and what a set teaches. Boxing gloves stop every injury, so they teach nothing.
- **Fatigue or a hurt hand** doubles the chance of injury. A broken hand cannot train until it heals.
- Every 10 points of hardening makes a punch less likely to hurt your hand, up to less than half as likely at 100.
- Hardening fades by one point for each day you go without a set.

Sets share fatigue with the rest of your training.

## Martial schools

A martial school is a way of fighting with your bare hands. You follow one school at a time and climb its ranks: **Novice**, **Student**, **Adept** and **Master**.

Each school opens with its own research under Martial Disciplines. A school can be researched from its era onward and stays open in every later era.

| Era | Schools |
| --- | --- |
| Stone | Grappling |
| Classical | Classical Pugilism, Pankration |
| Medieval | Shaolin Kung Fu |
| Imperial | Wing Chun, Hard-Hand Striking |
| Industrial | Savate |
| WW1 | Judo |
| WW2 | Military Combatives |
| Cold War | Krav Maga |
| Modern | Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu |
| Digital | Mixed Martial Arts |

**Choosing a school.** Use **Choose martial school** in the IC tab and pick a school you know the research for; you start as a Novice. You can leave your school at any time and keep your progress in it, so you can return later. After any change you wait 7 real days before you can take up a school again.

**Training.** You can train all the way to Master on your own, and company or gear only makes it faster:

- **Practise martial forms** in the IC tab runs through your school's drills. Empty both hands and stay put for about eight seconds. A set tires you like any other exercise, and the more tired you are, the less it teaches.
- Every punch you land counts a little. Fighting only teaches so much in a day, though: punches on anyone but a verified Master of your school stop counting after a while, until the next day.
- Hardening your hands counts a little more.

- **Training gear** teaches faster than forms. Empty your active hand, switch to **harm** intent and click the gear for a six-second drill set. Gear never hurts your hand.

| Gear | How to make it | Needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heavy bag | 4 leather, **sports** section | Martial Disciplines, some crafting skill |
| Wooden dummy | 8 wood, **sports** section | Wing Chun research, more crafting skill |
| Wrestling mat | 6 leather, **sports** section | Grappling research, some crafting skill |

A set on the wooden dummy teaches a little more than one on the heavy bag. The wrestling mat lies flat on the floor; it teaches grappling schools as much as the dummy, and other schools only half as much.

**Training together** is faster:

| Company | Speed |
| --- | --- |
| Another player of your school within 2 tiles | x1.5 |
| A Master of your school within 2 tiles | x2 |
| Landing punches on a Master of your school | x2.5 |

Your partner must be awake and playing. Only Masters who have verified their Discord account (**Verify Discord** in the OOC tab) give the Master bonuses.

It takes many hours of practice to become a Master. The first time you become a Master of any school, you also gain a large boost to Military Science, once per character.

**What a school teaches.** From Student on, your school changes how you fight, and the effect grows at Adept and Master:

- **Striking schools** hit harder with punches, and harder still on the targets they drill: Pugilism the head, Wing Chun the chest, Savate the legs, Krav Maga the groin and mouth.
- **Hard-Hand Striking** also turns hardened hands into extra damage.
- **Grappling schools** (Grappling, Judo, Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu, and in part Pankration, Military Combatives, Krav Maga and Mixed Martial Arts) tighten their holds faster and break free of holds more easily.

## Stamina, fatigue and rest

- Lifting, skipping and floor exercises use up your **stamina** while you keep at them, faster than it comes back. A rested character can keep going for about four minutes before they run out and stop. You need about a fifth of your stamina to start again.
- Each completed set adds **2 fatigue**, up to a maximum of 6. At 6 your sets teach nothing, but you keep training, and your sets pay again once you have recovered a little.
- Each set also makes you a little hungrier and thirstier, and you cannot gain while very hungry or thirsty.
- One fatigue point recovers per minute.
- The more fatigued you are, the less each set teaches you. Gains are also halved once a skill reaches 1.0.
- You must be conscious, standing and able to use your hands, and a skill below 0.1 is too low to train.
- Use **Stop Exercise** in the IC tab to stop. A set you stop part way through earns nothing.
- Hunger, thirst or reaching your gear's training limit also stop you.

Start a session, keep at it until you run out of stamina, then rest or do something else while your stamina and fatigue recover.
