# Epochs

Civ13 uses ten epochs. The date identifies the stage of the game, not the tier of a particular research bench; menus and research announcements sometimes use different names for the same date. These are game labels, not historical boundaries. Not every mode offers every starting epoch, and the list is not a live server status.

## How to use this guide

The advice below is for **persistent worlds** such as Gaia. Finishing a [capstone chapter](Guide_to_Books_and_Scholars.md#research-benches) moves the land you are in into its next age. Each land keeps its own age, so the other lands are not moved until a copy of that capstone reaches them. That opens the stage for new work; it does not teach every player its recipes. Study the relevant chapters and check your skills, stations and the [Crafting Atlas](https://civ13.github.io/Civ13/crafting/). [Faction research](Guide_to_Research.md) works differently in other modes.

Each age below has three parts: **what changes** when the world enters it, what to **focus** on while you are in it, and what to **prepare** so your group is ready to open the next one.

### What every age has in common

- **An age lives in writing.** A land stays in its age only while a book, manual or torn chapter somewhere in that land still holds the capstone that opened it (or a later one). If the last copy is destroyed or carried to another land, the land keeps its age for two hours. Bring a copy back in that time and nothing is lost; otherwise the land falls back one age, then one more each hour while the copies stay missing. Characters keep what they studied, and anyone who still knows the capstone can write it down again to bring the age straight back, with no bill to pay a second time.
- **Books carry ages between lands.** A capstone book carried into a land that has not reached its age yet does nothing at first. If it stays there for two hours, that land moves forward one age, then one more each hour until it reaches the book's age. Take it away before then and the wait starts again. So a book that leaves home for good slowly takes its age with it, while one brought back within two hours changes nothing.
- **Keep spare copies.** Store copies of each capstone in more than one place, with more than one group and in every land you want to keep in that age, so one fire, raid, lost bag or journey cannot cost a land its age.
- **Late capstones need materials.** From Steam Power on, each capstone also needs a bill of materials delivered to the bench while its book is on it. Anyone may deliver, from any faction. Deliveries are written into that book, so they travel with it, but a copied or printed book starts its bill again.
- **Some capstones need a demonstration.** Steam Power, Electricity, Nuclear Power, Digital Computing and Digital Networks each need the researcher to watch a working machine for a set time. Stay close to it and keep it running until the recording finishes.

## Stone Age (5000 B.C.)

- **What changes:** This is the starting age. Flint tools, campfires and basic shelters get you through the first days. Founding a faction gives its founder the Stone Age master book, holding every Stone Age chapter, and a writing slate; members still have to study the chapters to learn them.
- **Focus:** Get food, water and shelter sorted, then study **Basic Toolmaking** and set up a research bench. Start with [Your First Day](Guide_to_Your_First_Day.md).
- **Prepare for 313 B.C.:** Research **Bronze Working** and keep clay for the unfired axehead mold that completes it. Locate copper and tin so you can cast bronze once the age opens. **Stone Masonry**, **Cooperage & Containers** and **Resource Processing** are worth starting now too.

## Bronze or Classical Age (313 B.C.)

- **What changes:** **Bronze Working** opens this age. Bronze tools and other cast goods become possible for those who have studied it, and masonry, monuments and better containers come through their own chapters.
- **Focus:** Get copper and tin reliably, fire clay molds, and set up a kiln so you can make bronze tools rather than replacing stone ones one at a time. See [Metallurgy](Guide_to_Metallurgy.md).
- **Prepare for 1013:** Have a scholar research **Iron Smithing**. Save clay for its prototype, an unfired clay ingot mold, and locate iron ore and fuel for the next workshop.

## Medieval Age (1013)

- **What changes:** **Iron Smithing** opens this age. Ironworking, brickmaking, glassworking and papermaking give settlements new ways to build, equip and teach.
- **Focus:** Establish a bloomery and learn **Iron Tools** so the settlement can make better equipment. Use paper and books to spread knowledge beyond one smith or scholar.
- **Prepare for 1713:** Choose a route, or take both: **Papermaking** leads to **Printing Press**, whose prototype is a carved stamp; **Iron Smithing** leads to **Gunpowder**, which has its own prototype. Either one opens the next age. Also research **Watermills** (it needs **Resource Processing**), because Steam Power needs it later.

## Imperial Age (1713)

- **What changes:** **Printing Press** or **Gunpowder** opens this age. The press is a peaceful route into the same age; war-related recipes still need their own research.
- **Focus:** Spread useful chapters with [paperwork and printing](Guide_to_Paperwork.md), and develop water-powered workshops, blast furnaces and a bulk metal supply.
- **Prepare for 1873:** **Steam Power** needs **Watermills** and **Iron Smithing**. Stock 60 coal, 40 iron ingots and 10 copper ingots for its bill, and build a steam engine: the bench needs a recorded demonstration of it running for one minute, and study alone does not finish the chapter.

## Industrial Age (1873)

- **What changes:** **Steam Power** opens the Industrial Age. Steam machinery, factories, steelmaking and rail make bulk production and freight practical as their own chapters are learned.
- **Focus:** Pick one production chain to keep supplied and use [rail](Guide_to_Trains.md) where hauling is the bottleneck. Grow your fuel and metal supply before building more machines.
- **Prepare for 1903:** **Electricity** needs **Steam Power** and **Galvanic Cells**. Its bill is 80 copper ingots, 30 zinc ingots, 20 lead ingots and 20 rubber sheets, and the demonstration is an electric lamp kept lit from an engine or generator for one minute. Rubber comes from only some lands, so arrange a supply early.

## Early Modern Age (1903)

- **What changes:** **Electricity** opens this age, called **WW1** in some research announcements. Power generation, batteries and wiring support machines; the combustion engine and assembly lines open through separate chapters.
- **Focus:** Build a small, reliable [power grid](Guide_to_Power.md) and organize [factory production](Guide_to_Factory_Automation.md). Work out what your settlement can produce steadily and what you need to trade for.
- **Prepare for 1943:** Choose **Powered Flight** (needs the **Combustion Engine**) or **Mechanized Warfare** (needs **Field Artillery**, the **Combustion Engine** and **Steelmaking**). Powered Flight's bill is 120 steel sheets, 40 rubber sheets, 30 brass ingots and 40 cloth; Mechanized Warfare's is 200 steel sheets, 40 rubber sheets, 40 lead ingots, 30 sulphur and 30 saltpeter. These two have no demonstration; the bill takes the place of a prototype, but study is still required.

## World War II (1943)

- **What changes:** **Powered Flight** or **Mechanized Warfare** opens this age. [Aircraft](Guide_to_Aircraft.md), armour and heavier industry have their own downstream chapters; the two capstones do not grant each other's knowledge.
- **Focus:** Secure steel, rubber, fuel and spare parts before relying on aircraft or industrial machinery. Research **Alloys**, which much of the later tree builds on, and keep experienced crews and working infrastructure supplied.
- **Prepare for 1969:** **Nuclear Power** needs **Electricity** and **Alloys**. Its bill is 30 uranium ore, 120 lead ingots, 150 steel sheets and 100 concrete, and the demonstration is a reactor kept producing power, below overheating, for three minutes. Practise running a reactor safely before you record it.

## Cold War (1969)

- **What changes:** **Nuclear Power** opens this age. With more research, settlements can pursue automation and [electronic computing](Guide_to_Computers.md). The Moon comes within reach for groups that follow the rocketry and spaceflight chapters.
- **Focus:** Learn to run and maintain your power and production systems before expanding them. If you pursue spaceflight, follow the [space station guide](Guide_to_Space_Stations.md) and [The Moon & Mars](Guide_to_the_Moon.md).
- **Prepare for 2013:** **Digital Computing** needs **Automation**, **Electricity** and **Electronic Computing**. Its bill is 250 copper, 100 aluminium, 60 gold and 60 silver ingots, 60 electronics and 15 gems, and the demonstration is a computerized fabricator kept running for one minute.

## Modern Age (2013)

- **What changes:** **Digital Computing** opens this age. It lays the groundwork for advanced electronics and 3D printing; it is not a shortcut to Digital Age networks or consumer devices.
- **Focus:** Make electronics production reliable. Train enough specialists to keep power and computing working without relying on one person.
- **Prepare for 2026:** **Digital Networks** builds on **Digital Computing**. Its bill is 350 copper, 150 aluminium, 80 gold and 80 silver ingots, 100 electronics and 25 gems, and the demonstration is a mobile phone call kept connected through a cell tower for one minute. Arrange suppliers early and keep spare copies of the chapters.

## Digital Age (2026)

- **What changes:** **Digital Networks** opens the last age. Further research includes **Consumer Electronics**, **3D Printing**, **Smart Home** and **Drones**, and Mars comes within reach for groups that have done the spaceflight chapters.
- **Focus:** Pick useful projects and maintain the electricity, parts and material supply they depend on. Keep teaching and copying newer research that other players do not already know.
- **Prepare for what comes after:** There is no later age, so the work is keeping this one. Hold copies of the Digital Networks chapter in several safe places, and if your group settles off-world, secure life support, replacement equipment and a route home before committing to a base.
