# Peoples and Languages

Every character belongs to a people, and every people has its own language. This guide covers how you get your people and mother tongue, how well you know each language, and how to learn a new one.

```admonish info
This applies to [Civilizations & Nomads](../gamemodes/Civilizations_and_Nomads.md) on the lands listed below. Other maps give you a language with your faction or job.
```

## Your people

When you first arrive on a land you are asked two things:

1. **Which people do you belong to?** Where you start decides the choice:
   - **Among people.** If you start in a settlement, beside a recruitment station or at a bed in town, you are born into that community. The people most common there is the default, and you can pick any other people living there. A nation that has grown a people of its own peoples (see below) has its newcomers born into it.
   - **In the wild.** You can pick any people of that land, or choose **By birthplace** to belong to the people of the climate you arrived in.
   - **Coming back.** A new character who starts close to where your last one died keeps that people, if it lives there.
2. **Name and looks.** Take a name of your people and looks of your birthplace, or keep the ones from your character setup.

Your people gives you your **mother tongue**, your style of name and your starting clothes. Your looks come from your birthplace, never from your people: anyone can look any way. Your people is recorded in your notes under **Origin** together with the land you were born on, and it stays with your character for life. Joining a faction never changes it.

Nobody can see your people by looking at you. Others learn it from your accent, from what you tell them, or by asking.

| --- | --- |
| Gaia | Fjordfolk (Old Norse), Hearthlanders (Latin), Isleborn (Greek), Dunefolk (Egyptian), Grasslanders (Swahili), Mistwood Folk (Japanese) |
| Europe | Northmen (Old Norse), Gaels (Gaelic), Hellenes (Greek), Latins (Latin) |
| Africa | Swahili (Swahili), Zulu (Zulu), Egyptians (Egyptian), Arabs (Arabic) |
| Asia | Mongols (Mongolian), Koreans (Korean), Han (Chinese), Persians (Farsi), Yamato (Japanese), Viet (Vietnamese) |

On Gaia the peoples are named for the land: Fjordfolk in the tundra and taiga, Hearthlanders in temperate and semiarid country, Isleborn by the sea, Dunefolk in the desert, Grasslanders on the savanna and Mistwood Folk in the jungle.

## Peoples that grow from the world

Peoples are not fixed. Over long stretches of play they split, blend and take people in, much as languages do. Time is counted in years of the world calendar (a year is four seasons, about four real hours), and only years when members actually play count. Research and eras play no part.

- **A people can split.** When a nation's members of one people have no contact with any other faction for about 180 years in a row (roughly a month of play), and there are at least five of them making up at least half the nation, they become a people of their own. Contact means hearing people from other factions, or nationless people, speak, or spending time near them: trade, visits and shared settlements all count. A year with contact halves the count, so splits are rare.
- **Peoples can blend.** In a nation where two peoples each make up at least a quarter of its members for about 30 played years, a people of both grows. Its mother tongue is the larger group's. Nobody already living changes people: the blend is the people their newcomers are born into.
- **Naming.** The nation's leader names a new people with **Name Our People** in the IC tab, picking one of three suggested names. Each is a word of the people's own tongue with its meaning shown beside it: the land they live in (such as *the people of the frost*), a trait (such as *the steadfast*), or their eldest member (*the children of* that elder). **Suggest other names** offers new words for the same meanings. Naming needs a Discord-verified account (OOC > Verify Discord), and the name can't be changed later.
- **Raised among.** A character who spends about 20 played years living among another people has "Raised among" that people added to their notes. That people's leader can then welcome them with **Welcome Among Us** in the IC tab. Your people of birth never changes.
- **Peoples are never a rule.** No skill, price, law, job or door depends on your people.

**Check Known Languages** shows a **Your people** section: your people, the peoples it grew from, related peoples, and who you were raised among or welcomed by.

## How well you know a language

You know each language to a score from 0 to 100. **Check Known Languages** in the IC tab shows the level and percentage of every language you speak, and the ones you are still picking up.

| Level | Score | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A few words | 1 to 24 | You catch the odd word. You can't speak it yet. |
| Broken | 25 to 59 | You can speak it. Some of your words come out in your mother tongue instead, and you miss some of what you hear. |
| Conversational | 60 to 89 | Most words get through both ways. You can read and write paper in it. |
| Fluent | 90 and up | Everything gets through. |

The share of words you understand, and the share you get right when speaking, is your score. Until you are fluent, others also hear your accent, for example *(Latin, Old Norse accent)*.

Related languages give you a head start. A speaker of German already understands some Dutch, for instance.

Use **Set Default Language** in the IC tab to choose which language you speak.

## Learning a language

On your own, a second language takes several days of living among its speakers. A fluent teacher is much faster. Nothing ever lowers a score.

| Way to learn | Gain | Limit per real day, per language |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hearing** someone who speaks it better than you | About 6 points an hour among speakers | 12, shared with speaking |
| **Speaking** it to someone who speaks it better | A little per line they hear | 12, shared with hearing |
| **Lessons** with the **Teach Language** command | 6 points per one-minute lesson, never past the teacher's own score | 90 |
| **Language book** | 5 points per sitting, up to Broken | 10, shared with studying writing |
| **Study Writing** on a paper written in the language | A little per study, up to Broken | 10, shared with the language book |

Hearing and speaking slow to half speed once you are Conversational.

**Lessons.** A fluent speaker stands next to the learner and uses **Teach Language** in the IC tab. The learner accepts, and both stay put for about a minute. About fifteen lessons take a beginner to Fluent, so a patient teacher can get someone there in one sitting. Lessons count on top of what you pick up by listening.

**Language books.** Someone who is at least Conversational in two languages can write a language book with a pen. A reader who knows one of the two languages uses the book in hand to learn the other. A book only takes you as far as Broken; the rest you learn from people. Reading and writing need light.

**Study Writing.** Hold a paper with writing you can't read and right-click it to study it. Like a language book, writing teaches you up to Broken.

Roughly, living among speakers gets you from nothing to Conversational in about a week, while ten one-minute lessons do it in ten minutes.

## Languages that grow apart

A language can split over a long time, the way Latin became French and Spanish. It only happens to a nation that stays cut off from every other nation for a long time, and splits are rare.

- **Talking only among yourselves pulls your speech away.** When a nation's native speakers hear nobody but each other, their way of speaking slowly drifts from everyone else's. It takes many days of play.
- **Any real contact holds it together.** Regularly hearing people from other nations, or nationless people, in any language, pulls the drift back. A stranger passing by with a word or two doesn't count.
- **The split comes after a long isolation.** A nation whose speech has drifted far enough, has stayed that way for a week with no contact, and has at least five living native speakers is told its language is becoming one of its own. Research and eras play no part. The nation's leader names it with **Name Our Language** in the IC tab. Naming needs a Discord-verified account (OOC > Verify Discord), and the name can't be changed later.
- **Nobody loses anything.** The nation's native speakers get the new language as their mother tongue and speak it by default. They still know the old one. Speakers of the old language follow most of the new one, and it is easy to learn from its speakers. It has its own say key, shown in **Check Known Languages**.
- **It keeps its writing.** A new language writes in the same script as the language it grew from, so papers stay readable both ways.
- **It can grow further apart.** If the new language's speakers stay cut off long enough again, the two languages understand a little less of each other each time.

**Check Known Languages** shows a **Language families** section once one of your languages has relatives on your land: which language each grew out of, among which people and in which era it split, which ones you speak, and how much of each other their speakers follow.

Each language can split at most twice on a land. Travellers carry their languages with them, so a land that has never heard a new language learns it from them.

## Paper and books

- You need to be **Conversational** in a language to read or write paper in it. See [Paperwork](Guide_to_Paperwork.md).
- Research chapters are written in the language of whoever wrote them. You can't study a chapter if you are below Broken in its language, and studying is slower until you are fluent, twice as slow at Broken. The book's window lists the languages of its chapters. See [Books and Scholars](Guide_to_Books_and_Scholars.md).
- **Translating a chapter.** Copying a chapter by hand at a bench needs Conversational in its language, and the copy comes out in the language you write in. Set your default language to the one you want, then copy. Printed books and torn-out chapters keep their language.
- The chapters of a starting book are plain to everyone.

## Tips

- Settle near people who speak the language you want and talk with them. Speaking to better speakers teaches you faster than only listening.
- A translator is worth feeding. Someone Conversational in two languages can write language books, translate research and teach.
- Before trading books with another people, check which languages their chapters are in.
- Sleeping teaches you nothing: you only learn from speech you hear.
