# Placer Mining

Rivers carry gold, tin and other heavy grains down from the hills and drop them in the gravel of their banks. That gravel is **pay dirt**. You dig it with a shovel and wash it in water, and the heavy metal grains stay behind.

## Digging pay dirt

1. Stand on an uncovered dirt or sand river bank, right beside shallow fresh water.
2. Hold a shovel.
3. Use **Dig pay dirt** in the Crafting tab.

Each bit of bank gives three loads, then it is dug out for a while. Move along the river to keep digging.

## What the ground holds

Every stretch of river is one of three kinds of ground, and all the bank around it is the same kind:

| Ground | What washing finds |
| --- | --- |
| Gold | Gold, some silver and some tin |
| Tin | Mostly tin, a little gold |
| Barren | Sand, and black iron sand from the Classical age on |

To prospect, hold pay dirt in one hand and a wooden bowl in the other, beside shallow fresh water, and use the pay dirt on the bowl. Panning is slow and loses most of the metal, but it tells you what kind of ground you are on. If it is barren, try a different stretch of river.

## The sluice box

<img src="../images/Sluice_box.png"> A **sluice box** (6 wood) needs **Bronze Working**. It is a sloped wooden trough with cross riffles and a fleece in the bottom.

1. Build it beside water, or pour water into it with a bucket. Each load uses some water.
2. Hold pay dirt and use it on the sluice. You keep washing load after load until the pay dirt or the water runs out, or you step away.

A sluice washes much faster than a pan and catches about three times as much.

### Mercury in the riffles

Pour mercury from a flask or vessel into the sluice. The quicksilver grabs fine gold, so the sluice catches about a quarter more. A little mercury washes away with every load and fouls the ground around the sluice. See [Mercury](Guide_to_Mercury.md) for its dangers.

## The gold rocker

<img src="../images/Gold_rocker.png"> A **gold rocker** (5 wood) needs **Ore Dressing**. It is a wooden box on rockers with a sieve hopper on top. It has its own water tank, so you can carry it to any bank and fill it with a bucket. One person washes faster with a rocker than with a sluice.

To take a sluice or rocker apart, hit it with a hammer on harm intent. You get some of the wood back.
