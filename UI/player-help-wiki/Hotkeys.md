Movement is free: holding a movement key slides you smoothly at walking or running speed, and a quick tap moves you exactly one tile. You count as standing on whichever tile is under the middle of your body, so once you are more than halfway onto a tile, that is where you drop things and what you can reach from. Shots and thrown things hit you where your body is drawn, even when you stand between tiles, and your own shots leave from where you actually stand. That one-tile tap is called Tap To Step and is on unless you turn it off: click the footprint button on your HUD, or open **Settings > Controls and HUD** (also in the Game Menu on Esc). With it off, you stop exactly where you let go of the key instead of finishing the tile. The same Settings section holds the name shown under your mouse and its size, the HUD size, editing and resetting the HUD layout, and your key bindings. Moving into a corner slips you sideways into a doorway, and moving diagonally into a wall slides you along it. A tree only blocks you at its trunk, so you can walk beside it and under its branches. Anything you pull follows into the tile you were last standing on once you have fully stepped off it, just as when moving a tile at a time. Small movements that keep you on the same tile drag it along a few pixels instead, so you can nudge it into place. If something is in the way, it stays put and you lose your hold once you are a whole tile away from it. Crawling and riding a horse move freely too. Walk into a trench edge and you climb in or out from wherever you stand, keeping your place along the edge; climbing in takes half a second and climbing out two seconds. Bridges over trenches are level with the ground, so you walk onto them without climbing. When you and another player are both on help intent, you walk through each other, easing a little to the side as you pass. While you drive, you still move one tile at a time.

## Walking to a spot

**Ctrl+click** a spot you can see and your character walks there by the quickest way, up to 32 tiles away. The route goes round walls, deep water, lava, drops and fires, and through a shut door only when it would open for you: never a locked, barred or held door, or a shut window. It prefers fast ground: it keeps to roads and floors over snow, mud and wading, and goes round a steep hill when that is quicker than climbing it. Click something you can't stand on, such as a wall, a table or a tree, and you stop next to it. Ctrl+clicking something right next to you still pulls it, as before.

You walk or run at your usual pace and pull anything you were already pulling. You stop when you arrive, when you press any movement key, when you get hurt, when a hostile animal comes close, or when the way stays blocked. You can't set off while a hostile animal is already close, while driving, or while buckled, restrained or crouching. Use **Check Movement** (IC tab) to see what slows you down on the way.

## Opening worn storage

You can open what you wear without clicking its slot. **I** opens your worn storage one after another: your backpack, your belt, then anything attached to your clothes, such as a leather harness, chest webbing or pouches, your coat pockets, and bags in your pockets. Press it again to open the next one; pressing it on the last one closes it. **P** opens just your backpack, **K** just your belt and a pouch in your hip slot, and **Shift+I** goes through only the things on your clothes and in your pockets, each the same way. In typing mode use **Ctrl+I**, **Ctrl+P**, **Ctrl+K** and **Ctrl+Shift+I**. You can change all four keys under **OOC > Keybindings**.

## Typing bubbles

While you write a message in the Say window (**T**), a speech bubble shows over your character. The Emote window (**F3**) shows a different bubble, so others can tell you are about to act rather than speak. Typing straight into the chat bar shows no bubble. You can hide them with the **Typing indicator** setting in your game preferences.

## Changing keys

You can rebind prone, aiming mode, sights, the storage keys, resist (**Ctrl+B** in both modes), going up or down a level (unbound at first) and other action keys through **OOC > Keybindings** or the **Keybindings** menu. Hotkey Mode and typing mode keep separate key sets. You can unbind a key, restore one action, or restore all defaults. Reserved keys and keys already in use are refused, and typing-mode bindings need Ctrl or Ctrl+Shift. Your keys are saved with your account.
