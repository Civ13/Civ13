# Civilizations and Nomads

Read the map and research selection in the lobby. Start with the [Starter Guide](../Starter_Guide.md): find water, arrange a reliable food supply and build shelter before spending resources on expansion. Agree with your faction on who will gather supplies, build and research. The [Research Guide](../guides/Guide_to_Research.md) explains the technology tree and its crafting gates.

## What is Civilizations and Nomads mode?

In Nomads, everyone spawns without any predetermined roles or **[Factions](#factions)**. The **[map](#nomads-maps)** is a complete wilderness, ready to be explored and settled. There are no prebuilt structures, except for occasional ancient ruins.

You can stay factionless (a nomad), create your own faction, or join an existing one. Only members of a faction can research new technologies and accumulate research points.

A land has several peoples, each speaking its own language, which a speaker of a different language cannot understand at first. On most lands you choose your people when you arrive; see [Peoples and Languages](../guides/Guide_to_Peoples_and_Languages.md).

There is no predetermined goal.

What will each civilization desire? Military power? World domination? Scholars with advanced technology? Mass resource output and production? Commerce and trade? Raiding? It's up to you or your leaders to decide!

## What is RP Map mode?

In the RP mode, everyone chooses a role to play as before spawning. There may be one or more preset factions. The map has some prebuilt structures, but there is also plenty of space to expand with new buildings.

The map sets the era, starting technology and research rules. Check the round briefing before assuming research is available or that the technology rules match a Nomads round.

There may or may not be specific goals, which are listed at the start of the round. A brief outline for goals on different maps where they are present (mostly accumulating points for factions under certain conditions):

- Gulag (1946) - the prisoners - hold as much money as possible within your faction and escape; the camp guard - force the prisoners to work and send out the resources.
- **[The Art of the Deal](The_Art_of_the_Deal.md)** (1994) - the company members - arrange deals to acquire disks and money; the police - jail company members and confiscate disks and money.

Maps without a set goal:

* Bohemia (1013) - live in a city in Medieval Europe.
* **[Colony](../maps/Colony.md)** (1713) - build a New World colony (two maps with different climates).
* Little Creek (1873) - live in a Wild West town.
* Pioneers (1873) - develop a town in the prairies.

## What else is there?

There are also other modes and maps which are, effectively, a mix of the previous two modes, with a number of extra features. Currently it includes:

- **[Tribes](../maps/Tribes.md)** - a fantasy mode with several different races (chosen randomly upon spawning), each forming a preset faction.
- Wasteland (2013) - survive in a post-apocalyptic environment (two variations - zombies and nuclear apocalypse).

## Becoming Civilized

Perhaps you have noticed that your ability to craft things is a bit
limited in comparison to other people, and you would like to make more.
Perhaps you are finding surviving alone difficult. Perhaps you enjoy the
company and roleplay opportunity that comes with engaging with other
players. If those apply to you, then you should consider joining or
starting a faction.

The benefits of joining or assembling a faction:

- You gain access to that faction's research levels, which equates to craft. See **[The Research Milestones](#the-research-milestones)**.
- More opportunity to roleplay in a manner you want. From becoming a town's blacksmith, simple farmer, village idiot, to village chieftain, diplomat, emperor, or anything you want to try and make happen.
- Depending on the faction, you might have access to shared resources and facilities.
- Safety in numbers. Raiders will think twice before attacking a city, whereas a loner is an easy target for burglars, thieves, pillagers, and robbers.

### Factions

Factions are basically "teams." Everyone in the same faction shares the same research points, and everyone in the faction contributes to everyone else when they perform research.

But that also means that lazy people can take advantage of your technology and resources and not contribute a thing.

A small, tight-knit group of contributors is often better off than a large group of slackers that just consume.

Pick your members wisely. Or cunningly use them to your advantage.

Work smart, not hard!

#### General

* To **Create** a faction, open the **Faction tab** and click **Create Faction**. Choose a name for it and draw your faction's symbol on the 32x32 canvas, or stamp one of the classic shapes and draw over it. Your banner colours are taken from the drawing. As Leader you can change the symbol later with **Modify Faction Symbol** on the **Faction tab**.
* Both symbol canvases have **Copy design code** and **Paste design code**. The design code is the symbol written out as text, starting with `C13SYMBOL/`, so you can keep it outside the game and use it again for a new faction or on another land. Pasting only fills the canvas: in **Modify Faction Symbol**, press **Save** to put it on your banners. A code keeps up to 16 colours; a busier symbol keeps its 16 most used colours.
* To join a faction, you must be **Recruited** by its leader or by a member who has the **Recruitment** permission, or join through one of its [recruitment stations](#recruitment-stations). If you are already in another faction, accepting moves you over and you join as an ordinary member, without any rank or permissions you had before.
* You can leave a faction by clicking **Abandon Faction** on the **Faction tab**. Take note, that **you will not be able to rejoin the same faction for 24 hours!**
* Creating a faction makes you the default **Leader**.
* Every faction has a **member limit**: 10 members to begin with, raised by building a [faction hall](#faction-halls). A full faction cannot take new members, but anyone already counted among its members can rejoin. Your place counts for your account, so it is kept when your character dies; it is freed when you abandon the faction or after two weeks without playing a member.

#### Faction halls

A faction grows by building a hall. Craft a **faction hall seat** from **20 wood planks** (in the **economy & production** category) and place it; the leader clicks it and chooses **Designate**. Members then hand it materials to raise it, one stage at a time:

| Hall | Members | Needs | Materials |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Council fire | 15 | Stone Age | 40 wood, 40 stone |
| Longhouse | 25 | Classical Age, indoors | 150 wood, 100 stone, 30 cloth |
| Town hall | 35 | Medieval Age, indoors | 300 stone, 60 iron, 40 glass |
| Capitol | 50 | Industrial Age, indoors | 600 stone, 150 steel, 80 glass, 20 gold |

From the longhouse on, the hall must stand under a roof. If the hall is destroyed, the faction loses its stages and cannot take in new members beyond 10 until it builds again; nobody is removed. Each [colony](../guides/Guide_to_Travel.md#factions-and-colonies) your faction holds in another land adds room for 5 more members. Examine the hall to see how many members the faction may have and what the next stage needs.

To move the hall, the leader (or an officer allowed to move the faction's hall and fixtures) right-clicks it and chooses **Fasten or Unfasten**, pulls it to its new place, and fastens it again. It keeps its stage and any materials already handed in. While it stands loose it counts for nothing, and anyone can pull it away, so fasten it again quickly; only the leader or such an officer can. The same works for the nation assembly seat, recruitment stations, the colony charter and the faction strongbox.

#### Nations

In the connected lands of the wider world, factions can band together into a **nation**. Each faction keeps its own leader, research, members and treasury, and factions of one nation can still disagree or fight; the nation is what they stand for together. A nation can hold factions from different lands. Leaders use **Nation** on the **Faction tab**:

* **Found a nation**: stand within a few steps of the leader of another faction from your land, name the nation, and both agree. You become its head.
* **Apply to a nation**: the leaders of its member factions vote. Over half of them saying yes admits you; half or more saying no refuses you. An application not decided within 3 days lapses.
* **Vote for a head**: the head speaks for the nation, chosen when over half of the member factions agree. It gives no power over the other factions.
* **Vote on a common money**: over half of the member factions can adopt one member's coin as the nation's common money. Nation view shows it, and money changers list it next to their rate for that coin.
* **Secede**: leave at any time. Your faction cannot apply to that nation again for 3 days. A nation left with one faction dissolves.

Answers arrive within a few minutes, once the lands have conferred. A colony's members belong to their faction's nation, but only the leader in the faction's own land can act for it. Examining someone shows which nation they are a citizen of.

A nation holds **3 factions** at first. To hold more, a member faction builds a **nation assembly**: craft a **nation assembly seat** from **30 wood planks** (**economy & production**), place it in your faction's own land and **Designate** it, then members hand it materials like a faction hall:

| Assembly | Factions | Needs | Materials |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Meeting ground | 4 | Classical Age | 120 wood, 120 stone |
| Assembly hall | 6 | Medieval Age, indoors | 400 stone, 80 iron, 40 glass, 60 cloth |
| Parliament | 8 | Industrial Age, indoors | 800 stone, 200 steel, 100 glass, 40 gold |

The best assembly any member faction keeps standing counts. If it burns or loses its roof, the nation can take in fewer factions, but no faction is removed.

#### Nation government

Every nation starts as a **confederation**: the rules above, one vote per member faction. Under **Nation > Government** it can choose another system. Each member faction keeps its own leader, offices and courts whatever the nation's government.

| Government | Head | Needs | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Confederation | none | - | Member factions vote on everything; any member may leave at once. |
| Theocracy | High Priest | - | The leader of the state faith rules alone. Leaving takes a day's notice. |
| Monarchy | Monarch | Classical Age | The monarch rules for life, admits members and chooses the money. Holds 1 more faction. Leaving takes 2 days' notice. |
| Dictatorship | Dictator | Classical Age | The dictator rules alone. Holds 2 more factions. Leaving takes 3 days' notice. |
| Republic | Consul | Classical Age | A senate of faction leaders votes, each weighted by its active citizens. The consul is elected every 14 days. Holds 1 more faction. |
| Democracy | Archon | Classical Age | Citizens vote in referendums and elect the archon every 7 days. |
| Merchant league | Doge | Medieval Age | A council of faction leaders votes, each weighted by its strongbox (no faction over two fifths). The doge is elected every 14 days. Holds 1 more faction. |
| Council of elders | Eldest | - | Votes like a republic's senate. The eldest is elected every 14 days and may be away a week. Leaving takes a day's notice. |
| Feudal crown | King | Medieval Age | Like a monarchy, with an heir and a throne to claim. Keeps up to 8 vassal nations, which must serve 28 days before they can declare independence. A little easier to overthrow. Holds 1 more faction. |
| Constitutional monarchy | Prime Minister | Industrial Age | A parliament votes like a republic's senate. The prime minister is elected every 14 days. Hard to overthrow. Holds 2 more factions. |
| One-party state | Chairman | Cold War | The chairman rules alone. Holds 3 more factions, but a quarter of citizens are enough to start a revolt. Leaving takes 3 days' notice. |

A government's age counts when most member factions' lands have reached it. Under a confederation, two thirds of the member factions must agree to a change. A ruler decides alone, and elective governments put it to a vote. A change takes effect a day after it passes, at most once a week. The head must be a citizen with a verified Discord account.

**Citizens.** In elective governments, eligible citizens can vote, stand for election and, in a democracy, propose motions from **Nation > Motions and elections**. An eligible citizen has a verified account, has been a member for 3 days and has played this week. In a democracy, a motion needs over half the votes, with at least a third of eligible citizens taking part. A change of government needs two thirds of the votes, with half of them taking part. In a senate or council, motions pass on the weighted votes of faction leaders, and a change of government still needs two thirds.

**Succession.** A head who stays away too long loses the seat. That is 7 days for a monarch, king, eldest or High Priest and 3 days for the others.

* A monarch's or king's chosen heir (**Name an heir**) takes the throne. With no heir, any verified citizen can claim the empty throne by holding a nation assembly for 30 minutes. Another citizen standing nearby pauses the count.
* A consul's deputy (**Name a deputy**) stands in while a new election opens. An archon, doge, eldest or prime minister is replaced by an election.
* An absent dictator's nation falls back to a confederation. An absent chairman's seat stays empty until the member factions choose again.
* A theocracy's seat passes to whoever leads the state faith.

While a ruler's seat is empty, the member factions decide as in a confederation.

**Offices of state.** Every government with a head has three offices, filled from **Nation > Offices of state**:

* **Treasurer**: chooses the common money in the ruler's place, or brings it to a vote in an elective government.
* **Chancellor**: under a ruler, admits or refuses applications in the ruler's place.
* **Marshal**: guards the nation's assemblies. While the marshal stands within 3 tiles, nobody can make progress claiming a throne or holding a revolt there.

The head appoints a citizen with a verified account. A ruler's appointment stands at once, while a senate, council or the citizens must confirm it by a majority. The head can dismiss an officer and an officer can resign, both at any time. One person holds one office at most, and offices lapse when the government changes.

**Treaties.** Nations deal with each other from **Nation > Foreign affairs**:

* **Alliance**, **non-aggression pact** and **trade treaty**: both nations must agree, and none can be made while they are at war.
* **War**: one nation declares it alone, which ends any alliance or pact between them.
* **Peace**: both nations must agree, and it ends the war.

Each nation decides by its own government. A ruler decides alone. A senate, council or the citizens decide by a majority vote, and in a confederation more than half of the member factions' leaders must agree. One nation's agreement stands as an offer for 7 days, until the other agrees too. Either nation can renounce an alliance, pact or trade treaty on its own. Citizens hear when a treaty starts or ends, and **View nations** lists every nation's treaties.

Treaties also decide how nations fight over [provinces](#sieges-and-defence-windows). A nation allied to the holder, or bound to it by a non-aggression pact, can never take its provinces by stake. One nation can only besiege another while they are at war.

**Vassals.** One nation can become another's vassal, also from **Foreign affairs**:

* **By agreement:** one nation chooses **Take as a vassal** and the other **Become their vassal**, in either order. A nation losing a war can surrender this way, which ends the war.
* **Imposed:** a nation at war that has won **2 sieges** against the other in the last 14 days can choose **Impose vassalage** on its own. This ends the war and costs the new vassal some legitimacy.
* **Tribute:** each week, every member faction of the vassal pays a tenth of the coin in its strongbox, up to 100 silver, into the strongbox of one of the overlord's factions in the same land. If the overlord has no strongbox in that land, nothing is paid there.
* An overlord and its vassal can't declare war on each other. They count as allies, so they never take each other's provinces and they defend each other's besieged seats.
* The overlord can release a vassal at any time. After 14 days (28 under a feudal crown) a vassal can **Declare independence**, which frees it, starts a war and raises its legitimacy.
* A vassal can't have vassals of its own, a nation with vassals can't become a vassal, and an overlord can keep up to 4 vassals (8 for a feudal crown).

**Legitimacy.** Every government except a confederation shows a legitimacy score from 0 to 100, and a new government starts at 50. The score rises when the head plays each day, motions pass and factions join. It falls when the head stays away, the seat stands empty, motions fail and factions secede. Surviving a revolt raises it. The lower the legitimacy, the fewer signatures a revolt petition needs, down to half the usual share. A high score makes revolt harder.

**Revolt.** Citizens can overthrow any government:

1. An eligible citizen opens a petition from **Nation > Revolt**, naming the government they want and its head.
2. Eligible citizens have 2 days to sign it. The petition needs half of the eligible citizens. Against a dictatorship it needs only a third, a merchant league two fifths, a republic two thirds and a democracy three quarters, at a legitimacy of 50. **Nation > Revolt** shows the share needed now.
3. Once the petition fills, every citizen is warned. From a day later, the signers have 3 days to hold any member faction's nation assembly for 30 minutes. In a province, this only counts during its defence window. A citizen who did not sign standing within 3 tiles pauses the count.
4. Success installs the new government at once.

After a filled petition, the nation cannot be petitioned again for a week. Signing is not a crime, only the number of signers is shown, and a failed revolt removes no one from play.

**Secession.** Under any government but a confederation, **Secede** gives notice. **Stay** withdraws it before the notice runs out.

#### Starting with your faction

Once you have joined a faction in character, your new characters can start with it. When you join the round, you are asked whether to start **here, on your own** or **with** your faction. Choosing your faction starts you beside one of its [recruitment stations](#recruitment-stations), already a member. You cannot start with a faction you have never joined in play, and the faction needs a working recruitment station (see [Provinces](#provinces)) with free space beside it.

#### Recruitment stations

On maps that allow custom factions, a faction can take members through a **recruitment station** instead of recruiting each person by hand. Craft one from **10 planks** (in the **economy & production** category) and place it. The leader then clicks it and chooses **Designate for my faction**, and the station takes the faction's colours. Only the leader can release it again.

* **Joining:** click a station to see the faction's rules and whether you meet them. Depending on the rules you can **Join** straight away or **Apply** with a short introduction. You can **Withdraw** an application at any time. If it is approved, come back to a station and **Confirm and join**. You must not be in another faction to apply.
* **Reviewing:** the leader and members with the **Recruitment** permission can approve, reject or revoke applications at any station of their faction, or from anywhere with **Recruitment Applications** on the **Faction tab**. They can give a reason, which the applicant sees. Applicants and reviewers are told about new decisions when they log in.
* **Rules (leader only):** the leader chooses the mode:
  * **Closed** stops new applications.
  * **Review every application** sends every application to the queue. New stations start in this mode.
  * **Accept matching applicants at once** lets players who meet the rules join immediately.

  Further rules set a required language and minimum skill levels (0-10) in crafting, farming, medical and combat. Players who don't meet the rules either still go to review or cannot apply, as the leader chooses. **Open recruitment** sets the language and skill rules aside, so everyone eligible counts as matching. Automatic acceptance needs open recruitment or at least one rule.
* Applications and approved offers expire after **7 days**. Dying ends that character's application.

#### Provinces

Each continent is divided into **provinces** that follow the land: coasts, rivers, lakes, mountain ranges and straits. Choose **Province Holdings** on the **Faction tab** to see which province you stand in, whether you count as one of your faction's residents there (and if not, why), and how much stake every side has in every province.

* **Stake:** each resident is worth 1 point. A resident is a member who has been in the faction for 3 days, has had their own bed for 3 days within 40 tiles of one of the faction's fastened halls, and plays regularly: 10 hours in all on this land, and 4 hours over at least 3 different days of the last week. Your faction's best hall in the province adds 2 points per stage, never more than its residents there. Factions of one nation add their stake together, but a large group's residents count for less beyond the first 10.
* **Leading:** a side leads a province with at least 6 points from at least 4 people, 35% of all the stake there and 1.25 times the next side.
* **Taking and holding:** a side that leads a province for **72 hours** in a row takes it. It keeps it until a rival out-stakes it by the same margin for 72 hours. A holder that drops below 6 points from 4 people is warned and loses the province after **48 hours** unless it recovers, so rebuild destroyed beds and halls quickly. A side holds at most **3** provinces of a land. A take or a loss that falls due waits for the province's daily [defence window](#sieges-and-defence-windows). Leaders are told when their hold slips, recovers, is challenged, taken or lost.
* **Stations:** once the preview shown in Province Holdings ends, a recruitment station on a continent works only in a province its side holds, and only when its faction has **3** residents of its own there. An idle station keeps its faction, rules and applications, and examining it says why it is idle. Starting with your faction uses working stations only.
* **Plots fall to ruin:** once the preview ends, a faction whose registered plots lie in a province where it has had fewer than **3** residents for **14 days** in a row loses those plots. The deeds go void and the buildings stay for anyone to take over. Province Holdings warns you while the days run.

#### Settlements: Camp to City

In a province your side holds, your best hall there is the **seat** of a settlement: everything within 40 tiles of it. Click the hall to open its **Province ledger**, which shows the settlement's rank, what each rank needs with a tick beside what is done, and what the rank gives. A rank rises as soon as everything is in place. If something goes missing, the leaders are warned and the settlement drops a rank after **48 hours** unless it is repaired.

| Rank | Seat | Resident members | Also needs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Camp** | council fire | 4 | - |
| **Village** | longhouse | 7 | 3 members' beds under a roof, a well or cistern, a working recruitment station, 4 registered plots, 2 guest beds (fastened, unclaimed) |
| **Town** | town hall | 11 | a closed wall or palisade ring around the seat with a gate, a roofed upper floor within 10 tiles of a gate (a watchtower), the faction's strongbox with a sales or business tax set, 10 plots, a market stall, 4 guest beds |
| **City** | capitol | 16 | the whole ring of stone, brick or concrete, 12 arrow slits, gun loops or battlements in it, 2 gates with a portcullis or a murder hole above, 60 paved tiles inside, a research bench, 20 plots, 6 guest beds |

Each rank also needs everything the ranks below it need. Doors inside roofed buildings do not break the ring; a door or castle gate in the open is a gate.

What a rank gives:

* **Harder to take:** a rival needs 1.25 times your stake against a Camp, 1.35 times against a Village, 1.5 against a Town and 1.6 against a City.
* **Slower to lose:** if your hold slips you have 48 hours to recover, 60 for a Town and 72 for a City.
* **More members:** your side's best province adds 3 members a rank to each of its factions' member limits.
* **Safer beds:** a resident's bed start inside a Town costs 1.5 times what a station start costs, and inside a City 1.25 times, instead of double.

**Upkeep:** a ranked settlement costs coin from its faction's strongbox every week: nothing for a Camp, 50 silver for a Village, 150 for a Town and 400 for a City. Your side's second ranked province on a land costs 1.5 times as much and its third twice. The first week is free. Unpaid upkeep counts as something missing, so the leaders are warned and the settlement drops a rank after 48 hours unless coin is put in the box. The ledger shows when the next payment is due.

#### Sieges and defence windows

Every province your side holds has a daily **defence window** of 3 hours, or 4 when your side holds two or more provinces of the land. The ledger and the war banner show it. A leader chooses its start among the server's busiest hours with **Defence Window** on the **Faction tab**. A change takes effect 3 days later. A province changes hands only during its window, so a side that plays at other hours cannot lose it overnight.

* **Declaring:** a rival leader crafts a **war banner** from wood (in the **economy & production** category), plants it in the province and clicks it to declare. Their faction must be at least 7 days old, have 4 members with 2 hours played on the land, and hold **100 silver** in its strongbox as a deposit. On Gaia only a Town or City can be besieged.
* **The siege** begins in the first window at least 24 hours after the declaration. The attackers take the seat by standing within 3 tiles of it with no defender there: 10 minutes against a Camp or an unranked seat, 15 against a Village, 20 against a Town and 30 against a City. A defender at the seat pauses the count.
* **If nobody comes,** the attackers' deposit is spent. If they came but the window closes before they take the seat, the siege fails. Breaking the war banner calls the siege off. Either way, that side must wait 3 days to declare there again.
* **Nations and treaties:** a nation can only declare a siege on another nation's province while the two are at war. If they make peace, a pact or an alliance during the siege, it is called off, the deposit is kept and nobody has to wait to declare again. Members of a nation allied to the holder count as defenders at the seat. Allies of the attacker count as attackers, unless they are allied to the holder too, when they defend. Factions outside a nation siege as before.
* **A taken seat** suspends one rank's benefits for 7 days, and the province cannot be besieged again for 3 days. Against a Camp or an unranked settlement, or when the same side wins twice within 14 days, the holder's hold also starts slipping.

Lands across the sea have no provinces, so stations there always work. A faction's claimed land across the sea stays its own unless the faction leaves it empty (see [Settle, claim or abandon](../guides/Guide_to_Boats.md#settle-claim-or-abandon)), and only the people the faction lets land can reach it (see [Your faction's island](../guides/Guide_to_Boats.md#your-factions-island)).

#### Family and adoption

On Nomads maps you can right-click someone next to you and choose **Offer Adoption**. If they accept, their last name becomes yours. An adopted character can use **Un-adopt Yourself** in the IC tab to get their original name back. You cannot adopt someone who is already adopted, and you need a last name to offer.

#### Changing your name

You can take a new name, for a new faith, a trade, a fresh start or any other reason.

  - **In a faction:** ask whoever keeps your faction's papers (see **Census and papers** below). Stand beside them; they choose **Record a change of name** in **Faction Papers**, you type your new name, and you get a written record of the change. Your papers keep working and now show your new name.
  - **In no faction:** use **Take a new name** in the IC tab.
  - After a change you must wait 7 real days before changing again. You can't take a name someone else in the region already goes by.
  - Your old name stays on record. Your own locks, traps and crew places on boats still know you, and warrants or fines against you still stand.
  - Taking a new name ends an adopted family name for good, so **Un-adopt Yourself** goes away.

#### As a Leader

  - You can invite people to your faction by getting close to them, **right-clicking** them, and selecting **Recruit** from the menu.
  - You **cannot** force someone to leave your faction; They have to **abandon** it themselves. How to get them to do that, is up to
    you...
  - You can set a lower member limit than your hall allows with **Set Member Limit** on the **Faction tab**. Members already counted keep their place.
  - You can make someone else a Leader by clicking **Transfer Leadership** on the **Faction tab** while near to your successor.
  - You can rename a faction you founded with **Rename Faction** in the **Faction panel** (on maps that allow custom factions). For the next 8 seasons, the Faction List, examining a member and examining your banners and posters show that it was recently renamed, and from what.
  - You can split your members into **fire teams** with **Roster** in the IC tab. Click a team number next to a person to put them in that team, **none** to take them out, or **lead** to make them the team's leader. Teammates you can see show a numbered badge (gold for the leader), and examining someone shows their team. The tracker arrow points fire team members to their fire team leader. Fire teams are kept with each character when the server restarts.
  - You can choose **Rally Point** in the IC tab to plant a rally point where you stand. For ten minutes your members' tracker arrow points to it. Any member can right-click something they see and choose **Spot Enemy** to warn the faction. See [Spotting enemies and rally points](../guides/Guide_to_Leadership.md#spotting-enemies-and-rally-points).
  - You can jail someone: right-click a closed door and choose **Cell Timer** to hold it shut for 1 to 30 minutes. Nobody can open it until the time is served, then it opens by itself and everyone nearby hears it click. Examining the door shows the time left, and the time carries over when the server restarts. Use **Cell Timer** again to release the prisoner early. The door can still be broken down.
  - You can make **Announcements** that are received by every member of your faction. Open the **Officer tab** and select **Faction Announcement**.
  - You can delegate your power to other faction members by giving them certain **officer permissions**. **Right-click** a member of your faction and select **Faction Perms**. These are the permissions you can give or revoke, if the person already has them:
      - **Permission Management** - lets them give or revoke **Announcements** and **Giving Titles**. Only the leader can hand out Permission Management and Recruitment.
      - **Announcements** 
      - **Giving Titles**
      - **Recruitment** - lets them invite people into the faction. It does not make them a leader: they get no treasury, tax or permission powers.

#### Offices and elections

**Faction Offices** on the **Faction tab** lists your faction's offices, who holds them, and any election under way.

  - A faction starts with a **Treasurer**, who keeps the [strongbox](../guides/Guide_to_Money.md#faction-treasury), and a **Registrar**, who keeps the [land register](../guides/Guide_to_Money.md#land-and-deeds).
  - The leader can create up to 12 offices, such as Judge or Herald, and choose what each one may do. The choices are keeping the treasury, keeping the land register, cutting and revoking [seals](../guides/Guide_to_Paperwork.md), cutting coin dies, holding court, issuing papers, making treaties, and moving the faction's hall and fixtures.
  - The leader appoints members to offices, for a set number of days or with no limit, and can dismiss them.
  - A [faction seal](../guides/Guide_to_Paperwork.md) cut for an office gives that office's powers to whoever carries it. To lend an office, hand over its seal. Revoke the seal to take it back.
  - The leader can call an **election** for the leadership or for any office. The leader can also set a **term**. Any member can call a leadership election if the leader has served longer than the term, if no one leads, or if the leader hasn't played for more than 3 days.
  - An election lasts 30 minutes. Members **stand** as candidates and **vote**, one vote each. The most votes wins, and a tie goes to whoever stood first. An elected leader takes over the faction. An elected officer serves for the faction's term.

#### Laws and courts

**Faction Justice** on the **Faction tab** holds your faction's laws and court record.

  - The leader writes the faction's laws (up to 20) and can repeal them. Every member online hears about each change.
  - Any member can read the laws, the warrants, outlaws and fines, and the record of past rulings.
  - Anyone with the **justice** power can hold a trial: the leader, a Judge or any office given that power, or someone carrying that office's seal. Stand beside a **gavel block** (1 wood, paper & printing). Pick the accused from people nearby, the charge, the verdict and the sentence. The judge gets a written judgment to seal.
  - Sentences are:
      - a fine of up to 500 silver
      - jail for up to 30 minutes
      - banishment
      - outlawry
      - a warning
  - Fines are paid at the faction's [strongbox](../guides/Guide_to_Money.md#faction-treasury) with **Pay a fine from my hand**. Anyone can pay one, even for someone else.
  - For jail, shut the convict behind a door and use **Cell Timer** on it. Judges can set cell timers as well as the leader.
  - Judges can issue a **warrant**, which prints a wanted poster, and can **pardon** someone.
  - Examining someone shows if a faction has a warrant out for them or has declared them an outlaw. The game doesn't punish anyone by itself. The faction carries out its own rulings.

#### Census and papers

**Faction Papers** on the **Faction tab** keeps your faction's census and its papers.

  - Any member can read the **census**, which shows each member's join date, dues paid and plots registered to them, along with each office, who holds it and how many papers are in force.
  - Anyone with the **papers** power can issue papers to a person standing beside them. That means the leader, an office given that power, or someone carrying that office's seal. Each one uses a blank sheet of paper from your hands or at your feet.
      - **Citizenship papers** last until they are revoked.
      - A **travel pass** lasts 1 to 30 days.
      - A **building permit** or **trading licence** lasts 1 to 30 days, or until revoked.
  - Papers name their holder and only work for that person, so stolen or copied papers open nothing. Examine papers to check whether they're still in force. The papers power can revoke any papers.
  - The papers power also records a **change of name** for a member standing beside them, using a blank sheet. The census lists the changes on record.
  - Stand beside your faction's doors to set who they open for: members only, members and citizens, or members, citizens and pass holders. Papers only count when held in your hands or worn, not packed in a bag.
  - Permits aren't required unless the papers power chooses **Require permits on our land**. That can require a **building permit** to build on the faction's registered plots, a **trading licence** to set up a market stall there, or both. Members never need one, and neither does whoever carries the plot's deed. Permits don't open border doors.

#### Treaties

**Faction Treaties** on the **Faction tab** keeps the treaties made in your land.

  - Anyone can read every treaty: its terms, the parties, who signed for each and when, and what has happened to it since.
  - Anyone with the **diplomacy** power (the leader, an office given that power, or someone carrying that office's seal) can draft a treaty with one to five other factions. Give it a title and terms. Your faction signs as soon as you draft it.
  - Each other faction's diplomacy power reads the draft and signs or refuses it. Once every party has signed, the treaty is in force. The members of every party hear about each step.
  - A party can **renounce** a treaty in force and give a reason. The treaty stays on record, marked as renounced, along with who broke it and why.
  - You can write out a copy of a treaty on a blank sheet of paper and seal it.
  - The game doesn't enforce treaties. The factions do.

#### As a Member

  - You can ask the leader to give you **officer permissions**.
  - Members with the **Recruitment** permission can recruit new members while the leader is away.
  - Anyone in the faction can become a new leader if the current one is dead, by clicking **Become Faction Leader** on the **Faction tab**.

## Research

For rounds using the newer tech-tree mode, use the [Research guide](../guides/Guide_to_Research.md). The sections below describe legacy modes; their point-pool rules do not describe the tech tree. Always check the lobby's selected mode.

Research is one of the most important aspects of Civilizations and
Nomads. It is with it that your faction can unlock new craftables and
better equipment\!

A small faction with advanced research and guns can usually topple the
large group of Stone Age characters armed with bows and arrows.

Research methods depend on the map and lobby selection. For technology-tree progression, requirements and crafting gates, use the [Research Guide](../guides/Guide_to_Research.md). The following sections describe the other research selections.

Research mode is voted at the lobby before the game begins.

```admonish note
Remember, you need to create or join a faction to save research points.
```

### Classic Research

The original research method! And the most tedious.

Use a **Research Kit** on Scientific Literature, Scientific Rock Slate,
Scientific Books, or Bookcases.

You can make those with the following materials: **wood, bamboo, stone, sandstone and marble**.

The points you get depend on how advanced the person who wrote the literature was, and what the subject of their writing is.

```admonish tip
If you are struggling with your own research, it might be a good idea to see if any neighboring factions will let you study their bookcases. Buy, trade, and negotiate access to them to boost your research. Or fight them, rob them... get creative (within the rules).
```

### Auto Research

As the name suggests, all factions in the world gain the same amount of
points in all areas at the same rate, without doing anything.

Sit back and relax. Or go plan a war against that one guy who looked at
you funny.

Just remember, if you can make guns, so can they...

The world keeps advancing age by age, past the Modern Age into the
**Digital Age (2026)**, with its smartphones, drones, lithium-ion batteries,
LED lighting and 3D printers.

### Resource-Based Research

In this mode, you create a **Research Bench** with **wood**, place any
items on it, and select a category to research.

This means that this mode rewards productivity and exploitation of
natural resources. No slacking off\!

Different items are worth different amounts.

Experiment!

### Chad Mode

*It started as a joke, now it's too late to go back...*

Chad Mode uses no research progression. Read the mode briefing and [server rules](../rules/Rules.md) for its roleplay and combat rules; the research selection does not waive server rules.

### Chad Mode+

Just like Chad Mode, but with research! To do chad research, build an **Altar of Chad**. Using living or dead characters on the altar provides research points; follow the mode briefing and server rules. Living are worth 12, dead are worth 8.

```admonish danger
Chad Mode+ can be chaotic and dangerous; the game may become very unpredictable.
```

**If a faction member is sacrificed to advance a certain category of research, that faction loses all their research points in that category!**

### No Research

In this mode, you're stuck in the epoch you started in, with all
technology of that epoch unlocked. The lobby offers starts from the Bronze
Age up to the **Digital Age (2026)**, the newest age, which comes after the
Modern Age.

Research mechanics won't yield any improvements.

## Environments

The environment lives and breathes and will be the single biggest factor that influences your life!

Each map has either a single biome or several different ones. 

There are seasons of the year, effects of which are different in every biome. Some biomes have four seasons, while others have only two.

On large maps with multiple biomes each biome has its own list of plants that could be grown there. Many plants also grow only in certain seasons. See the **[Guide to Farming](../guides/Guide_to_Farming.md)**.

To determine the biome you are in, read the descriptions below and look at your surroundings. Looking at the map of **[Pangea](../images/map_pangea.png)** that has almost every biome may also help. From top to bottom: Tundra, Taiga (roughly the southern half of the snowed area, before the northern mountains), Temperate, Semi-Arid, Desert, Savanna, Jungle. The one biome missing is Sea.

### Biomes and seasons

#### Tundra

* Snow covers the ground all year round and does not melt during summer (unlike in Temperate). 
* Trees and animals are somewhat rare here.

Seasons:

- Summer: Mild temperatures, some snow melts, revealing dirt, and crops can grow. No chance of snowfall or blizzard. Wild animals respawn.
- Winter: Icy temperatures that require a coat, a high chance of snowfall, and a chance of deadly blizzard. Crops stop growing and snow covers all land. Have a shelter ready or you probably won't survive until summer.

#### Taiga

Similar to Tundra in many regards, but considerably more trees.

#### Temperate

Plenty of trees and grassy meadows ("wild grass" tiles).

Seasons:

  - Spring: Mild temperatures, a little leftover snow from the winter.
    Sparse rains cause mud slowdowns. Snow starts melting.
  - Summer: Hot temperatures, no rain. Chance of heat-waves that
    evaporate all puddles. All snow melts. Wild animals respawn at a
    faster rate.
  - Fall: Cooler temperatures, sparse rain and occasional snowfall will
    slow you down.
  - Winter: Around freezing by day and colder at night; a coat over your
    clothes keeps you safe. Some lakes and rivers freeze over, allowing
    you to walk on them. Constant
    snowfall slows you down, **crops will not grow and animals won't
    respawn**. Chance of blizzards that cause a whiteout, and can kill
    you even with fur coats. Have a shelter ready\!

#### Sea

Smaller islands on larger maps usually have this biome.

#### Semi-Arid

Large expanses of dry dirt with occasional groves.

#### Desert

Sand. Lots of sand.

Seasons:

  - Dry Season: All the water sources dry up, has a chance of
    sandstorms, which impair vision, but cause no other ill-effects.
    Heat is intense and can harm you if you stay in the sun too long.
  - Wet Season: All water sources fill up, sandstorms die down. Wild
    animals respawn.

#### Savanna

Wide plains covered with sunburnt dry grass. Acacia trees are a common sight here.

#### Jungle

Lush vegetation with massive jungle trees and large bushes; thick jungle grass covers the ground.

Seasons:

* Dry Season: Fertile floodplains on the riverbanks are uncovered, sunny weather most of the time.
* Wet Season: Heavy rains and the arable land along the rivers is flooded.
