# Industrial plastics

From WW2, **Industrial Plastics** follows **Assembly Line**, **Combustion Engine**
and **Electricity**. On a book-research world, learn the Industrial Plastics
chapter. The construction recipe is in the global catalog; individual maps may
not provide the same starting equipment or crude petroleum deposits.

1. Collect crude petroleum from an oil well, or from an existing source on your
   map. Bring it in a suitable container to a cabled **Bakelizer**. When it is
   stopped, **Change Setting → Thermoplastic pellets**. Use the petroleum
   container on the machine, start the Bakelizer, and use **Remove Plastic** to
   collect finished solid pellets. Five game units of crude give one measure
   per 60-second batch. Direct plastic sheets, phenolic resin, epoxy, nylon and
   photopolymer remain the separate existing settings.
2. Alternatively, when your existing laboratory work yields polyethylene or
   polypropylene, secure the beaker in the **Laboratory reaction bench** and
   choose **Granulate … into thermoplastic pellets**. Four game units of the
   selected polymer give one measure. The bench still offers its old direct
   products; ordinary plastic sheets and printer resin cannot stand in for
   pellet feed.
3. Build the **Plastics molding machine** from 15 steel sheets, cable it, and
   click it to select one of the fitted dies below. Use a held pellet stack on
   the machine or bring it by factory arm. It holds at most two runs' worth.
   Collect the finished product from its tile. Losing power pauses the molder,
   with no materials spent until a cycle completes. Clear the recipe to
   recover buffered pellets. A world load stops the Bakelizer; only the molder
   rechecks and resumes a valid saved recipe when its inputs and power return.

| Die | Product | Pellet measures |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Extrusion | Plastic sheet, compatible with existing sheet recipes and 3D printing | 1 |
| Injection | Tool handle that fits existing compatible heads | 2 |
| Injection | Plastic comb | 1 |
| Blow molding | Empty plastic bottle | 1 |
| Blow molding | Plastic play ball | 1 |
| Film forming | Reusable plastic bag | 1 |

The new machine cannot run other factory or printer recipes, and other
machines cannot select these dies. Its outputs deliberately reuse existing
game items and graphics. This is an in-game manufacturing loop, not a real
synthesis procedure or a simulation of tooling temperatures or pressures.
